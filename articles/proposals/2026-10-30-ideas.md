# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-10-30

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot **plus** a
quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
and confounding-by-indication caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed from
our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

primary-source URLs below could not be fetched in-session; they are included because they are

## 1. A boxed warning for a signal everyone already knew: IV iron and hypophosphatemia

**Working title:** "Three Labels, One Signal: What the Injectafer Boxed Warning Says About
Reading a *Product-Specific* Class Effect in FAERS"

**Safety question.** Ferric carboxymaltose (Injectafer) causes a well-understood, mechanistically
specific drug reaction — FGF23-mediated renal phosphate wasting → symptomatic hypophosphatemia,
and downstream osteomalacia and fractures on repeat dosing. The question for this site is not
*whether* it happens (it is established pharmacology) but whether disproportionality **separates
the IV-iron products correctly**: does ferric carboxymaltose stand apart from ferumoxytol, ferric
derisomaltose, iron sucrose, and iron dextran on a *Hypophosphataemia* / *Blood phosphorus
decreased* / *Osteomalacia* / *Fracture* PT cluster, and does the within-class contrast survive
once you account for the fact that the effect is already labeled (stimulated reporting)?

**Why it matters now.** On **September 1, 2026** the FDA added a **boxed warning** to Injectafer
describing the risk of symptomatic hypophosphatemia — the agency's most prominent warning level —
after finding the reaction kept being reported *despite* prior labeling changes in February 2020
and May 2023, and after Sentinel data showed serum-phosphate testing happens in **fewer than 20%**
of administration episodes. The label was marked revised 08/2026 and generic ferric-carboxymaltose
labels are expected to follow. This is a this-quarter regulatory action on a high-volume drug with
deep FAERS coverage, and the "it was already known and still under-monitored" framing is squarely
in the site's methodology lane.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for ferric
carboxymaltose against the phosphate/bone PT cluster, with the other IV irons as the within-class
comparators (the honest denominator here is *other IV iron*, not the whole database — a point the
article can make explicitly). Show the latest-quarter snapshot with observed counts and the
quarterly caterpillar; the trajectory should let us see whether the 2020 and 2023 label changes
visibly bent the reporting curve (a label-date-as-natural-experiment check the site already likes).
**Methodology centerpiece:** a *product-specific* class effect is the mirror image of the AAV-liver
piece (route-tracks-the-signal) and the GLP-1 alopecia piece (the signal moves) — here one member
of a therapeutic class carries a mechanism the others don't, and good disproportionality should
localize it to that member. Pair with the under-monitoring fact (<20% phosphate testing) as the
ascertainment lesson: a boxed warning can be driven by *reporting persistence despite labeling*,
not by a new discovery.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low.** All IV irons are high-volume with standard PTs and years of
FAERS history; the phosphate/bone cluster is unambiguous. The analysis is routine — the value is in
the within-class framing and the label-trajectory read.

## 2. When the adverse-event term is a creature of the drug: ARIA and the anti-amyloid antibodies

**Working title:** "The 10,000-Fold Signal That Means Almost Nothing: ARIA, Lecanemab, and Why
the Denominator Is the Whole Argument"

**Safety question.** Lecanemab (Leqembi) and donanemab (Kisunla) throw the largest disproportionality
numbers in all of FAERS for *amyloid-related imaging abnormalities* (ARIA-E, ARIA-H) — reporting odds
ratios in the thousands. But "ARIA" is a MedDRA concept that essentially *only* arises with
anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies, so a signal against the whole-database background is close to
tautological. The real safety questions are the ones disproportionality against the full database
can't answer: (a) **within class**, does lecanemab differ from donanemab on ARIA-E vs ARIA-H? and
(b) the risk axis that actually drives management — APOE ε4 genotype and homozygosity — is **invisible
to FAERS** (no genotype field), exactly the blind spot the sevoflurane piece raised for ancestry.

**Why it matters now.** The class is in active label motion in 2026. Leqembi's prescribing
information was revised 7/2026 to add the once-weekly subcutaneous IQLIK autoinjector, with the ARIA
warnings last revised 12/2025; the FDA extended review of the subcutaneous *start-dose* sBLA with a
new action date of **August 24, 2026**; and Kisunla received a label update for a more gradual
first-months titration **specifically to lower severe-ARIA risk**. Three fresh 2026 FAERS
disproportionality studies now exist as method precedent, including one covering reports through
**Q2 2026** (published Sept 24, 2026). The live label changes make a natural-experiment framing
possible: does the titration change bend donanemab's ARIA-E trajectory?

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run the four-method engine for lecanemab,
donanemab (and aducanumab as the retired-class anchor) against ARIA-E, ARIA-H, cerebral
microhaemorrhage, and infusion-related reaction. The **methodology centerpiece** is the denominator
lesson: show the absurd whole-database EB05/ROR, then re-run *within class* (anti-amyloid vs
anti-amyloid) to demonstrate that the meaningful contrast (per the literature: lecanemab skews
ARIA-H, donanemab skews ARIA-E) is an order-of-magnitude smaller and genotype-blind. This extends —
rather than repeats — the AEMS "the raw leaderboard lies" page: there the artifacts were
confounding/route/litigation; here it's a **label-coined event term with no meaningful external
comparator**, a distinct gotcha ("disproportionality needs a denominator that could plausibly have
produced the event too"). Secondarily, note the APOE ε4 subgroup blind spot, cross-referencing the
sevoflurane invisible-subpopulation piece so the two don't collide.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Both drugs are well represented in FAERS and the PTs
are standard; the within-class recompute is straightforward. Difficulty is conceptual (making the
tautology argument cleanly), not computational.

## 3. A live vaccine whose serious events hide in one age stratum: IXCHIQ in VAERS

**Working title:** "The Signal Is in the Stratum: Why Whole-Database VAERS Misreads a
Live-Attenuated Chikungunya Vaccine"

**Safety question.** IXCHIQ (VLA1553) is a live-attenuated chikungunya vaccine whose early
post-marketing serious adverse events concentrated almost entirely in older, multimorbid
recipients — including systemic chikungunya-*like* illness (the live virus doing a mild version of
the disease the vaccine prevents), neurological events, three deaths, and a probable case of
vaccine-strain encephalitis. The methodology question: a whole-database VAERS disproportionality
run pools all ages and will **dilute an age-restricted serious-event signal**; does age
stratification (or a dose-denominator view) recover what the aggregate hides, and can VAERS — which
has age but not frailty/comorbidity — even see a risk that tracks multimorbidity?

**Why it matters now.** The peer-reviewed post-marketing evaluation (Vaccine, 2026) integrated VAERS
with EudraVigilance/Canada Vigilance/MHRA and the EMA Article 20 referral: by **31 August 2025**,
~55,900 doses → **35 serious adverse events**; adults ≥65 were a third of doses but **77% of SAEs**;
median SAE age **73**, **91% comorbid**; most events within 7 days. Regulators tightened the product
information rather than withdrawing it, and in **February 2026 the UK MHRA** stopped recommending the
vaccine for adults over 60 or those with hypertension/CVD/diabetes/CKD, citing 28 serious reactions
with 3 fatal. The U.S. pause (2025) was lifted with a label tightening. This is a live,
internationally active signal with a VAERS spine — the site's vaccine wheelhouse.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Run the four-method engine for IXCHIQ against the
reactogenicity/neuro/cardiac PT cluster, first whole-database, then **age-stratified** (≥65 vs <65),
to show the dilution/recovery contrast directly; show observed counts beside every EB05 given the
small N, and pool quarters since doses are sparse. **Methodology centerpiece:** stratify-or-miss —
a serious-event signal confined to one age band is structurally understated in a pooled run, the
inverse framing of the sevoflurane piece (there the stratum was genetic and *unavailable* in the
data; here it's age and *available*, so stratification is the fix, not a lament). Flag the
live-attenuated "events resembling the target disease" theme it shares with the queued
Zostavax-vs-Shingrix piece, and distinguish it: this one is about **age-stratified serious-event
ascertainment + a regulatory natural experiment**, not zoster-as-the-outcome.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** IXCHIQ is new and low-volume, so counts are small and the
analysis leans on pooling and careful small-N caveats; VAERS age fields support the stratification,
but comorbidity/frailty is not codable, which is itself part of the lesson.

## 4. Stress-testing our own engine: what empirical-null calibration does to an EB05 ≥ 2 signal

**Working title:** "Would Our Own Signals Survive Calibration? Negative Controls and the Empirical
Null, Applied to Our Four-Method Rule"

**Safety question.** The site's house rule is EB05 ≥ 2.0 with ≥2 of 4 methods agreeing. A 2026
FAERS re-analysis argues that *uncalibrated* disproportionality cannot separate pharmacology from
reporting at all, and that the verdict often flips once you fit an **empirical null** from a set of
negative-control drug–event pairs and correct for multiplicity. The honest, self-critical question
this site is well placed to ask: **if we re-ran one of our own past signals through negative-control
calibration, would it survive?** This is the methodology-first lane at its purest — turning the
critique on our own method rather than on a drug.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, İnkaya A, Güngör HS, "Empirical-Null Calibration
Challenges Comparative CNS Safety Signals for Androgen-Receptor Pathway Inhibitors: A FAERS
Re-Analysis," *Pharmaceuticals* 2026 (pub. **Aug 1, 2026**) computed RORs across the full FAERS
database (20,328,575 reports), then recalibrated against an empirical null from 18 negative controls
with Benjamini–Hochberg correction: **22 of 32 raw signals** flagged, but **41 of 72 negative-control
pairs also flagged** (so the naive null does not hold); after calibration the count fell to **six —
or, under a more conservative fit of the null, zero** surviving multiplicity correction. It is a
clean, recent, quantitative demonstration that the calibration *design*, not the drug, can set the
verdict — directly relevant to how the site reports EB05 thresholds.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS (and the method itself).** Pick one previously
published site result (e.g., a GLP-1 or carbidopa/levodopa pair already in the article set), assemble
a transparent panel of negative-control pairs (drug–event pairs with no plausible mechanism), fit an
empirical null for the site's EB05/IC statistics, and report the signal *before and after* calibration
with FDR correction — plus a sensitivity check on how the null is fitted (the paper's key caveat).
**Methodology centerpiece:** a transferable "reading FDA data" lesson — a raw EB05 ≥ 2 is a starting
point, not a verdict; show readers what negative-control calibration is, why it matters, and how far
it moves one of our own numbers. Distinct from the AEMS page (which explains *why* raw leaderboards
are artifactual) by giving the **statistical remedy** and applying it to ourselves. Could seed a
standing "how we read our own signals" methods note.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The negative-control panel must be chosen carefully and
defensibly, and fitting/validating an empirical null is more work than a standard run — but it uses
the same FAERS parquet and the site's existing statistics, so no new data is needed. Highest
methodological payoff of the batch; lowest novelty of *drug* subject (by design — the subject is the
method).

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: İnkaya A, Güngör HS. "Empirical-Null Calibration Challenges Comparative CNS
  Safety Signals for Androgen-Receptor Pathway Inhibitors: A FAERS Re-Analysis." *Pharmaceuticals*
  (Basel) 2026;19(8):1213 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph19081213)
- medRxiv preprint, "Adverse drug withdrawal event signals in FAERS and EudraVigilance databases: a
  stratified disproportionality analysis" (2026-08-29; high reporting frequency ≠ strong
  disproportionality — the cross-database replication companion lesson) —
  https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.29.26361707v1.full
- (Background method reference for empirical-null calibration in observational safety analytics:
  Schuemie MJ et al., "Interpreting observational studies: why empirical calibration is needed to
  correct p-values," *Stat Med* 2014 — cite the canonical source in the piece.)

## 5. A boxed warning comes *off*: hormone therapy as a reporting natural experiment

**Working title:** "When the Warning Is Removed: Does FAERS Reporting for Estrogen Change After the
Boxed Warning Comes Off?"

**Safety question.** Almost every pharmacovigilance gotcha this site writes about involves a warning
being *added* and reporting rising afterward (notoriety/stimulated reporting). The February 2026 FDA
action runs the experiment in reverse: boxed warnings for cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, and
probable dementia were **removed** from six menopausal hormone-therapy products. So: does the
*reporting* of those classic estrogen endpoints (VTE, stroke, breast cancer, dementia) in FAERS shift
after a boxed warning is withdrawn — and if it does, what does that tell us about how much of any
disproportionality signal was ever about the drug versus about the label?

**Why it matters now.** On **February 12, 2026** the FDA approved labeling changes for six menopausal
hormone-therapy products removing boxed-warning statements on CVD, breast cancer, and probable
dementia (the endometrial-cancer boxed warning was retained for estrogen-alone systemic products in
women with a uterus); the agency called it the *first* batch, and as of late October 2026 the action
was still in force with further relabeling expected. A boxed warning coming *off* a high-volume,
long-marketed class is a rare, clean, dated natural experiment — and the site already treats a known
label date as a natural experiment (per the queue's methodology catalogue).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Build the quarterly caterpillar for the estrogen
products against VTE/stroke/breast-cancer/dementia PTs spanning the label change, and read the
trajectory for a step or slope change around 2026-Q1/Q2 (with the usual lag for label uptake); use
the latest-quarter snapshot with observed counts. **Methodology centerpiece:** the symmetric test of
the stimulated-reporting thesis — if adding warnings inflates reporting, removing them should deflate
it, and a *flat* trajectory would itself be informative (reporting driven by litigation/legacy WHI
awareness rather than the current label). Explicitly can't-show: this measures **reporting behavior,
not incidence or risk**, and confounding-by-indication plus decades of WHI-era baseline make any
causal read off-limits — which is the honest point of the piece.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** The products are long-marketed and high-volume with
standard PTs, so the trajectory is easy to build; the only subtlety is defining the product set to
match the six relabeled products and allowing for label-uptake lag (the post-change window is only a
few quarters as of this writing, so the piece may need to be framed as an *early* read or revisited).
