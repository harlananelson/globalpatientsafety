# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-07-03

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, and quarterly caterpillar plots.

Voice reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit
about what disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting and
confounding caveats. These proposals aim to fit that register.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed —
every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the literature, not to our data.

## 1. Does FAERS corroborate the new alli (orlistat) kidney warning built from just 12 cases?

**Working title:** "Twelve Cases and a Label Change: Testing the New OTC Orlistat Kidney
Warning Against the Full FAERS Record"

**Safety question.** On the strength of a small case series, FDA added an acute-kidney-injury
and kidney-stone warning to over-the-counter alli (orlistat 60 mg). Does independent
disproportionality analysis across the *entire* FAERS database show a matching excess of
renal reporting for orlistat — and is it specifically the oxalate-nephropathy pattern the
FDA cases suggest, rather than generic renal noise?

**Why it matters now.** On **June 10, 2026** FDA approved Drug Facts labeling changes for
alli to warn of acute kidney injury and kidney stones. The action rested on **12 cases**
identified between the 2007 approval and Dec 31, 2023 — 8 acute kidney injury, 2 AKI with
oxalate nephropathy, 2 hyperoxaluria with calcium-oxalate stones — with 8 hospitalizations,
5 requiring dialysis, and a risk the agency described as **not dose-dependent**. A warning
built on a dozen cases is exactly the situation where a full-database disproportionality
pass is informative: it either corroborates the regulator's small series or shows the
signal is invisible at population scale.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** Orlistat is a drug → **FAERS**, not VAERS. Run the
four-method engine for orlistat (both prescription Xenical and OTC alli spellings) against
the renal MedDRA PT cluster: *Acute kidney injury*, *Oxalate nephropathy*, *Nephrolithiasis*,
*Calculus urinary*, *Hyperoxaluria*, *Renal failure*. Report EB05 with ≥2-of-4 agreement,
then an **EBGM caterpillar over FAERS quarters** to show whether the renal signal predates
the 2026 label action (it should, if the biology is real) and whether reporting spikes after
the June 2026 communication (stimulated-reporting check). A mechanism sidebar — orlistat's
fat malabsorption → enteric hyperoxaluria → calcium-oxalate deposition — makes the oxalate
specificity testable rather than hand-waved.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Orlistat is a long-marketed, high-volume drug,
so FAERS coverage is deep; the renal PTs are standard. Main nuance is separating OTC alli
from prescription Xenical reports (often collapsed under "orlistat") and controlling for the
weight-loss-population confounder (dehydration, other agents).

**Citations.**
- FDA Drug Safety Communication, "FDA Approves Labeling Changes for OTC Weight Loss Drug alli (Orlistat) to Warn of Risk of Kidney Stones and Kidney Injury" — https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-approves-labeling-changes-over-counter-otc-weight-loss-drug-alli-orlistat-warn-risk-kidney
- Drugs.com FDA alert (June 2026), case-count details — https://www.drugs.com/fda/fda-approves-labeling-changes-over-counter-otc-weight-loss-alli-orlistat-warn-risk-kidney-stones-14814.html
- Renal & Urology News, "FDA Adds Kidney Injury Label Warning to OTC Weight Loss Aid Alli" — https://www.renalandurologynews.com/news/fda-updates-orlistat-label-kidney-injury/

## 2. Compounded GLP-1s are being pushed off the market — what does FAERS say about the dosing-error harm that drove it?

**Working title:** "Dosing Errors, Not Just Nausea: A FAERS Reanalysis of the Semaglutide /
Tirzepatide Medication-Error Signal as FDA Ends Compounded Supply"

**Safety question.** FDA's move to end large-scale compounded GLP-1 supply is driven largely
by **medication/dosing errors** (self-drawn syringes, unit confusion), not the drugs'
intrinsic pharmacology. Does FAERS show a disproportionate medication-error signal for
semaglutide/tirzepatide relative to comparators — and can the reporting even *see* the
compounded-vs-branded distinction, or is that a blind spot worth stating plainly?

**Why it matters now.** On **May 1, 2026** FDA proposed (in the Federal Register) removing
semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B bulk-substances list, with a public
comment period running through **June 29, 2026** (a comment-docket deadline on the proposal —
*not* itself a supply cutoff). FDA has tied the action to adverse-event reports — **>455
compounded-semaglutide and >320 compounded-tirzepatide reports (FDA's count as of early 2025),
with hospitalizations, many from dosing errors**. (A "≥10 deaths" figure circulates in
secondary coverage but I could not confirm it against a primary FDA source — verify before
citing.) This is a live policy story with a safety-data spine the site is built to examine.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method EBGM/PRR/ROR/IC for semaglutide
and tirzepatide against the medication-error PT cluster — *Medication error*, *Accidental
overdose*, *Incorrect dose administered*, *Product dose omission*, *Wrong technique in product
usage* — benchmarked against a branded injectable comparator. Quarterly caterpillar to show
whether the error signal rises through the 2023–2026 compounding boom. **The honest
methodological centerpiece:** FAERS rarely tags a report as "compounded," so branded and
compounded product often collapse under the same ingredient name — the piece can *demonstrate*
that limitation (e.g., manufacturer-field sparsity) rather than paper over it, which is
squarely in this site's wheelhouse.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium–High.** Signal detection itself is easy (huge report
volume). The hard, interesting part is the compounded-vs-branded attribution: it likely
*can't* be cleanly done in FAERS, and the article's value is partly in showing why.

**Citations.**
- Pharmacy Times, "FDA Moves to Permanently Close the Door on Compounded GLP-1s" — https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s
- Coverage of the June 29, 2026 semaglutide compounding deadline — https://www.myprofusions.com/what-the-fdas-june-29-2026-semaglutide-deadline-means-for-your-weight-loss-plan/
- Comparative renal-safety FAERS disproportionality study of tirzepatide vs semaglutide (method precedent) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12610529/

## 3. A new boxed warning on a pediatric drug — setting the FAERS baseline before the signal can appear

**Working title:** "Before the Signal: A Pre-Expansion FAERS Baseline for Teplizumab (Tzield)
Viral-Reactivation Risk in Kids"

**Safety question.** Teplizumab's newly expanded pediatric indication carries a **boxed
warning for serious EBV and CMV reactivation**. Children 8–17 are now newly exposed. What
does the *existing* (largely adult/adolescent) FAERS record show for viral-reactivation and
immunosuppression events — i.e., what is the honest baseline against which any pediatric
post-market signal should later be judged?

**Why it matters now.** On **June 12, 2026** FDA granted accelerated approval of Tzield
(teplizumab) for pediatric patients aged 8–17 recently diagnosed with stage 3 type 1
diabetes — the approval hinged on a C-peptide surrogate endpoint, with a confirmatory study
ongoing, and the label carries a **boxed warning for life-threatening EBV/CMV reactivation**.
A brand-new indication is precisely when a surveillance baseline is most useful and least
likely to already exist.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for
teplizumab against the immunosuppression/viral-reactivation PT cluster — *Epstein-Barr virus
infection*, *Cytomegalovirus infection*, *BK virus infection*, *Lymphopenia*, *Cytokine
release syndrome* (a known infusion effect). The framing is deliberately modest and matches
the site's voice: **it is too early for a pediatric signal**, so the deliverable is a
*baseline* — current EB05 values, report counts, and a quarterly caterpillar — that a
follow-up piece can compare against once pediatric exposure accrues. A worked example of
doing surveillance *prospectively* rather than only retrospectively.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium–High.** Teplizumab is low-volume (narrow indication,
recent launch), so counts will be small and several EB05 estimates may sit below threshold —
which is itself the honest finding for a baseline piece. Requires care not to over-read
sparse data.

**Citations.**
- FDA press announcement, "FDA Approves New Indication for Tzield (teplizumab) for Certain Pediatric Patients with Recently Diagnosed Stage 3 Type 1 Diabetes" — https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-new-indication-tzield-teplizumab-certain-pediatric-patients-recently-diagnosed-stage-3
- Drugs.com Tzield label (boxed warning, EBV/CMV reactivation) — https://www.drugs.com/tzield.html

## 4. Confounding by indication: when a "drug signal" is really the disease — a methods piece using immune-checkpoint endocrine events

**Working title:** "Is It the Drug or the Cancer? Reading Disproportionality Signals Through
a Confounding-Aware Lens"

**Safety question.** Disproportionality flags associations, not causes — and one of its most
under-discussed failure modes is confounding by indication: an event that tracks the
*disease* the drug treats masquerades as a *drug* signal. Recent immune-checkpoint-inhibitor
(ICI) work formalizes a "confounding-aware" disproportionality approach. Can the site's
four-method engine reproduce the distinction between disease-inherent and drug-attributable
endocrine signals — and use it to explain, in plain terms, why this matters for reading
*any* EB05 table on the site?

**Why it matters now.** A **2026** confounding-aware disproportionality study separated
disease-inherent hormonal dysregulation from therapy-attributable endocrine sequelae for
ICIs across 40 FAERS quarters (Q1 2015–Q4 2024) — a concrete, current
methods advance the site can apply and explain. It pairs naturally with the site's existing
habit of foregrounding stimulated-reporting and confounding caveats (as in the Cotton piece).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for an ICI
(e.g., pembrolizumab or nivolumab) against endocrine PTs — *Hypothyroidism*, *Hypophysitis*,
*Adrenal insufficiency*, *Type 1 diabetes mellitus*, *Thyroiditis* — then demonstrate how
comparator choice reshapes the signal: full-database background vs. a within-oncology
(active-comparator) restriction. The teaching payload is a worked contrast showing which
endocrine PTs survive an active-comparator restriction (drug-attributable) and which collapse
(disease-inherent). A companion illustration of the same trap: the finasteride "post-finasteride
syndrome" reporting surge, where a nocebo/awareness dynamic inflates disproportionality (per
PubMed, Gupta et al. 2026, *J Dermatolog Treat*).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** ICI reporting volume is large and endocrine PTs are
well-populated, so the analysis is tractable. The lift is conceptual (constructing a defensible
active-comparator restriction) rather than in data wrangling — a good fit for a methods-forward
article.

**Citations.**
- Frontiers in Pharmacology (2026), "Confounding-aware disproportionality analysis reveals disease-inherent versus drug-attributable endocrine safety signals of immune checkpoint inhibitors" — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2026.1868030/full ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1868030))
- *Journal of Investigative Dermatology*, "Disproportionality Analysis and the Evaluation of Drug Safety Signals" (methods review) — https://www.jidonline.org/article/S0022-202X(25)00472-5/fulltext
- According to PubMed: Gupta AK et al., "Oral finasteride use and sexual adverse events: signal detection from disproportionality analyses of FAERS," *J Dermatolog Treat* 2026 — nocebo/awareness confounding example ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/09546634.2025.2611703))

## 5. Did the COVID-vaccine myocarditis signal actually resolve? A VAERS caterpillar across formulation eras

**Working title:** "A Signal That Faded: Tracking mRNA-Vaccine Myocarditis Disproportionality
in VAERS Across Formulations"

**Safety question.** The claim now central to the 2026 ACIP debate is that the mRNA-vaccine
myocarditis signal was **real in 2021 but has since returned to background** as formulations
changed. VAERS can't measure incidence, but it can ask a narrower, testable question: does
the *disproportionality* (EB05) for myocarditis decay across successive vaccine eras
(original monovalent → bivalent → 2023–24 → 2024–25), or does it persist?

**Why it matters now.** In **March 2026** CIDRAP published op-eds arguing the myocarditis
signal "was real but is now resolved," citing real-world incidence falling to roughly **2 per
million doses** (bivalent), **~5 per million** (2023–24), and **~2 per million** (2024–25) in
12–39-year-olds, and criticizing ACIP's March agenda for re-litigating a resolved historical
signal. The debate is live and directly downstream of the site's existing VAERS work (the
Cotton piece established the myocarditis EB05 signal; this piece asks whether it *fades over
time* — a distinct, temporal question).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for
*Myocarditis*, *Pericarditis*, *Myopericarditis* stratified by vaccine formulation/era, then
the site's signature **EBGM caterpillar over quarters** to visualize whether EB05 trends
downward across eras. Includes the standard, essential caveat the site already deploys: VAERS
disproportionality is a within-database reporting comparison inflated by 2021 stimulated
reporting, so a *declining* EB05 across later formulations is more informative than the raw
2021 magnitude. Explicitly frames what VAERS can (relative reporting trend) and cannot
(incidence) settle.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low.** Myocarditis/pericarditis are among the best-populated
signals in the VAERS parquet already on hand; the only real work is a clean formulation/era
stratification (mapping VAERS vaccine-type/lot fields to formulation windows).

**Note on recency.** The anchoring op-eds are from **March 2026** (~4 months old), slightly
outside the 1–2-month window, but the ACIP controversy they describe is ongoing through 2026;
if a fresher regulator statement lands before drafting, lead with that instead.

**Citations.**
- CIDRAP Op-Ed (March 3, 2026), "The COVID vaccine myocarditis signal was real but is now resolved. ACIP's March agenda pretends otherwise." — https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/covid-19/cidrap-op-ed-covid-vaccine-myocarditis-signal-was-real-now-resolved-acips-march-agenda
- CIDRAP Op-Ed follow-up (March 19, 2026), "The report that proves the point—ACIP, COVID vaccines, and predetermined conclusions" — https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/covid-19/cidrap-op-ed-report-proves-point-acip-covid-vaccines-and-predetermined-conclusions
- medRxiv systematic review/meta-analysis (2025), COVID-19 vaccine myocarditis safety-signal methodology — https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.18.25329834.full.pdf
