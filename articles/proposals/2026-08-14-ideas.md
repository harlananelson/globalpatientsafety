# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-08-14

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot **plus**
quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
channelling, and confounding-by-indication caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

## 1. A new pill enters a class we can already read — a pre-exposure FAERS baseline for oral PCSK9

**Working title:** "Before the Prescriptions Arrive: Reading the PCSK9 Class in FAERS Before the
First Oral Inhibitor Scales"

**Safety question.** The FDA has just approved the first *oral* PCSK9 inhibitor. When a pill
version of an injectable class moves into primary care, prescribing volume can jump by an order
of magnitude — and with it, spontaneous reports. Before enlicitide generates its own FAERS
history, what does the *established* injectable PCSK9 class (alirocumab, evolocumab, inclisiran)
actually show under four-method disproportionality for the events that have dogged the class in
the literature — neurocognitive complaints, hepatic signals, new-onset dysglycaemia? A pre-exposure
class baseline is the honest reference frame: without it, the first wave of oral-agent reports
will be read against nothing, and every early signal will look novel when it may just be the
class's known reporting fingerprint plus notoriety from a heavily marketed launch.

**Why it matters now.** On **July 16, 2026** the FDA approved enlicitide (Lipfendra), the first
oral PCSK9 inhibitor, for LDL-lowering in adults with hypercholesterolaemia including HeFH; Phase
3 data showed ~56–59% placebo-adjusted LDL-C reduction at 24 weeks. An oral PCSK9 is explicitly
positioned for primary-care reach the injectables never had — the exact setting where reporting
volume, channelling, and launch publicity will distort early disproportionality. This is a
this-month regulatory event with a clean "establish the baseline before the surge" methodology
hook the site has flagged before (the teplizumab pre-expansion idea) but never drafted.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for the three
established PCSK9 agents against the historically-debated PT clusters — neurocognitive
(*Memory impairment*, *Cognitive disorder*, *Confusional state*), hepatic (*Hepatic enzyme
increased*, *Hepatic function abnormal*), glucose (*Blood glucose increased*, *Diabetes mellitus*),
and injection-site (a built-in negative control: an oral agent *cannot* reproduce injection-site
reports, so that cluster is a clean check that any oral-agent signal is not just inherited class
labelling). Show observed counts beside every EB05 and the quarterly caterpillar so the class's
stationary baseline is visible. The transferable lesson: a new route of administration resets the
reporting dynamics, and the only defence against over-reading the launch surge is a pre-registered
baseline computed *now*, before the reports arrive.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** The three injectable PCSK9 agents are well-covered
in FAERS with standard PTs; the baseline is straightforward. Enlicitide itself will have little to
no FAERS data yet — which is the point (baseline first), not a gap.

## 2. When disproportionality and a real cohort agree — the antipsychotic-hyponatremia worked example

**Working title:** "Two Methods, One Answer: What a JAMA Study Teaches About Trusting (and Not
Trusting) FAERS Disproportionality"

**Safety question.** Disproportionality can only measure *relative reporting*; a cohort study can
estimate *risk*. When do the two agree, and what does it mean when they do? A brand-new JAMA
Network Open study runs both on the same question — the comparative risk of hyponatremia across
second-generation antipsychotics — using FAERS reporting-odds against olanzapine as the reference,
*and* a propensity-weighted cohort of ~55,000 patients. Can the site reproduce the FAERS half
(most antipsychotics report hyponatremia *less* than olanzapine; aripiprazole notably lower) and
use the paper's cohort as the external check that tells us when a reporting-odds ranking is
tracking something real versus an artifact of who-reports-what?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Hatano M, Koseki T, Saito T, Yamada S, "Comparative
Risk of Hyponatremia Among Patients Treated With Second-Generation Antipsychotics," *JAMA Netw
Open*, published **August 3, 2026**, paired a FAERS disproportionality analysis (Q4 1997–Q3 2023,
reporting-odds vs olanzapine) with a Japanese claims cohort (new-user design, stabilized IPTW) and
found aripiprazole associated with significantly lower hyponatremia risk than olanzapine
(adjusted HR 0.52; 95% CI 0.35–0.76), with the FAERS ranking broadly concordant
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.28796)). A top-journal, this-month study that
puts spontaneous-reporting and cohort evidence side by side is a near-ideal teaching vehicle for
the site's core question — *what can disproportionality actually settle?*

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the reference-drug design: instead
of a whole-database background, compute EBGM/PRR/ROR/BCPNN for each SGA against **olanzapine as the
comparator** for *Hyponatraemia* / *Blood sodium decreased* / *SIADH*, and rank them. (2) The
methodology payload: show that an *active-comparator* disproportionality (vs a same-class drug)
sidesteps the confounding-by-indication that wrecks whole-database antipsychotic signals (everyone
is psychiatric), then use the published cohort HR as the external validity check — a live
demonstration that concordance between a reporting-odds ranking and a propensity-weighted cohort is
what promotes a signal from "hypothesis" to "corroborated." Explicitly state the asymmetry:
agreement strengthens confidence, but a FAERS-only ranking never yields incidence.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** SGAs are high-volume in FAERS with clean PTs, so the
reporting-odds ranking is tractable. The real work is implementing the *reference-drug* comparator
(vs olanzapine) rather than the default background — which is itself the article's methods lesson.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Hatano M, Koseki T, Saito T, Yamada S, "Comparative Risk of Hyponatremia
  Among Patients Treated With Second-Generation Antipsychotics," *JAMA Netw Open* 2026;9(8):e2628796
  — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.28796)
- According to PubMed (method precedent for reference-drug / active-comparator disproportionality):
  *Journal of Investigative Dermatology*, "Disproportionality Analysis and the Evaluation of Drug
  Safety Signals" — https://www.jidonline.org/article/S0022-202X(25)00472-5/fulltext

## 3. Reading a brand-new, unlabeled signal responsibly — bisphosphonates and glaucoma subtypes

**Working title:** "A Signal Nobody Has Labeled Yet: How to Read the Fresh Bisphosphonate–Glaucoma
Numbers Without Over-Believing Them"

**Safety question.** A just-published FAERS study reports disproportionality between specific
bisphosphonates and specific glaucoma *subtypes* — with time-to-onset ranging from 10 days to over
four years. Here there is no boxed warning, no litigation cluster, no FDA action: just a fresh,
plausible-but-unconfirmed signal resting on a few hundred reports. That makes it the perfect
teaching case for the discipline the site preaches — how do you read a *new* signal responsibly?
What does subtype granularity (open-angle vs angle-closure vs normal-tension) buy you, what does a
1,579-day median time-to-onset imply about causal plausibility versus surveillance artifact, and
why does a 539-report total demand the trajectory before any of it means anything?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Zhang Y, Huang YH, Xiao YL, Zhu XX, Guo MS,
"Bisphosphonates and glaucoma: a pharmacovigilance signal detection analysis using the FAERS
database," *Can J Ophthalmol*, published **August 12, 2026** (FAERS Q1 2004–Q1 2025), found
group-level signals for alendronic acid (ROR 2.42) and pamidronic acid (ROR 2.23), with
subtype-specific signals — alendronic acid/open-angle (ROR 5.67) and normal-tension (ROR 4.60);
pamidronic acid/open-angle (ROR 8.62); risedronic acid/angle-closure (ROR 2.93) — and median
time-to-onset from 10 days (risedronic acid) to 1,579 days (pamidronic acid), on 539 reports
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjo.2026.06.019)). It is fresh (this week), unlabeled, and
tailor-made for a "how to weigh a new signal" piece — bisphosphonates are taken by millions, so the
reach is broad while the honest conclusion is likely *watchful, not alarmed*.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run the four-method engine for the individual
bisphosphonates against the glaucoma PT cluster at *subtype* granularity (*Open angle glaucoma*,
*Angle closure glaucoma*, *Normal tension glaucoma*, *Glaucoma*, *Intraocular pressure increased*),
observed counts beside every EB05, plus the quarterly caterpillar. The methodology centrepiece is
threefold: (1) **PT granularity** — a subtype-specific signal is more interpretable than a lumped
"glaucoma" one, but each subtype splits an already-small N; show how the ≥2-methods + observed-count
rule protects against reading a 3-report cell as biology; (2) **time-to-onset as a plausibility
lens** — a 10-day onset and a 1,579-day onset cannot share a single mechanism, so heterogeneous TTO
is a flag to disaggregate, not to pool; (3) **the absence of a regulatory anchor** is itself the
lesson — with no label date to use as a natural experiment, the trajectory and observed counts are
*all* the evidence, and the honest verdict for a fresh small-N signal is "worth watching," not
"confirmed."

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Bisphosphonate strings are well-covered in FAERS; the
glaucoma-subtype PTs are specific but present. Small per-subtype counts are the analytic risk —
which is exactly the point of the piece, handled with the site's observed-count discipline.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Zhang Y, Huang YH, Xiao YL, Zhu XX, Guo MS, "Bisphosphonates and glaucoma: a
  pharmacovigilance signal detection analysis using the FAERS database," *Can J Ophthalmol* 2026 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcjo.2026.06.019)
- According to PubMed (companion example of time-to-onset / Weibull reading of FAERS signals):
  Teng Y et al., "Disproportionality analysis of adverse events associated with tyrosine kinase
  inhibitors in gastrointestinal stromal tumors in the FAERS," *Naunyn Schmiedebergs Arch Pharmacol*
  2026 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-026-05767-y)

## 4. A known label date as a natural experiment — glatiramer anaphylaxis, and whether the trajectory shows the warning

**Working title:** "Did the Boxed Warning Move the Reports? Reading the Glatiramer-Anaphylaxis
Signal Around a Known Date"

**Safety question.** In January 2025 the FDA added a boxed warning to glatiramer acetate (Copaxone,
Glatopa) for anaphylaxis. A boxed warning is a dated, public event — a natural experiment. A new
cross-database study (FAERS + Canada's CVAR) characterizes the anaphylaxis reporting profile and
finds an "early-failure" onset pattern (events cluster early, often within an hour of injection).
Two site-favourite questions follow: (1) does the FAERS *trajectory* show a stimulated-reporting
inflection after the warning — i.e. is some of the post-2025 signal the warning talking to itself?
— and (2) does a second national database (CVAR) corroborate the same PTs, which is the strongest
cheap check a spontaneous-reporting signal can get?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Nie T, Liu H, Cheng C, Li J, Lu J, "Reported
Characteristics of Anaphylaxis Associated With Glatiramer Acetate: A Pharmacovigilance Analysis of
the FDA and Canadian Databases," *Pharmacol Res Perspect*, published **August 2026** (FAERS Q1
2004–Q1 2025; CVAR Q1 1996–Q1 2025), identified 9,816 FAERS reports with 29 significant
disproportionality signals, all showing early-failure Weibull onset, and 17 PTs overlapping between
FAERS and CVAR ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.70303)). The regulatory anchor is concrete: the
FDA added the glatiramer anaphylaxis **boxed warning on January 22, 2025**, following a review of
82 anaphylaxis cases (1996–May 2024), 6 fatal. The fresh paper plus a hard label date is exactly
the "natural-experiment" structure the site used in the NAION/GLP-1 piece.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS + a cross-database check.** (1) Four-method engine
for glatiramer acetate against the anaphylaxis/allergic PT cluster (*Anaphylactic reaction*,
*Anaphylactic shock*, *Hypersensitivity*, *Flushing*, *Chest discomfort*, *Dyspnoea*), observed
counts beside every EB05, quarterly caterpillar. (2) The methodology payload: overlay the
**Jan-22-2025 boxed-warning date** on the trajectory and ask whether reporting inflects afterward —
a demonstration of the site's "known label date is a natural experiment / stimulated reporting"
gotcha, with the honest reading that a post-warning bump is *reporting behaviour*, not rising
incidence. (3) Use the Canadian CVAR overlap qualitatively as the corroboration lesson: a PT that
flags in two independent national systems is far harder to dismiss as one country's reporting
quirk. Frame the early-within-an-hour onset as biologically coherent (IgE-type reaction) while
stressing that coherence ≠ incidence.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Glatiramer is moderate-to-high volume in FAERS with clean
anaphylaxis PTs; the trajectory-around-a-date analysis is standard for the site. CVAR is used only
as a published corroboration reference (no need to reprocess Canadian data). **Freshness caveat:**
the boxed warning (Jan 2025) is >1 year old; the *fresh* hook is the August 2026 cross-database
paper. Include only if the batch wants a natural-experiment piece; if a newer glatiramer/anaphylaxis
regulatory action lands before drafting, lead with that.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Nie T, Liu H, Cheng C, Li J, Lu J, "Reported Characteristics of Anaphylaxis
  Associated With Glatiramer Acetate: A Pharmacovigilance Analysis of the FDA and Canadian
  Databases," *Pharmacol Res Perspect* 2026;14(4):e70303 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.70303)
- FDA Drug Safety Communication, "FDA adds Boxed Warning about a rare but serious allergic reaction
  called anaphylaxis with the multiple sclerosis medicine glatiramer acetate (Copaxone, Glatopa)"
  (Jan 22, 2025) —
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-adds-boxed-warning-about-rare-serious-allergic-reaction-called-anaphylaxis-multiple-sclerosis
- Pharmacy Times, "FDA Adds Boxed Warning to Glatiramer Acetate Over Risk of Rare, Serious Allergic

## 5. The signal that belongs to *neither* drug — isolating a drug–drug-interaction with a dual-comparator screen

**Working title:** "It's Not Either Drug Alone: How to Find a Drug–Interaction Signal FAERS Wasn't
Built to Show"

**Safety question.** Most of the site's pieces ask "does drug X flag for event Y?" But a whole class
of real harms belongs to a *combination*, not to either agent alone — and naive disproportionality
can't see them, because a combination-vs-background signal is contaminated by each drug's solo
effects. A new FAERS study on lithium + haloperidol (a common, decades-old psychiatric pairing with
a long-suspected neurotoxicity interaction) demonstrates the fix: a **dual-comparator screen** that
keeps only the signals reported *more* with the combination than with *either* drug used without the
other. Can the site reproduce the technique and teach why it — not a naive combination background —
is the correct tool for interaction signals?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Zhou C, Zheng L, Wu X, Xiong J, Zhang S,
"Characterization and clinical prioritization of neurotoxicity-related safety signals associated
with lithium and haloperidol co-administration: A pharmacovigilance study based on FAERS," *J Affect
Disord*, published **July 10, 2026** (FAERS Q1 2004–Q4 2025), stratified reports into
lithium-without-haloperidol (3,688), haloperidol-without-lithium (5,628), and co-administration
(429), and used report-level dual-comparator screening to retain 14 combination-specific signals
after sensitivity analysis — neuroleptic malignant syndrome, neurotoxicity, encephalopathy, and
delirium among them ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.122241)). Drug–drug interaction
signal detection is a methodology the site has *not* yet covered, it is squarely in the
"reading FDA data correctly" lane, and the lithium/haloperidol interaction is a real, textbook
clinical concern — a strong, distinctive teaching piece.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Show the failure mode first: run the standard
four-method engine for the lithium+haloperidol combination against a whole-database background and
demonstrate that the resulting "signals" are dominated by each drug's own well-known neuro/psychiatric
PTs — uninterpretable as an *interaction*. (2) Then the fix: implement the dual-comparator screen
(combination reports vs lithium-alone reports *and* vs haloperidol-alone reports), retaining only PTs
elevated against *both* single-agent comparators, for the neurotoxicity cluster (*Neuroleptic
malignant syndrome*, *Neurotoxicity*, *Encephalopathy*, *Delirium*, *Extrapyramidal disorder*).
Observed counts beside every estimate; a caterpillar where counts allow. The transferable lesson: a
combination signal is only an *interaction* signal if it survives subtraction of each drug's solo
reporting — and the small co-administration N (429) is a hard ceiling that mandates the site's
observed-count discipline and a "hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory" verdict.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium–High.** Lithium and haloperidol are individually high-volume,
so the solo strata are rich; the co-administration stratum is small (429 in the paper), which is the
analytic constraint. The three-way report stratification (combination / drug-A-alone / drug-B-alone)
is more wrangling than a standard single-drug run — but implementing it *is* the article's
methods content, and it gives the site a reusable drug-interaction harness.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Zhou C, Zheng L, Wu X, Xiong J, Zhang S, "Characterization and clinical
  prioritization of neurotoxicity-related safety signals associated with lithium and haloperidol
  co-administration: A pharmacovigilance study based on FAERS," *J Affect Disord* 2026;413:122241 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2026.122241)
- According to PubMed (methods context for combination / interaction signal detection with
  reference-group screening): Hatano M et al., "Comparative Risk of Hyponatremia Among Patients
  Treated With Second-Generation Antipsychotics," *JAMA Netw Open* 2026 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.28796)
