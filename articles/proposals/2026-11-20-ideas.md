# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-11-20

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS (now AEMS) using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR,
BCPNN/IC), EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot
**plus** quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
and confounding-by-indication caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the current queue (Zostavax-vs-Shingrix zoster,
GLP-1→NAION survivability, AEMS *data-pipeline* reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC) and all prior
batches (orlistat kidney, compounded GLP-1 dosing, teplizumab baseline, ICI endocrine
confounding, myocarditis-across-eras, sevoflurane subpopulation, atorvastatin huge-PRR,
teprotumumab hearing loss, vamorolone-vs-deflazacort, IXCHIQ, avacopan, masking, infant-RSV
baseline, orforglipron). Two ideas sit *near* existing items and are deliberately
differentiated in-line: **#2** (test FDA's *signal watchlist*, distinct from the queued
exercise of re-deriving a known EB05 to validate our own data pipeline) and **#5** (older-adult
RSV *vaccine* → GBS, distinct from the queued infant-nirsevimab *baseline* piece). No GLP-1
idea is proposed — note the GLP-1→NAION class-wide label did land Nov 9, 2026 (FDA DSC), which
is the natural-experiment date the *existing* queued NAION piece should now cite; it is not a
new article here.

## 1. A signal FAERS sees screaming — and three escalating label actions anyway

**Working title:** "PRR = 520 and Still a Boxed Warning: What the Injectafer Hypophosphatemia
Story Says About the Limits of a Loud Signal"

**Safety question.** On September 1, 2026 the FDA added a **boxed warning for symptomatic
hypophosphatemia** to ferric carboxymaltose (Injectafer) — the *third* escalation after label
changes in February 2020 and May 2023. Hypophosphatemia is already one of the loudest
disproportionality signals in the whole database for this drug (a 2025 FAERS/VigiBase study
reported **PRR ≈ 520, IC ≈ 8.0** in FAERS). So the signal was never hidden. The methodological
question is the interesting one: *what does it mean when disproportionality is maximally loud,
the mechanism is well understood, and regulators still had to escalate three times over six
years?* And why did it take **FDA Sentinel active-surveillance data** (phosphate testing in
fewer than 20% of administration episodes) rather than FAERS to move the needle to a boxed
warning?

**Why it matters now.** The boxed warning is dated **September 1, 2026** (FDA Drug Safety
Communication; revised 08/2026 label), and Injectafer/hypophosphatemia was already carried on
the FDA's **January–March 2026 AEMS "potential signals" quarterly report** as under evaluation.
FDA's stated rationale: symptomatic hypophosphatemia "continues to be reported" despite the
2020 and 2023 labeling changes, and Sentinel showed phosphate was checked in <20% of episodes.
Ferric carboxymaltose is high-volume (iron-deficiency anemia), so the piece has broad reach and
a clean, dated regulatory spine.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** (1) Reproduce the headline: four-method
engine for ferric carboxymaltose against *Hypophosphataemia* and the symptomatic downstream
cluster (*Osteomalacia*, *Bone pain*, *Fatigue*, *Muscular weakness*, *Fracture*). Confirm the
lab-value signal is enormous and show observed counts beside the EB05. (2) The methodology
payload — the **natural-experiment trajectory**: plot the quarterly caterpillar across the three
regulatory dates (2020, 2023, 2026) and ask whether reporting *responded* to each label action
or kept climbing (stimulated-reporting vs. true persistence). (3) The transferable lesson the
site hasn't made yet: **disproportionality magnitude is not regulatory sufficiency** — a PRR in
the hundreds tells you the association is real and reportable, but says nothing about whether
clinicians are *acting* on it, which is exactly the gap active surveillance (Sentinel) filled
here. Passive DA and active surveillance answer different questions; this is a worked example of
why you need both.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low.** Ferric carboxymaltose is high-volume with deep coverage
and a standard PT (*Hypophosphataemia*). The analysis is trivial; the value is the
trajectory-across-label-dates framing and the passive-vs-active-surveillance argument.

## 2. Grading the FDA's own homework: can independent disproportionality reproduce the AEMS watchlist?

**Working title:** "The FDA Published Its Suspect List — We Tested It: Reading the AEMS
'Potential Signals' Quarterly Report Through Four-Method Disproportionality"

**Safety question.** Every quarter the FDA publishes a list of **drug/event pairs it has
*first identified* as potential signals of serious risk** and is actively evaluating. These are
FDA's own signals — arrived at through a mix of data mining, case review, and regulatory
judgment, not pure disproportionality. So the honest methodological test is: **take the current
AEMS watchlist and run each pair through our independent four-method engine. Which reproduce,
which don't, and what does a mismatch teach?** A pair that FDA flags but DA *misses* is not FDA
being wrong — it is a lesson about what disproportionality structurally can and cannot see
(confounding by indication, rare events, case-level nuance DA throws away).

**Why it matters now.** The **April–June 2026 AEMS quarterly report** lists, among others,
**ABECMA (idecabtagene vicleucel) / immune effector cell-associated enterocolitis**,
**ALTUVIIIO / thromboembolic events**, and the anti-CD20 antibodies **(Briumvi, Kesimpta,
Ocrevus / Ocrevus Zunovo) / pneumonitis and serum sickness**; the **January–March 2026** report
lists **Beyfortus / immune thrombocytopenia**, **Elahere / extravasation**, **gadolinium-based
contrast agents / angioedema**, **Injectafer / hypophosphatemia**, and **Qelbree / seizure**.
This is a standing, dated, site-matched feed of exactly the kind of drug-event pairs the engine
is built to examine — and the site already has an AEMS data primer as its featured article, so
this is squarely on-brand.

**Distinct from the queued "AEMS reproducibility" item.** That queued piece re-derives a *known*
EB05 (e.g., the Cotton myocarditis result) from AEMS-era extract files to prove our *data
pipeline* survived the FAERS→AEMS migration. This piece instead tests FDA's *substantive signal
watchlist* — different question (do FDA's flagged pairs reproduce independently?), different
payload (what DA can/can't corroborate).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** Pick 3–4 tractable pairs from the live
watchlist and run the four-method engine for each, latest-quarter snapshot + caterpillar,
observed counts beside every EB05. Expected teaching contrasts: (a) **ALTUVIIIO / thromboembolic
events** — confounding by indication in its purest form (hemophilia patients receiving a
clotting factor), where a raw signal is near-uninterpretable without an active comparator; (b)
**Elahere / extravasation** and **Beyfortus / immune thrombocytopenia** — rare, recently flagged
events where small-N EB05 needs the trajectory before it means anything; (c) a pair that *does*
reproduce cleanly as the positive control. The through-line: **the FDA watchlist is a published
set of hypotheses, and disproportionality is one lens on them — agreement is corroboration,
disagreement is a lesson about the lens, and neither is a causation verdict.**

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The drugs are a mix of high- and low-volume; the
newer biologics (ABECMA, ALTUVIIIO, Elahere) will be small-N and need careful trajectory
handling. Mapping FDA's signal phrasing (e.g., "immune effector cell-associated enterocolitis")
to MedDRA PT clusters is the main wrangling, and is itself good methods content.

## 3. The AAV harm FAERS *can't* see: Zolgensma, insertional oncogenesis, and the mirror image of the liver story

**Working title:** "Nine Tumors in Six Thousand Patients: Why Disproportionality Caught the AAV
Liver Signal but Will Never Catch the Cancer One"

**Safety question.** On **November 6, 2026** the FDA issued a safety communication reporting
postmarketing **malignancies in Zolgensma (onasemnogene abeparvovec) recipients, including
*confirmed* insertional oncogenesis** — AAV vector DNA integrated near the **GLI1** gene in
tumor tissue — and extended the concern to the intrathecal formulation (Itvisma). Novartis has
reported **9 tumor cases out of >6,000 patients treated since 2019**. This is the exact inverse
of the site's existing (drafted) AAV-liver piece: hepatotoxicity after IV AAV is *acute,
common-ish, and route-driven*, so it lights up FAERS disproportionality; integration-driven
malignancy is *rare, long-latency, and biologically confirmed at the molecular level* — and is
therefore **structurally undetectable** by disproportionality. Can the site use the two AAV
harms side by side to teach *what determines whether a real effect is visible in a spontaneous
reporting database at all*?

**Why it matters now.** The FDA safety communication and MedWatch alert are dated **November 6,
2026** — the freshest possible hook. FDA notes vector DNA integrated near GLI1 in two cases
(a third mechanism still under investigation), tumors of the CNS/PNS, spinal/paraspinal regions,
and kidney, and that the existing label already carried a *theoretical* tumorigenicity warning
that is now being re-evaluated. FDA also cites two published AAV-integration tumors in *other*
programs (GLI1 in FBX-101, PLAG1 in RGX-111) and says it is monitoring neoplasia risk across the
AAV gene-therapy class — a class-level framing that fits the site's "is it the product or the
class?" lane.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** (1) The honest negative result: run the
four-method engine for onasemnogene abeparvovec against a neoplasm PT cluster and show that
9 events across a global program, with multi-year latency, produce *nothing* a disproportionality
threshold would flag — observed counts beside the (absent) EB05 make the point visually. (2) The
contrast: put it next to the AAV *hepatotoxicity* signal (the drafted liver piece), where acute,
clustered, route-driven events *do* clear threshold. (3) The transferable lesson: **spontaneous
reporting is an acute-event instrument.** Rare, delayed, mechanism-specific harms — insertional
oncogenesis being the textbook case — need long-term registries and molecular follow-up, not
disproportionality. A *flat* FAERS cancer signal for a gene therapy is not reassurance; it is a
demonstration of the instrument's blind spot for latency. Explicitly non-clinical framing: the
causation here is established by *molecular* evidence (vector integration), not by DA — which is
precisely why DA is the wrong tool.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Onasemnogene abeparvovec is a single, named,
low-volume product; pulling its reports is trivial. The analytical "work" is the disciplined
negative result and the paired contrast with the liver signal — a conceptual piece, strong fit
for the methodology lane. *Care:* avoid any implication of incidence/causation from the 9 cases;
the molecular-confirmation point must carry the causal weight, not the counts.

## 4. When a signal is loud in one database and silent in another — ranitidine, cancer, and cross-database divergence

**Working title:** "Same Drug, Different Database, Opposite Answer: Reading the Ranitidine–Cancer
Signal as a Lesson in What Disproportionality Can't Settle"

**Safety question.** Ranitidine (Zantac) was withdrawn in 2020 over NDMA contamination and is the
subject of mass litigation claiming cancer risk — a setting that generates enormous *stimulated*
reporting. A newly published analysis frames ranitidine-and-cancer explicitly as a lesson in
**FAERS data divergence**: the same drug-outcome question yields high-magnitude signals in one
spontaneous-reporting database and much weaker or absent ones in comparative pharmaco-
epidemiology, because cancer endpoints are the worst case for disproportionality (long latency,
**protopathic/reverse-causation bias**, litigation-driven reporting, and no exposure
denominator). Can the site reproduce the divergence and turn it into a transferable rule:
**cross-database reproducibility is an artifact filter — a signal that won't travel between
databases is a warning, not a discovery?**

**Why it matters now.** The methods anchor is fresh and dated: a **Drug Safety** paper published
**October 10, 2026**, "Disproportionality Analysis of FAERS Data Divergence and a Systematic
Review of Comparative Safety Studies: Lessons Learned from the Example of Ranitidine and Cancer
Risk," uses ranitidine-cancer as the worked example of why raw DA and comparative studies
disagree. A second, October 29, 2026 *PLoS ONE* framework paper on gynecological-malignancy
signal triage independently names **ranitidine and talc** as drugs showing "marked
disproportionality but … reporter and temporal features heavily compatible with stimulated
reporting," and notes that FAERS cancer time-to-onset reflects *administrative reporting
intervals*, not biological carcinogenesis — a clean, citable statement of the exact gotcha.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS** (with cross-database framing). (1)
Reproduce the loud raw signal: four-method engine for ranitidine against a GI/GU cancer PT
cluster, observed counts beside every EB05, and the quarterly caterpillar — which should show a
reporting *spike tied to the 2019–2020 withdrawal and litigation*, not a biological trajectory
(a natural-experiment date, as in the NAION piece). (2) The teaching payload: lay the raw DA
result next to what comparative epidemiology found (summarized from the Drug Safety review) and
make the methodological point directly — **when disproportionality and controlled studies
diverge for a latency-dominated endpoint, DA is the one to distrust.** Protopathic bias,
litigation-cluster stimulated reporting, and the missing denominator are the three named
mechanisms. Extends the site's existing "the raw leaderboard lies" and "cross-database
reproducibility" threads to the hardest endpoint class (cancer).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Ranitidine is high-volume with deep historical
coverage; the raw signal is trivial to reproduce. The genuine work is honest framing — the site
runs FAERS, not a comparative cohort, so the cross-database contrast is *cited* (from the Drug
Safety review) rather than recomputed, and the article must be explicit that its own
contribution is the raw-DA half plus the interpretation, not a new epidemiological study.

**Citations.**
- "Disproportionality Analysis of FAERS Data Divergence and a Systematic Review of Comparative
  Safety Studies: Lessons Learned from the Example of Ranitidine and Cancer Risk," *Drug Safety*
  (published Oct 10, 2026) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-026-01730-1)
- According to PubMed: Ma Y, Li D, Duan J, et al., "Gynecological malignancy signal triage: A

  https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-requests-removal-all-ranitidine-products-zantac-market

## 5. A small, real vaccine signal — can passive VAERS see what active surveillance found? (RSV → GBS)

**Working title:** "Forty-Two Days and a Confounder: Testing the Older-Adult RSV-Vaccine
Guillain-Barré Signal in VAERS, With the Age Skew and the Disease Itself Accounted For"

**Safety question.** The older-adult RSV vaccines (Abrysvo, Arexvy) carry an FDA label mention
of Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS), and the latest season-review evidence continues to describe a
**possible increased GBS risk in the 42 days after vaccination in adults ≥60**, flagged by a
self-controlled case series while randomized trials saw none. This is the mirror of several
site themes: a *small, genuine, regulator-recognized* signal detected by **active surveillance
(SCCS)** — does **passive VAERS disproportionality** corroborate it, and only with **age
stratification**? And there is a subtle, citable confounder the piece can foreground: **RSV
*disease itself* raises GBS risk** (a Medicare SCCS found an adjusted IRR ≈ 2.1, rising to ≈ 4
in those ≥75), so the background rate against which a vaccine signal is read is not flat.

**Why it matters now.** The anchor is current: the **JAMA "RSV Immunization Effectiveness and
Safety for the 2026–2027 Respiratory Season"** review (published **September 2, 2026**)
reaffirms that "among older adults, 1 self-controlled case series reported a possible increased
risk of GBS during the 42 days following vaccination," while noting trials found none — i.e.,
the signal is live and still under monitoring entering this season. The companion influenza
review (same date) found *no* GBS signal for flu vaccine in a ~10-million-person SCCS, giving a
clean contrast. The RSV-disease-GBS confounder is established in a 2026 *Clinical Infectious
Diseases* Medicare SCCS.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for the RSV
vaccines (Abrysvo, Arexvy; and, as a contrast, nirsevimab/Beyfortus) against *Guillain-Barré
syndrome* and the related neuro cluster (*Miller Fisher syndrome*, *Demyelinating polyneuropathy*,
*Facial paralysis*), **stratified by age band (≥60, ≥75)**, with the caterpillar over available
quarters and observed counts beside every EB05. The methodology payload: (1) a pooled VAERS
signal can *understate* a risk concentrated in the oldest band — age stratification changes the
read (as in the proposed IXCHIQ age-skew piece, but here against a real label signal); (2)
**active (SCCS) vs. passive (DA) surveillance** detect differently — a small 42-day-window
signal may be near-threshold or invisible in raw DA, which is itself the honest finding; (3) the
stimulated-reporting caveat is doubly important given RSV-vaccine media attention, and the
RSV-disease-GBS background must be stated plainly as a reason DA alone cannot settle causation.
Explicit what-VAERS-can (relative reporting pattern, age skew) and cannot (incidence, causation)
framing.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** RSV adult vaccines launched in 2023, so counts are
modest and several age-stratified EB05 estimates may sit below threshold — which is the honest
result, not a failure. Clean mapping of RSV vaccine type codes and age bands in VAERS is the
main wrangling; separating the vaccine products (Abrysvo vs Arexvy) matters for the read.

**Note on scope.** Distinct from the queued *infant-nirsevimab VAERS baseline* idea (that one
baselines a product *before* a signal; this one tests an *existing, older-adult* GBS signal). If
a fresher ACIP/FDA RSV action lands before drafting, lead with it.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Senerth E, Sheikholeslamian SM, Babatunde I, et al., "RSV Immunization
  Effectiveness and Safety for the 2026-2027 Respiratory Season," *JAMA* (published Sept 2, 2026;
  older-adult SCCS GBS signal in the 42-day window) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.17871)
- According to PubMed: Liang C, Judy J, Chilson EL, et al., "Estimating Risk of Guillain-Barré
  Syndrome in US Medicare-enrolled Older Adults Following Medically Attended Respiratory Syncytial
  Virus Disease: A Self-controlled Case Series Analysis," *Clin Infect Dis* 2026;82(4):e799–e806
  (RSV *disease* → GBS IRR ≈ 2.1; the background confounder) —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/cid/ciaf621)
- According to PubMed: Senerth E, et al., "Influenza Vaccine Effectiveness and Safety for the
  2026-2027 Respiratory Season," *JAMA* (published Sept 2, 2026; ~9.97M-person SCCS, *no* GBS
  signal — the clean contrast) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.18126)
