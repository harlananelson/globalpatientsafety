# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-11-13

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/AEMS and VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR,
BCPNN/IC), EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot
**plus** quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece and the carbidopa/levodopa "warning built on 14
cases" piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what disproportionality can and cannot
show; careful with stimulated-reporting, notoriety, litigation-cluster, reverse-causation, and
confounding-by-indication caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed from
our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

## 1. Grading the FDA's own watchlist: which "Potential Signals of Serious Risks" can disproportionality actually see?

**Working title:** "The FDA Publishes What It's Watching. We Checked Which Signals the Data
Already Shows — and Which It Can't."

**Safety question.** Every quarter the FDA posts a public table of **Potential Signals of
Serious Risks** identified in its adverse-event system — drug/event pairs the agency has decided
are worth evaluating "the need for regulatory action," but has *not* yet concluded on. This is
the regulator stating a hypothesis out loud, with a date attached. The natural site question:
take the newest quarter's list and run each named pair through the four-method engine. Which
flagged pairs are *already* loud, well-populated signals (the agency is confirming what the data
shows)? Which are small-N or invisible in disproportionality (the agency is acting on case-level
review, Sentinel, or literature — not on a reporting-rate signal)? The gap between the two is the
whole lesson: **a spot on the FDA watchlist is not the same thing as a disproportionality
signal, and neither one is causation.**

**Why it matters now.** The watchlist is current and dated. The **April–June 2026** table
(posted ~2026-09-04) includes **Nardil (phenelzine) → drug-induced liver injury** and an
**orexin-receptor-antagonist (incl. Belsomra/suvorexant)** entry, each marked "FDA evaluating
the need for regulatory action." The **January–March 2026** table (updated 2026-06-04) carries
three **Tepezza (teprotumumab)** signals — **hypertension, encephalopathy, hypersensitivity** —
alongside severe-cutaneous-reaction entries for products such as **Braftovi** and **Kesimpta**.
These are live, unresolved regulatory hypotheses on this quarter's official list — the freshest
possible "reading FDA data" material, and a format the site has not used: *reproducing the
regulator's own watchlist* rather than picking a pair ourselves.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** For each named pair, run GPS/EBGM + PRR +
ROR + BCPNN against the latest-quarter snapshot, print observed counts beside every EB05, and
show the quarterly caterpillar. Expected spread of outcomes is the article's spine: Tepezza is a
known, moderate-volume drug where several of its listed signals (and its *already-labeled*
hearing loss, from the queued/retired-from-this-batch teprotumumab work) should be populated and
visible; Nardil is a decades-old, low-volume MAOI where a DILI signal may rest on few reports;
the orexin-antagonist entry lets us show a class vs single-agent read. The transferable gotchas:
(1) the FDA acts on more than disproportionality (individual causality assessment, Sentinel,
literature), so **a flat signal is not a refutation of an FDA action**; (2) conversely, a loud
EB05 on a watchlist pair is corroboration, not proof; (3) watchlist inclusion itself can
*stimulate* reporting — a notoriety check keyed to the posting date applies directly.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** The pairs are named explicitly by the FDA, so
there is no term-hunting; Tepezza and orexin antagonists are well-covered. The only care needed
is Nardil's old/sparse reporting and mapping the orexin class (suvorexant/lemborexant/
daridorexant) cleanly. Confirm the exact table contents on fda.gov before drafting (see network
note above).

## 2. Thirteen drugs "linked to dementia" — a clinic in reverse causation and the drugs-of-aging trap

**Working title:** "Apixaban, Insulin, a Bisphosphonate — and Rofecoxib: Why a FAERS
'Dementia Drug' Leaderboard Is Almost All Artifact"

**Safety question.** A November 2026 FAERS study used LASSO variable selection plus four
disproportionality methods to name **13 drugs associated with dementia-related adverse-event
reports** — apixaban, insulin glargine, tafamidis, insulin lispro, sacubitril/valsartan,
quetiapine, zoledronic acid, alendronic acid, enzalutamide, pregabalin, rofecoxib, dapagliflozin,
valproic acid. Nearly every one is a drug of older age (anticoagulants, insulins, heart-failure
and bone and prostate-cancer therapies). Dementia is, overwhelmingly, a condition of older age.
The site's question: when you run these pairs in the four-method engine, how much of each
"signal" survives once you reckon with **confounding by age/indication and protopathic/reverse
causation** — the patient was already developing dementia, or was simply old enough to, when the
drug was started? This is the "raw leaderboard lies" gotcha in one of its purest forms, on an
emotionally charged endpoint.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Huang M, Ren H, Ren X, et al., "Drugs Associated
with Dementia-Related Adverse Event Reports: A Real World Pharmacovigilance Study," *Braz J
Psychiatry*, published **2026-11-06**, reports the 13-drug list above (FAERS 2004Q1–2025Q1), a
median time-to-dementia of 73 days, and ranks the agents by mortality/disability within
dementia reports ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.47626/1516-4446-2026-4811)). A 73-day median from drug
start to a *dementia* report is itself a red flag: dementia does not develop in ten weeks, so the
reports are capturing recognition/coding of pre-existing decline, not drug-induced disease — a
textbook protopathic pattern the site can make visible. Broad public interest + a clean teaching
target.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** (1) Reproduce the disproportionality for
a handful of the named pairs (e.g., apixaban, zoledronic acid, pregabalin, rofecoxib) against a
dementia PT cluster (*Dementia*, *Dementia Alzheimer's type*, *Alzheimer's disease*, *Cognitive
disorder*, *Memory impairment*), observed counts beside every EB05. (2) The teaching payload:
show the time-to-onset distribution (a dementia "caused" within weeks is reverse causation); show
how an **age-restricted or active-comparator** read collapses signals driven purely by the
drug's elderly indication; and use **rofecoxib** (withdrawn 2004, so its reports are a frozen
historical cohort) as a reporting-era artifact illustration. The transferable lesson: on
age-correlated endpoints, a disproportionality leaderboard mostly ranks *how old the typical
user is*, not *what the drug does* — and LASSO on confounded features launders that into a
tidy-looking list.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** All 13 are high- or moderate-volume with standard
PTs; the dementia cluster is well-defined. The work is the honest framing (age/indication, TTO,
reverse causation), which is the article's methods content, not data wrangling.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Huang M, Ren H, Ren X, Wen P, Hu J, Xu K, Liu L, Yang Z, Zhang M, Yang M,
  Su Y, "Drugs Associated with Dementia-Related Adverse Event Reports: A Real World
  Pharmacovigilance Study," *Braz J Psychiatry* 2026 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.47626/1516-4446-2026-4811)
- According to PubMed (method/analogy for reverse-causation framing): Melamed B, et al.,
  "Semaglutide and Panic Attacks: Active-Comparator Disproportionality Analysis, Temporal
  Assessment of Notoriety Bias and Evidence Triangulation …," *Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf* 2026
  — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.70517)

## 3. A class is not a drug: QT, ondansetron, and why within-class heterogeneity breaks "the class flags for X"

**Working title:** "Ondansetron ROR 27, Olanzapine ROR 9: The Antiemetic Class Hides a
Ten-to-One Spread — and a 2012 FDA Dose Cut to Test It Against"

**Safety question.** A November 2026 clinical review (synthesizing FAERS among other sources)
reports that antiemetic QT-prolongation risk is **highly heterogeneous within classes** — a
2026 FAERS disproportionality analysis gave **ondansetron a QT-related ROR of 27.2 vs
olanzapine's 8.7**, consistent with ondansetron's dose-dependent IKr/hERG blockade, while
**palonosetron has minimal effect** despite being the same 5-HT3-antagonist class. The site's
question: can the four-method engine reproduce the *within-class spread* (not just "antiemetics
flag for QT"), and does the signal for high-dose ondansetron bend around the **2012 FDA
restriction of the 32 mg IV dose** — a clean natural-experiment date? The transferable lesson is
one the site's gotcha catalogue hasn't stated explicitly: **"the class flags for X" is almost
always an average that hides agent-level differences large enough to flip clinical decisions.**

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Moustafa AT, Dabash O, Panigrahi PK, Khan HR,
"Antiemetic-associated QT prolongation: a clinical review and risk-stratified prescribing
framework for long QT syndrome," *Eur Heart J Cardiovasc Pharmacother*, published **2026-11-04**,
cites the ondansetron-vs-olanzapine ROR contrast (27.2 vs 8.7) from a 2026 FAERS analysis and
the 2012 FDA 32 mg IV ondansetron restriction, and lays out the per-agent QT-liability spread
across 5-HT3 antagonists, butyrophenones, phenothiazines, and NK-1 antagonists
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjcvp/pvag077)). Antiemetics are among the most prescribed drugs
worldwide, so a concrete "don't read a class as one drug" lesson has wide reach.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** Four-method engine for each antiemetic
agent (ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, promethazine,
droperidol, olanzapine) against a QT/arrhythmia PT cluster (*Electrocardiogram QT prolonged*,
*Torsade de pointes*, *Ventricular tachycardia*, *Ventricular fibrillation*, *Sudden cardiac
death*, *Cardiac arrest*). Plot EB05 **by agent** with observed counts to show the spread; then
the ondansetron caterpillar across quarters, annotated with the 2012 dose restriction, to test
whether the reporting signal attenuated after the high-dose cut. Caveats: QT is a
stimulated/known effect for several agents (differential reporting), and indication mixes
(chemotherapy vs post-op vs pregnancy) differ by agent. The payload: a class average is a
statistical fiction when agent-level EB05 spans ~3× and the mechanism (IKr blockade) is
dose-dependent.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Multiple agents means several contingency tables, and a
few (droperidol, palonosetron) are lower-volume. The 2012 natural-experiment date is older than
the usual 1–2-month hook, but the *paper* framing it is this-month and the within-class-spread
lesson is the durable content.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Moustafa AT, Dabash O, Panigrahi PK, Khan HR, "Antiemetic-associated QT
  prolongation: a clinical review and risk-stratified prescribing framework for long QT
  syndrome," *Eur Heart J Cardiovasc Pharmacother* 2026;12(7):556–567 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjcvp/pvag077)
- FDA Drug Safety Communication (2012), "Updated information on 32 mg intravenous ondansetron
  (Zofran) dose and pre-mixed ondansetron products" (the natural-experiment date) —
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-drug-safety-communication-updated-information-32-mg-intravenous-ondansetron-zofran-dose-and

## 4. The signal that disappears because the database is 81% something else — RSV-vaccine GBS in VAERS

**Working title:** "Denominator Dilution: Why COVID Reports Can Hide a Real GBS Signal — and
How the RSV Vaccines Surfaced Anyway"

**Safety question.** A 21-year VAERS analysis finds disproportionate Guillain-Barré-syndrome
reporting for **both RSV vaccines (Arexvy ROR 3.01, Abrysvo ROR 6.82)** and zoster vaccine,
consistent with post-marketing estimates of **3–9 excess GBS cases per million doses** — but
reports **no concordant GBS signal for COVID-19 vaccines**, which the authors attribute to
**denominator dilution**: COVID reports are 81% of the whole database, swamping the ratio. This
is the mirror image of the site's sevoflurane "invisible subpopulation" idea, but on the *other*
axis — a real signal washed out not by a small numerator but by a **giant, unrelated
denominator**. The site's question: reproduce the RSV/zoster GBS signals with the four-method
engine and the project's consensus filter, show the characteristic **delayed onset** (median
~13 days, 8–30-day autoimmune window), and demonstrate how stratifying or restricting the
comparator set rescues the diluted COVID read.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Su Y, Huang Y, Pan L, Fu H, Wang X, "Temporal and
disproportionality analysis of vaccine-associated Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS
study," *Front Neurol*, published **2026-08-04** (VAERS 2004–2024; 9.76M reports, 2,216 GBS
cases; four algorithms ROR/PRR/IC/EBGM), reports the RSV and zoster novel signals, the COVID
denominator-dilution null, and the delayed-onset pattern
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)). The regulatory anchor is current: both RSV
vaccine package inserts have carried a **GBS warning since January 2025**, and a **JAMA**
analysis of the **2026–2027 respiratory season** (published Oct 2026) revisits RSV immunization
effectiveness and safety including GBS — so this is a live, this-season vaccine-safety question.
It also sits squarely in the site's existing VAERS vaccine lane (COVID, shingles, Christine
Cotton).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for Arexvy,
Abrysvo, and (as the clean positive control) zoster vaccines against *Guillain-Barré syndrome*
and related PTs (*Miller Fisher syndrome*, *Demyelinating polyneuropathy*), with observed counts
and the consensus filter. Three methods payloads: (1) **denominator dilution** — recompute the
COVID-vaccine GBS read against (a) the whole database vs (b) a vaccine-only or respiratory-
vaccine-only comparator set, and show the signal re-emerge, demonstrating that comparator/
denominator choice can manufacture *or erase* a signal; (2) **time-to-onset as corroboration** —
plot the onset distribution and show the biologically plausible 8–30-day cluster vs day-0 noise;
(3) the standard **absolute-risk reframing** — 1–9 per million doses against disease-prevention
benefit, stated plainly so the piece informs rather than alarms. Explicit what-VAERS-can/cannot
show throughout.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** GBS is a well-coded, serious, well-reported PT; RSV
and zoster vaccines are identifiable in VAERS. The interesting work is the denominator/comparator
reconstruction and the onset-window plot — both methods content. The site already has VAERS
tooling from the COVID/shingles pieces.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Su Y, Huang Y, Pan L, Fu H, Wang X, "Temporal and disproportionality
  analysis of vaccine-associated Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS study," *Front Neurol*
  2026;17:1814946 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)
- JAMA, "RSV Immunization Effectiveness and Safety for the 2026–2027 Respiratory Season"
  (Oct 2026; GBS warning context) — https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2853780
- CDC, "RSV Vaccine Guidance / Safety" and the January 2025 RSV-vaccine GBS label warning
  (regulatory anchor; verify current wording) —
  https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/vpd/rsv/index.html

## 5. Two techniques the site hasn't used: sex-stratified signals and cross-database replication (allopurinol vs febuxostat)

**Working title:** "A Signal That Only Shows Up in Women (or Men): Sex-Stratified
Disproportionality, Checked Twice Across Two Databases"

**Safety question.** A September 2026 study ran **sex-stratified** disproportionality for the
gout drugs allopurinol and febuxostat across **two independent systems (FAERS and
EudraVigilance)**, and found signals that are strongly sex-specific: allopurinol → **pancytopenia
in women** (ROR 6.87; 271 cases), febuxostat → **rhabdomyolysis in men** (ROR 4.55; 79 cases),
with directional consistency across databases. A pooled, both-sexes analysis would blur both.
The site's question is methodological, not clinical: what do you gain by (a) **splitting the
contingency table by sex** before computing EB05, and (b) **replicating in a second database**
before believing a signal? Both are standard pharmacovigilance moves the site's pieces have not
yet demonstrated, and they're the natural next additions to the "reading FDA data" toolkit.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Chen Y, Lu W, Tan L, Liao Z, Li L, Liu C, He W, Wang
W, Feng H, "Sex-stratified disproportionality signals of adverse events with allopurinol and
febuxostat: a retrospective pharmacovigilance analysis of FAERS and EudraVigilance," *Clin
Rheumatol*, published **2026-09-30** (FAERS 2004Q1–2024Q4, EudraVigilance confirmation), reports
the sex-specific allopurinol/febuxostat signals above with cross-database directional consistency
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-026-08452-w)). It pairs naturally with the September 2026
HIV-PrEP FAERS study showing that **comparator choice** reshapes which signals appear
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40261-026-01602-7)) — together making a tidy "the analysis
choices *are* the result" methods piece. Gout drugs are high-volume and familiar, and allopurinol
hypersensitivity (toward the SCAR/HLA-B\*58:01 story) gives an optional pharmacogenomics coda.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/AEMS.** (1) Compute the four-method engine for
allopurinol and febuxostat against the named clusters (*Pancytopenia*/*Agranulocytosis*/
*Aplastic anaemia*; *Rhabdomyolysis*/*Blood creatine phosphokinase increased*) **pooled**, then
**split by sex**, and show pairs where the pooled EB05 understates one sex and overstates the
other. (2) Treat EudraVigilance (public line-listing exports) as the replication check — same
direction = more trustworthy; divergence = a prompt to look for a reporting/channelling
explanation. Caveats the piece must foreground: sex-stratification multiplies comparisons (guard
against spurious sex-specific hits), the two databases overlap in sources, and raw ROR without
exposure denominators can't give incidence. The transferable lesson: **a single pooled EB05 from
a single database is the *least* evidence you can report** — stratify and replicate before you
believe it.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Allopurinol/febuxostat are well-covered in FAERS;
sex-split tables are a straightforward recompute. The replication arm requires pulling
EudraVigilance public data (format differs from FAERS) — the only real wrangling, and itself part
of the article's methods demonstration.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Chen Y, Lu W, Tan L, Liao Z, Li L, Liu C, He W, Wang W, Feng H,
  "Sex-stratified disproportionality signals of adverse events with allopurinol and febuxostat:
  a retrospective pharmacovigilance analysis of FAERS and EudraVigilance," *Clin Rheumatol*
  2026;45(11):6947–6959 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-026-08452-w)
- According to PubMed: Rozza D, Poluzzi E, Antonazzo IC, et al., "Formulation-Specific Safety
  Issues of HIV Pre-exposure Prophylaxis Drugs: A Disproportionality Analysis of the FAERS
  Database," *Clin Drug Investig* 2026;46(11):1327–1339 (companion "comparator choice changes the
  result" example) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40261-026-01602-7)
