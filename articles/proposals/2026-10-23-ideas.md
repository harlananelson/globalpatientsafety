# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-10-23

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months (Sept–Oct 2026), matched to what this site already does: independent
disproportionality reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR,
ROR, BCPNN/IC), EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot
**plus** quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
and confounding caveats.

**A through-line this week: the comparator *is* the method.** Three of these ideas (ranitidine,
daridorexant, PrEP) turn on the same move — a raw, whole-database signal that **dissolves,
survives, or changes meaning** once you pick the right comparator — but each teaches a
*different* facet of it (litigation-driven reporting, confounding by indication/co-report,
formulation vs. clinical context). The remaining two add genuinely different gotchas:
**denominator dilution** (one product's mega-reporting *masking* real signals for others) and
the **invisible lab harm** (a boxed warning for something FAERS structurally under-captures).

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the current queue (Zostavax-vs-Shingrix zoster, GLP-1→NAION
survivability, AEMS reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC), the 2026-07-03 batch (orlistat kidney,
compounded GLP-1 dosing errors, teplizumab pediatric baseline, ICI endocrine confounding,
myocarditis-across-eras), and the 2026-07-24 batch (sevoflurane invisible subpopulation,
atorvastatin huge-PRR-on-tiny-N, teprotumumab hearing loss, vamorolone-vs-deflazacort,
IXCHIQ chikungunya). No GLP-1 idea is proposed. Note: a new VAERS herpes-zoster paper landed
this cycle (see *Also considered*) — it **corroborates** the queued Zostavax piece rather than
replacing it, so that queue item stands.

## 1. When a cancer "signal" is really a lawsuit: the ranitidine (Zantac) FAERS reanalysis

**Working title:** "140,000 Reports, Zero Carcinogenicity: How Litigation and a Recall Manufacture
a FAERS Cancer Signal — and the Comparator That Erases It"

**Safety question.** Ranitidine (Zantac) was withdrawn globally in 2020 after NDMA (a probable
human carcinogen) was found in the product. In the unrestricted FAERS database, ranitidine lights
up with huge disproportionality for multiple cancers. But was that a carcinogenicity signal, or
the fingerprint of a recall-and-litigation reporting surge? Can the site's four-method engine
reproduce the two corrections that make the signal vanish — an **active comparator** (famotidine,
a same-class H2 blocker with no NDMA story) and a **time-restricted** (pre- vs post-recall) split —
and use the contrast to teach *why the raw leaderboard lied here*?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, a methods paper published in *Drug Safety* on
**October 10, 2026** reanalyzed ranitidine-cancer signals across FAERS and Japan's JADER with an
active comparator (famotidine) and pre-/post-recall time restriction. It reports a **surge of
140,985 ranitidine cancer reports in FAERS during 2020–21 — more than two-thirds of all cancer
reports in that window** — overwhelmingly consumer-submitted and geographically clustered in
regions with regulatory action and litigation. Unrestricted analysis showed high-magnitude GI and
GU cancer signals; those were **markedly attenuated or absent** under active-comparator and
time-restricted analysis, with ranitidine and famotidine reporting profiles converging. In JADER,
a suspect-drug signal (PRR = 3.54) **vanished to PRR = 0.92** when all involvement types were
included — a protopathic-bias signature. Most large cohort studies in the paper's systematic review
found no increased cancer risk. *Drug Safety* is the field's flagship methods journal, so this is
both a fresh, dated hook and a direct extension of the site's gotcha catalogue — **"the raw
leaderboard lies"** and a clean worked example of litigation-cluster + protopathic + stimulated
reporting all at once.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the reporting surge: plot the
quarterly trajectory of ranitidine cancer reports across the 2019–2021 recall window and show the
2020–21 spike (the natural-experiment date, as in the NAION piece). (2) Run the four-method engine
unrestricted (ranitidine vs. whole database) for a cancer PT cluster and show the large EB05s.
(3) Switch to the **active comparator** (ranitidine vs. famotidine) and the **time-restricted**
pre-recall window, and show the signal collapse — observed counts beside every EB05 throughout.
The transferable lesson: a disproportionality signal that evaporates under a within-class comparator
or a pre-event time window was never about the drug; it was about who was reporting and why. Ties to
the site's familiar "consumer-report surge" and "the calendar is a natural experiment" themes.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Ranitidine and famotidine are both high-volume with
deep FAERS coverage; the recall window is well-defined. The only real work is a defensible cancer
PT cluster and the pre-/post-recall split — which is itself the article's methods content. Lowest-risk,
highest-payoff idea of the batch.

## 2. The signal that hid behind COVID: denominator dilution and the RSV-vaccine GBS finding (VAERS)

**Working title:** "When One Vaccine Floods the Database: How 7.9 Million COVID Reports Can *Hide*
a Real Guillain-Barré Signal (VAERS)"

**Safety question.** Disproportionality compares a drug/vaccine's report share against a background.
But what happens when a *single* product comes to dominate the entire background? A 21-year VAERS
study finds concordant Guillain-Barré (GBS) signals for both RSV vaccines and the zoster vaccine —
yet **no** concordant COVID-vaccine GBS signal, which the authors attribute not to safety but to
**denominator dilution**: COVID reports are ~81% of the database, so COVID's own events inflate the
background against which COVID is measured. This is the mirror image of notoriety bias (where
publicity *creates* a signal): here, mega-reporting *masks* one. It is a gotcha the site has not yet
written, and it has a concrete regulatory anchor in the RSV vaccines.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, "Temporal and disproportionality analysis of
vaccine-associated Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS study," *Frontiers in Neurology*,
published **August 4, 2026**, analyzed 9,755,435 VAERS reports (2004–2024; 2,216 GBS cases) with
four algorithms (ROR, PRR, IC, EBGM). It reports **novel GBS signals for both RSV vaccines (Arexvy
ROR 3.01, Abrysvo ROR 6.82) and zoster vaccine (ROR 3.01)**, consistent with post-marketing
estimates of 3–9 excess cases per million doses, and a biologically plausible delayed onset (median
13 days). Critically, **COVID-19 vaccines showed no concordant signal despite established
Janssen/GBS associations, which the authors attribute to denominator dilution from 7.89 million
COVID reports (81% of the database)** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)). The
regulatory anchor is dated and real: on **January 7, 2025** FDA required a GBS warning in the
prescribing information for both Abrysvo and Arexvy, based on trial data, VAERS, and a Medicare
post-marketing study. A VAERS-native methodology piece with a flagship regulatory hook.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** (1) Reproduce the headline: four-method
disproportionality for Arexvy, Abrysvo, and zoster vaccine against a GBS PT cluster (*Guillain-Barré
syndrome*, *Miller Fisher syndrome*, *Demyelinating polyneuropathy*, *Areflexia*), with observed
counts and the quarterly caterpillar — including the ~8–30-day onset window as a plausibility check.
(2) The methods centerpiece: **demonstrate the masking directly.** Recompute the COVID-vaccine GBS
signal (a) against the full VAERS background and (b) against a background with COVID reports removed,
and show the signal emerge once the denominator is no longer dominated by COVID. The transferable
lesson: disproportionality's background is not neutral — a single high-volume product can suppress
*other* products' signals, so a flat signal in a COVID-saturated database is not reassurance.
Explicit on what VAERS can (relative reporting, onset timing) and cannot (incidence, causation) show,
and that excess-risk magnitudes here are tiny and outweighed by prevention benefit.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The vaccines are identifiable by VAERS vaccine-type codes;
RSV-vaccine reports are newer and lower-volume (small-N caveats on some PTs). The masking
demonstration requires a careful background-set manipulation (remove-COVID-and-recompute), which is
straightforward in principle but must be documented transparently so it reads as a method, not a
thumb on the scale.

**Citations.**

  of vaccine-associated Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS study," *Front Neurol* 2026;17:1814946
  — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)
- FDA, "FDA Requires Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) Warning in the Prescribing Information for the RSV
  Vaccines Abrysvo and Arexvy" (January 7, 2025) —

## 3. A suicidal-ideation signal that survives "all drugs" but not "other sleeping pills": daridorexant

**Working title:** "Compared to What? A Daridorexant Suicidal-Ideation Signal That Depends Entirely
on the Comparator You Chose"

**Safety question.** Daridorexant (Quviviq), a newer dual orexin-receptor antagonist (DORA) for
insomnia, has shown a significant suicidal-ideation signal in FAERS *when compared against all other
drugs*. But insomnia itself — and the depression that so often travels with it — is a powerful
confounder. Does the signal survive a **comparator-restricted** analysis (daridorexant vs. other
insomnia medications, within insomnia-indication reports), and what happens when co-reported
depression is removed? This is the purest form of the site's "confounding by indication" lesson,
applied to a drug class the site has not yet covered.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, "Suicidal ideation associated with daridorexant
treatment in insomnia patients: Comparator-restricted disproportionality analysis and assessment of
potential heterogeneity in reporting patterns," *Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology*,
published **October 16, 2026**, reports that the suicidal-ideation signal — significant against all
other drugs in prior work — **was no longer significant** under comparator-restricted analysis using
insomnia-indication reports and common insomnia medications as comparators, and **weakened further**
when reports with co-reported depression-related events were excluded (depression was the only
significant concurrent event) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00127-026-03227-9)). This is a
textbook demonstration that the *choice of comparator* can flip a conclusion — directly actionable
for the site's methodology lane, and timely given DORAs' growing use as "safer" hypnotics.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the naive signal: four-method engine
for daridorexant vs. the whole database against *Suicidal ideation* / *Suicide attempt* /
*Depression* PTs, with observed counts and trajectory. (2) The fix: restrict to insomnia-indication
reports and use an **active comparator** set (zolpidem, eszopiclone, the other DORAs lemborexant and
suvorexant, trazodone) and show the signal attenuate. (3) Sensitivity: strip co-reported depression
and show the further drop. The transferable lesson — comparator choice and co-reported-event
confounding can manufacture a psychiatric signal for a drug whose *indication* already carries that
psychiatric burden. A clean companion to the site's existing "confounding by indication" callouts,
with a within-class natural control built in.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Daridorexant is a recent launch (lower volume; small-N on
the rarer psychiatric PTs), and building the insomnia-indication restriction plus a defensible
hypnotic comparator set is real work — but it is the article's methods content, and the DORA class is
small and well-defined. Channelling bias (which patients get the newest hypnotic) must be stated
plainly rather than hidden.

**Citations.**

  patients: Comparator-restricted disproportionality analysis and assessment of potential
  heterogeneity in reporting patterns," *Soc Psychiatry Psychiatr Epidemiol* 2026 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00127-026-03227-9)

## 4. A boxed warning for something FAERS can barely see: Injectafer and the invisible lab harm

**Working title:** "The Harm With No Report: Why FAERS Under-Counts the Injectafer Phosphate Warning
— and What Regulators Used Instead"

**Safety question.** On September 1, 2026 FDA added a **boxed warning** to ferric carboxymaltose
(Injectafer) for symptomatic hypophosphatemia — a lab-defined harm (low serum phosphate) that can
progress to osteomalacia and fractures. But asymptomatic lab abnormalities are exactly what
spontaneous-reporting systems systematically *miss*: no symptom, no report. So the honest two-sided
question is (a) what does FAERS disproportionality actually show for ferric carboxymaltose against a
phosphate/bone PT cluster, and (b) why did a boxed warning rest on a **monitoring-gap** finding
(phosphate simply wasn't being measured) rather than on a towering FAERS signal? A worked example of
FAERS's blind spot for lab-defined, often-asymptomatic harms — a cousin of the queued sevoflurane
"invisible subpopulation" piece, but here the invisibility is about *event type*, not population.

**Why it matters now.** FDA's **September 1, 2026** Drug Safety Communication adds the boxed warning
and recommends serum-phosphate monitoring for at-risk patients and for anyone receiving a repeat
course within three months. FDA's review identified **134 reports and published cases of serious
symptomatic hypophosphatemia (July 1, 2022–June 30, 2025)** and — the key methodological fact —
found that **serum phosphate was tested in fewer than 20% of ferric carboxymaltose administration
episodes**. A warning had already been added in February 2020; reports continued, prompting
escalation to a boxed warning. This is a live, this-cycle regulatory action with a spontaneous-
reporting spine the site is built to examine, and a rare chance to show *why* a regulator escalated
on under-monitoring rather than on raw report counts.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method engine for ferric carboxymaltose
against a phosphate/bone PT cluster — *Hypophosphataemia*, *Blood phosphorus decreased*,
*Osteomalacia*, *Fracture*, *Bone pain*, *Hypophosphataemic osteomalacia* — with observed counts
beside every EB05 and the quarterly caterpillar through the 2020 (first warning) and 2026 (boxed
warning) dates as natural-experiment markers. Add the within-class **active comparator**: ferric
carboxymaltose vs. other IV irons (iron sucrose, ferumoxytol, iron dextran, ferric derisomaltose),
since the mechanism (FGF23-mediated phosphate wasting) is specific to certain formulations — a clean
"formulation matters" contrast. The methods payload: a boxed-warning harm can be *under-represented*
in FAERS because it is a lab value, not a felt symptom; a modest or lagging FAERS signal is therefore
not reassurance, and labeling escalations can be driven by monitoring data the reporting system never
captures.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Ferric carboxymaltose and the comparator IV irons are
well-represented in FAERS with standard PTs; the formulation contrast is straightforward. The
interesting (and honest) finding may be that the FAERS signal is weaker than the boxed warning would
suggest — which *is* the article. Watch for reporting bumps tied to the 2020 and 2026 label actions.

**Citations.**
- FDA Drug Safety Communication, "FDA Adds Boxed Warning to Labeling for Ferric Carboxymaltose
  Injection (Injectafer) to Describe Risk of Low Phosphate Levels" (September 1, 2026) —
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-adds-boxed-warning-labeling-ferric-carboxymaltose-injection-injectafer-describe-risk-low
- Healio, "FDA adds boxed warning to Injectafer for hypophosphatemia risk" (September 3, 2026;

## 5. Same drug class, different formulation — and the comparator decides what you see (HIV PrEP)

**Working title:** "Oral, TAF, or a Shot: Reading Formulation-Specific Safety Signals Across HIV PrEP
— and Why the Comparator Changes the Story"

**Safety question.** HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis now spans oral FTC/TDF, oral FTC/TAF, and
long-acting injectable cabotegravir — with twice-yearly lenacapavir newly added to the landscape.
A FAERS study finds **distinct, formulation-specific** reporting patterns across these agents, and —
the methodological crux — that whether a signal looks real depends on the comparator (all non-PrEP
reports vs. other HIV drugs). Can the site show both halves: that formulation genuinely shapes the
safety fingerprint (renal/metabolic for TDF; injection-site/dermatologic for the shot), *and* that a
naive all-drugs comparator over-reads signals that attenuate against a clinically matched comparator?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, "Formulation-Specific Safety Issues of HIV Pre-exposure
Prophylaxis Drugs: A Disproportionality Analysis of the FAERS Database," *Clinical Drug
Investigation*, published **October 10, 2026** (FAERS 2012–2024), analyzed 3,201 PrEP-related reports
(FTC/TDF 2,688; FTC/TAF 463; cabotegravir 50) and found FTC/TDF with a broad renal/metabolic/muscular
pattern that **attenuated against an HIV-treated comparator**, FTC/TAF with a narrower
hepatobiliary/metabolic-monitoring pattern, and cabotegravir with reproducible injection-site and
dermatologic signals; it concludes that **"comparator choice substantially influences the detection
and interpretation of signals"** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40261-026-01602-7)). The timing is
apt: FDA approved twice-yearly lenacapavir (Yeztugo) for PrEP on **June 18, 2025**, so the PrEP
formulation landscape — and the question of how to compare safety across very different delivery
modes — is squarely current.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method engine for each PrEP agent against two
comparator backgrounds — all non-PrEP reports, then HIV-treatment reports — for formulation-relevant
PT clusters: renal/metabolic (*Renal impairment*, *Blood creatinine increased*, *Fanconi syndrome*,
*Hypophosphataemia*) for the TDF arm; injection-site/dermatologic (*Injection site reaction*,
*Injection site pain*, *Rash*) for cabotegravir; hepatobiliary for TAF. Show observed counts beside
every EB05 and the two-comparator contrast side by side. The transferable lesson: when agents in one
indication differ by *formulation* (oral vs. TAF prodrug vs. injectable), the safety fingerprint is
real and distinct — but the all-drugs comparator inflates indication/reporting-context signals that a
clinically matched comparator strips out. Standard small-N caveats loudest for cabotegravir (n = 50)
and for lenacapavir if included (very new; likely too sparse — note this honestly).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** FTC/TDF is well-covered; FTC/TAF moderate; cabotegravir and
(especially) lenacapavir are small-N, which bounds what can be said about the injectables — itself an
honest finding. Separating PrEP-indication reports from HIV-*treatment* reports for the same molecules
(FTC/TDF and FTC/TAF are also treatment backbones) is the main wrangling and must be done carefully.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: "Formulation-Specific Safety Issues of HIV Pre-exposure Prophylaxis Drugs: A
  Disproportionality Analysis of the FAERS Database," *Clin Drug Investig* 2026 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40261-026-01602-7)
- FDA, "FDA Approves Lenacapavir for HIV Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)" / Gilead Yeztugo approval
  (June 18, 2025, formulation-landscape context) —
  https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-new-medication-pre-exposure-prophylaxis-reduce-risk-sexually-acquired-hiv

**Recommendation.** **#1 (ranitidine)** is the standout: a this-cycle *Drug Safety* methods paper, a

be pure litigation/recall artifact — reproducible with an active comparator and a time window the site
already knows how to build. **#2 (GBS masking)** is the most *novel* for the site — "denominator
dilution" is a gotcha not yet in the catalogue, it is VAERS-native, and it carries a flagship FDA
label anchor (RSV GBS warning). **#3 (daridorexant)** is the tightest pure "confounding by indication /
comparator choice" lesson and a new drug class. **#4 (Injectafer)** is the only regulator-anchored,
this-month idea and teaches the under-reporting-of-lab-harms blind spot. **#5 (PrEP)** rounds out the
comparator through-line with a formulation twist and strong topicality, but carries the most small-N
risk on the injectable arms. A strong two-piece pairing: **#1 + #2** (a signal that shouldn't be
believed, and a signal that was wrongly *disbelieved*) would make an excellent "reading FDA data" diptych.
