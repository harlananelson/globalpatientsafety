# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-09-04

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months (July–September 2026), matched to what this site already does: independent
disproportionality reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR,
BCPNN/IC), EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot **plus**
quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
and confounding caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the current queue (Zostavax-vs-Shingrix zoster,
GLP-1→NAION survivability, AEMS reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC), the 2026-07-24 batch
(sevoflurane invisible-subpopulation, atorvastatin huge-PRR, teprotumumab hearing loss,
vamorolone-vs-deflazacort, IXCHIQ chikungunya), and the 2026-07-03 batch (orlistat kidney,
compounded GLP-1 dosing errors, teplizumab pediatric baseline, ICI endocrine confounding,
myocarditis-across-eras). Each idea below carries a *distinct* methodology lesson not yet in the
site's running gotcha catalogue: (#1) the mechanism is loud but the disabling endpoint is
invisible; (#2) "no signal" is not "safe," and the endpoint/SMQ scope you pick decides the
answer; (#3) a dominant product can *mask* a real signal by diluting the disproportionality
denominator; (#4) a real signal whose *cause* is the indication/setting, not the drug; (#5)
cross-national replication and an internal negative control as robustness checks.

## 1. FDA's brand-new boxed warning: the loud mechanism and the crippling endpoint FAERS barely sees

**Working title:** "The Loud Mechanism and the Quiet Cripple: Ferric Carboxymaltose,
Hypophosphatemia, and the Endpoint Spontaneous Reporting Undercounts"

**Safety question.** On **August 13, 2026** FDA added a **Boxed Warning** to ferric
carboxymaltose (Injectafer) for severe symptomatic hypophosphatemia and its downstream
complications — osteomalacia and insufficiency fractures. In FAERS the *mechanism*
(hypophosphatemia) is one of the loudest signals in the whole database; the *clinically
disabling endpoint* (osteomalacia, atraumatic fractures) is rare, delayed by months, and — per
FDA's own finding — systematically under-monitored and therefore under-reported. Can the site's
four-method engine reproduce the enormous hypophosphatemia signal, contrast it with the
near-silent osteomalacia/fracture signal, and use the gap to teach that **a spontaneous-reporting
system plus a monitoring gap will undercount exactly the outcome that matters most**?

**Why it matters now.** Per FDA and the petitioning group (Cure HHT), on **August 13, 2026** the
FDA granted a citizen petition and approved a Boxed Warning for ferric carboxymaltose after
reviewing **134 reports and published cases of serious, symptomatic hypophosphatemia between
July 1, 2022 and June 30, 2025**, several severe/prolonged/difficult-to-treat, and found that
phosphate testing was happening **far less often than the label already recommended**. This is a
this-month, top-tier regulatory action (a new Boxed Warning) with a spontaneous-reporting spine
the site is built to examine. Method precedent already exists: according to PubMed, Galigutta et
al. (FAERS + VigiBase, May 2025) found a hypophosphatemia signal for FCM of **PRR ≈ 520.7,
IC ≈ 8.0** — among the largest disproportionality values one ever sees — while finding **no
significant death signal**, a perfect illustration of the loud-mechanism / invisible-outcome
split.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for ferric
carboxymaltose against (a) *Hypophosphataemia* / *Blood phosphorus decreased* and (b) the
downstream cluster *Osteomalacia*, *Bone pain*, *Pathological fracture*, *Stress fracture*,
*Fatigue*, *Muscular weakness*. Add a **within-class active comparator** — iron sucrose
(Venofer), ferric derisomaltose (Monoferric), iron dextran, ferumoxytol — because FCM's HPP risk
is known to exceed other IV irons, so the comparator is the honest denominator, not the whole
database. Show observed counts beside every EB05 and the quarterly caterpillar. The methodology
payload: the hypophosphatemia signal will be gigantic and the osteomalacia/fracture signal small
or absent — **and the FDA has just told us why (under-monitoring), so "small signal" here means
"under-ascertained," not "rare."** Extends the catalogue's "the rare severe outcome is invisible;
the mechanism is loud" theme with a fresh, regulator-anchored example, and adds a new sub-lesson:
a documented monitoring gap is a reason to *distrust a negative*, not to relax.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** FCM is high-volume with deep FAERS coverage and
standard PTs; the hypophosphatemia analysis is trivial and the within-class IV-iron comparator is
clean. The only judgment call is defining the osteomalacia/fracture PT cluster conservatively.

## 2. "No signal" is not "safe" — and the endpoint you pick decides the answer

**Working title:** "Absence of Evidence, Evidence of Absence: What Esomeprazole's Missing
Fracture Flag Actually Tells You"

**Safety question.** A brand-new network-meta-analysis-plus-FAERS study reports that individual
proton-pump inhibitors differ in fracture risk — and, strikingly, that **esomeprazole shows
*no* overall fracture signal in FAERS yet a *strong* narrow-scope osteoporosis/osteopenia SMQ
signal**. That single discordance is a whole methodology lesson: whether a drug "has a signal"
depends on which endpoint (fracture vs osteoporosis) and which MedDRA query scope (narrow vs
broad SMQ) you choose. Can the site reproduce the per-agent heterogeneity and use the
esomeprazole split to teach that **"no disproportionality signal" must never be read as evidence
of safety**, and that endpoint/scope choices silently drive conclusions?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Piao H-L, Wu Z-H, Zhou L-Y, et al., "Differential
fracture risk among proton pump inhibitors in older adults: evidence network and pharmacovigilance
validation," *Front Pharmacol*, published **August 19, 2026**, combined an NMA (9 studies,
257,445 participants) with a FAERS disproportionality analysis (16,908 PPI fracture events) and
found omeprazole with a significant fracture signal (ROR 1.25) while **esomeprazole showed no
fracture signal (ROR 0.89) but a strong Osteoporosis/Osteopenia narrow-SMQ signal (ROR 2.44)**,
concluding that "absence of a fracture signal should not imply skeletal safety"
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1835602)). PPIs are among the most-used drugs on earth,
and FDA has warned on this endpoint before (bone-fracture labeling, **May 25, 2010**) — so a
concrete "how to read a *negative* correctly" piece has broad reach and directly extends the
site's gotcha catalogue.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run the four-method engine per agent
(omeprazole, esomeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole) against (a) a *Fracture* PT
cluster and (b) an *Osteoporosis/Osteopenia* set at both narrow and broad SMQ scope, and lay the
results side by side. The teaching payload is the **discordance table**: same drug, different
endpoint/scope, opposite conclusion — with observed counts beside every EB05 and a caterpillar
that shows whether the "negative" agent is genuinely flat or merely under-populated. Ties the
site's existing "the raw leaderboard lies" and "small-N EB05 needs the trajectory" lessons to
their mirror image: the *bottom* of the leaderboard lies too, and a null is a modeling choice as
much as a finding.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low.** PPIs are extremely high-volume; coverage and PTs are
trivial. The only real work is defining the fracture PT cluster and the two SMQ scopes defensibly
— which is itself the article's methods content.

  "Differential fracture risk among proton pump inhibitors in older adults: evidence network and
  pharmacovigilance validation," *Front Pharmacol* 2026;17:1835602 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1835602)

## 3. When 81% of the database is one vaccine: the signal COVID hid (VAERS)

**Working title:** "Denominator Dilution: Guillain-Barré, the RSV Shots, and the Real Signal a
Dominant Vaccine Can Bury"

**Safety question.** A just-published 21-year VAERS analysis flags Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS)
for influenza formulations (validating decades of surveillance) and surfaces **new** signals for
**both RSV vaccines and the zoster vaccine** — yet finds **no concordant GBS signal for COVID-19
vaccines**, precisely because COVID-19 reports are **81% of the entire database** and dilute the
disproportionality denominator. This is the mirror image of the queued/July "invisible signal"
theme: not a subgroup too small to see, but a real signal *masked* by a single dominant product.
Can the site reproduce the RSV/zoster GBS signals, show the delayed-onset window as a
biological-plausibility filter, and demonstrate the masking directly by recomputing the COVID GBS
signal with and without the COVID reports in the background?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Su Y, Huang Y, Pan L, Fu H, Wang X, "Temporal and
disproportionality analysis of vaccine-associated Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS study,"
*Front Neurol*, published **August 4, 2026** (VAERS 2004–2024; 2,216 GBS cases), reported
signals for 26 vaccines meeting all four algorithms, including **Arexvy (ROR 3.01) and Abrysvo
(ROR 6.82)** and zoster (ROR 3.01), a **median onset of 13 days (IQR 5–29)** consistent with the
autoimmune window, and explicitly attributed the *absent* COVID-19 GBS signal to denominator
dilution from 7.89 million COVID reports ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)). The
regulatory anchor is concrete: FDA required a **GBS Warning in the prescribing information for
Abrysvo and Arexvy on January 7, 2025** (postmarketing estimates of ~9 and ~7 excess cases per
million doses in adults ≥65). RSV vaccines are newly and widely deployed, and ACIP continues to
weigh their risk-benefit, so this is a live, VAERS-native story.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Four-method engine for the RSV vaccines, zoster,
and an influenza reference against *Guillain-Barré syndrome* / *Miller Fisher syndrome* /
*Demyelinating polyneuropathy*, with the site's caterpillar over available quarters and a
time-to-onset histogram to show the 8–30-day plausibility window (contrast with the median-0-day
"same-day" reactogenic events that are almost certainly not GBS). The methodology centerpiece is a
**masking demonstration**: recompute the COVID-vaccine GBS disproportionality against the full
background vs against a background with COVID reports removed, and show the signal reappear — a
concrete lesson that a dominant product inflates the "expected" cell and hides real signals.
Frames plainly what VAERS can (relative reporting pattern, latency shape, age skew) and cannot
(incidence, causation) settle, and keeps the benefit-risk framing the source stresses
(1–9 excess cases/million is far outweighed by disease prevention). Distinct from the July
sevoflurane piece (there: a signal too *rare/subgroup-restricted* to see; here: a real signal
*masked* by report-volume competition), and from the queued Zostavax→zoster piece (a different
endpoint — GBS, not vaccine-strain zoster).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** RSV vaccines are recent (2023 launch) so GBS counts are
modest and several EB05s may sit near threshold — which is itself the point. The masking
demonstration requires care in reconstructing the background set with/without COVID reports, and
in age-restricting to the ≥65 population where the label estimates apply.

- FDA, "FDA Requires Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) Warning in Prescribing Information for RSV

## 4. Is it the drug or the diagnosis? A real signal whose cause is the indication

**Working title:** "On-Pathway or Confounded? Antipsychotics, Aspiration Pneumonia, and the
Signal You Can't Attribute"

**Safety question.** A new FAERS study finds **aspiration pneumonia the dominant
infection-and-infestation signal across the major second-generation antipsychotics** (clozapine,
olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone, aripiprazole). The signal is almost certainly *real* — but
its *cause* is the whole question: aspiration pneumonia is mechanistically **on-pathway**
(sedation, dysphagia, clozapine-associated hypersalivation) *and* heavily **confounded by
indication and setting** (elderly, institutionalized, dementia-related off-label use — the exact
population under the antipsychotic Boxed Warning for increased mortality, where deaths are often
infectious/pneumonia). Can the site reproduce the signal and use it to teach how to reason about a
disproportionality result that is robust yet **un-attributable** from spontaneous data alone —
separating an on-pathway drug effect from confounding by indication and care setting?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Zhou X, Zhang G, Chen J, Li D, "Risk assessment of
infections and infestations associated with second-generation antipsychotics: A comprehensive
analysis of the FAERS database," *Schizophr Res*, published **September 1, 2026** (FAERS
2016–2025), identified aspiration pneumonia as the predominant positive infection signal for
clozapine, olanzapine, quetiapine, risperidone, and aripiprazole, with Weibull time-to-onset
differing sharply by drug (olanzapine/quetiapine median 30 days; clozapine 2,493 days)
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2026.08.034)). The interpretive stakes are set by the
long-standing **FDA Boxed Warning that antipsychotics increase mortality in elderly patients with
dementia-related psychosis** (atypicals 2005; extended to all antipsychotics 2008), where the
excess deaths are largely cardiovascular or **infectious (pneumonia)** — so the very endpoint this
paper flags sits inside a known, population-driven mortality signal. That tension is the article.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method engine for the SGAs against
*Pneumonia aspiration* and a supporting cluster (*Dysphagia*, *Hypersalivation/Salivary
hypersecretion*, *Sedation*, *Pneumonia*), with observed counts and the quarterly caterpillar, and
the site's within-class contrast (clozapine's late-onset, hypersalivation-linked pattern vs the
early-onset sedation-linked pattern of others). The methodology payload is a structured
**attribution ladder**: (1) is the signal on the drug's mechanistic pathway? (2) is it
concentrated in an indication/age/setting that independently causes the outcome? (3) what would it
take — active comparator, age restriction, external denominators — to move from "associated" to
"caused"? A clean, transferable treatment of confounding-by-indication layered on an on-pathway
mechanism, with the standard "FAERS cannot separate drug from disease/setting" caveat foregrounded.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** SGAs are high-volume with deep coverage; the
aspiration-pneumonia PT cluster is straightforward. The honest work is the caveat structure and,
optionally, an age-restricted / within-class comparison to probe (not resolve) the confounding —
which is exactly the article's point.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Zhou X, Zhang G, Chen J, Li D, "Risk assessment of infections and
  infestations associated with second-generation antipsychotics: A comprehensive analysis of the
  FAERS database," *Schizophr Res* 2026;297:304–313 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2026.08.034)
- FDA, Boxed Warning on increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis
  treated with antipsychotics (atypicals 2005; extended to all antipsychotics June 2008; deaths
  largely cardiovascular or infectious/pneumonia) — Psychiatric News, "FDA Extends Black-Box
  Warning to All Antipsychotics" — https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/pn.43.14.0001

## 5. Does it replicate? GLP-1 reflux across three national databases, with an internal negative control

**Working title:** "Robustness by Replication: A GLP-1 Reflux Signal Tested in Three Countries —
and Why Exenatide Is the Control That Matters"

**Safety question.** A new study reports a consistent gastroesophageal-reflux (GERD) signal for
GLP-1 receptor agonists across **three independent national reporting systems** (FAERS, Japan's
JADER, Canada Vigilance), benchmarked against **DPP-4 inhibitors as an active comparator**, with
the signal strongest for semaglutide and — tellingly — **inverted for exenatide** (a short-acting
agent), which functions as an internal negative control. The methodology question is not "does
GLP-1 cause reflux" but **how much confidence a signal earns when it replicates across countries
and databases, survives an active-comparator design, and behaves sensibly on a drug where you'd
predict a weaker effect**. Can the site demonstrate cross-database replication and internal-control
reasoning — robustness tools it has not yet showcased — on a topical drug class?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Choi J-G, Gong E-J, Bang C-S, Lee J-J, "Multi-Database
Pharmacovigilance Analysis of Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease Associated with GLP-1 Receptor
Agonists: A Cross-National Signal Validation Study," *Gut Liver*, published **September 2, 2026**,
analyzed 260,417 GLP-1 reports and found significant GERD signals in **FAERS (ROR 2.83), JADER
(ROR 4.76), and Canada Vigilance (ROR 2.91)** vs DPP-4 inhibitors, strongest for semaglutide,
with **exenatide showing an inverse association in FAERS (ROR 0.60)** and signal strength rising
with age ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.5009/gnl260189)). The clinical relevance is live — GLP-1
delayed gastric emptying underlies the 2023 ASA perioperative-aspiration guidance and a wave of
upper-GI attention — but the *article's* value is the robustness template: replication + active
comparator + internal negative control.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS (primary).** Reproduce the FAERS arm with the
four-method engine: GLP-1 agents (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, tirzepatide, exenatide)
vs a GERD PT cluster (*Gastrooesophageal reflux disease*, *Reflux oesophagitis*, *Dyspepsia*),
**using DPP-4 inhibitors as the reference rather than the whole database** (the site's
"raw-leaderboard/confounding-by-indication" fix, applied as a positive design choice), with
observed counts, the quarterly caterpillar, and an explicit **age-stratified** read. The
methodology payload is threefold: (1) an **active-comparator** contingency table beats a
whole-database background for a drug class with a strong indication; (2) **exenatide as an internal
negative control** — a short-acting agent that should, and does, behave differently — is worth more
than a p-value; (3) the site can only *reproduce the FAERS arm*, so the piece frames cross-database
replication as corroborating external evidence, honestly noting we do not hold JADER/Canada data.
Caveats: channelling (semaglutide's obesity-driven, higher-BMI population may have more baseline
reflux) and stimulated reporting around GLP-1 media attention.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** GLP-1 agents and DPP-4 inhibitors are all
high-volume in FAERS; the active-comparator tables and age strata are straightforward. **Caveat:
the queue is already GLP-1-heavy** (NAION survivability, alopecia, compounded dosing) — this idea
earns its place only as a *methods* piece (replication + negative control), on a **new endpoint**
(reflux) with a **new technique**, and should be ranked accordingly if a non-GLP-1 idea is
preferred for class balance.

**Citations.**

  Analysis of Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease Associated with GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: A
  Cross-National Signal Validation Study," *Gut Liver* 2026 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5009/gnl260189)
- American Society of Anesthesiologists, "Consensus-Based Guidance on Preoperative Management of

**Recommendation.** **#1 (ferric carboxymaltose)** is the standout: the freshest possible hook — a
**this-month FDA Boxed Warning (Aug 13, 2026)** — squarely in the methodology lane, with an
existing FAERS method precedent (hypophosphatemia PRR ≈ 520) and a clean, teachable split between a
screaming mechanism and an under-ascertained disabling outcome that FDA itself says is
under-monitored. **#2 (PPI "no signal ≠ safe")** is the best pure "reading FDA data" lesson and
pairs naturally with #1 as a two-sided story about trusting negatives (endpoint/scope choices and
monitoring gaps both manufacture false reassurance). **#3 (GBS/RSV)** is the only VAERS-native idea,
anchored to a real FDA labeling action, and teaches masking/denominator-dilution — a genuinely new
gotcha for the catalogue. **#4 (antipsychotics)** is the most conceptually careful piece
(attribution reasoning) but resolves to "we cannot attribute from FAERS alone," so it reads best as
an explicit methods essay. **#5 (GLP-1 reflux)** carries the most novel *technique* (cross-database
replication + internal negative control) but is deprioritized for class balance given a
GLP-1-heavy queue — take it only if a methods-forward GLP-1 piece is wanted.
