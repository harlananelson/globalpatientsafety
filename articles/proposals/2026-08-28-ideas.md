## 1. The FDA flagged two brand-new signals in its first AEMS-era report — can our four methods see them?

**Working title:** "When the FDA Names a Signal: Testing the AEMS Watch-List Against Our Own
Disproportionality Filter"

**Safety question:** In its October–December 2025 quarterly "Potential Signals of Serious Risks"
report — the first such list published under the renamed FDA Adverse Event Monitoring System
(AEMS) — the FDA named two new signals it is "evaluating the need for regulatory action" on:
**hypogammaglobulinemia** across the bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) class (Columvi/glofitamab,
Epkinly/epcoritamab, Imdelltra/tarlatamab, Kimmtrak/tebentafusp, Talvey/talquetamab), and
**aplastic anemia** with **Calquence (acalabrutinib)**. Do these FDA-identified signals reproduce
in a standard four-method disproportionality pass, and — for the BiTE class — is the signal a
uniform class effect or driven by one or two products?

**Why it matters now (dated source):** The FDA posted this signal list on **2026-03-31** as the
Oct–Dec 2025 AEMS quarterly report — the first watch-list under the AEMS rebrand our own AEMS
primer already covers. It's an unusually clean test case: the regulator has publicly committed to
a specific drug→event pair *before* any label change, so our analysis is a same-data replication
of an official signal rather than a fishing expedition. (FDA AEMS "New Safety Information or
Potential Signals of Serious Risks," Oct–Dec 2025; press release 2026-03-31.)

**Which of our data/methods:** FAERS. Two mini-analyses in one piece:
- **BiTE → hypogammaglobulinemia (+ downstream infection PTs):** EBGM/EB05 caterpillar across the
  five named products vs. background, latest-quarter snapshot with observed counts; then the
  *class-effect* read used in the AAV liver article — does the signal track the shared
  on-target mechanism (B-cell/plasma-cell depletion) across all five, or collapse to the BCMA/CD20
  agents? Report both hypogammaglobulinemia (PT) and the infection cluster it predisposes to.
- **Acalabrutinib → aplastic anemia:** small-N single-drug EB05 with the trajectory (aplastic
  anemia is rare; show whether the count is large enough to mean anything), ideally against the
  BTK-inhibitor class (ibrutinib, zanubrutinib) as active comparators to separate a drug-specific
  signal from a class cytopenia background.

**Expected data difficulty:** Low–moderate. All six drugs are well-represented recent oncology/heme
products; the PTs ("Hypogammaglobulinaemia," "Aplastic anaemia") are standard MedDRA. Main risk is
thin counts for the newest BiTEs (tarlatamab, tebentafusp) — pool quarters and show observed N.

**Citations:**

  Identified by the FDA Adverse Event Monitoring System (AEMS)," fda.gov (list posted 2026-03-31).
  https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-adverse-event-monitoring-system-aems/new-safety-information-or-potential-signals-serious-risks-identified-fda-adverse-event-monitoring
- EIN Presswire summary of the Oct–Dec 2025 AEMS report (names the flagged products/events),
  2026. https://www.einpresswire.com/article/903012954/
- "The silent signals: emerging safety concerns in bispecific antibody therapy for multiple
  myeloma," PMC12380792 (2025), on hypogammaglobulinemia/infection as the underrecognized BiTE
  toxicity. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12380792/

## 2. A first-in-class drug's first full year in FAERS — reading Cobenfy against an active comparator

**Working title:** "One Year of Cobenfy: What the First Muscarinic Antipsychotic's Early FAERS
Reports Do and Don't Tell Us"

**Safety question:** Xanomeline–trospium (Cobenfy), the first-in-class M1/M4 muscarinic agonist for
schizophrenia, has now accumulated enough post-market reports for a disproportionality read. A
June 2026 FAERS study (1,410 reports) found the reporting profile dominated by gastrointestinal
(nausea, projectile vomiting), urinary retention, and acute-psychiatric-activation signals, with an
"early failure" onset (86.8% of events within 30 days, median 6.4 days) and — vs. olanzapine —
*lower* metabolic/extrapyramidal but *higher* autonomic/GI reporting. Does that active-comparator
contrast hold up under our filter, and how much of it is just the drug's known cholinergic/
anticholinergic pharmacology surfacing as reports?

**Why it matters now (dated source):** Cobenfy was approved **September 2024** (first-in-class), so
2026 is the first window with a usable post-market signal; the anchoring FAERS analysis published
**2026-06-09** (Psychiatry Research). Separately, the 2026 ARISE adjunctive-therapy trial *missed*
its primary endpoint, keeping monotherapy the main real-world exposure — clean for a single-drug
read. A great worked example of the methodology gotcha "a novel drug's early FAERS profile is
shaped by labeling expectations and channeling, not just biology."

**Which of our data/methods:** FAERS. Active-comparator EBGM/ROR of xanomeline–trospium vs.
olanzapine (and optionally vs. the whole atypical-antipsychotic background) for the GI, urinary
(urinary retention — a predictable antimuscarinic-then-trospium story), and neuropsychiatric PT
clusters; Weibull/time-to-onset to reproduce the "early failure" pattern; sex-stratified read of
the cholinergic-vs-anticholinergic divergence. Emphasize observed counts and the young-drug
caveat (reporting still ramping; disproportionality unstable at low cumulative N).

**Expected data difficulty:** Moderate. Depends on whether our parquet's quarters extend into
2025Q3–2025Q4 with enough Cobenfy reports; the cited study used 2024Q4–2025Q4. If our snapshot
lags, this is a "watch and revisit" candidate. Drug string may appear as "xanomeline,"
"xanomeline and trospium chloride," or "Cobenfy" — check all.

**Citations:**
- Zeng Y, et al. "Post-marketing safety surveillance of the novel muscarinic antipsychotic
  Xanomeline-Trospium for schizophrenia: A comparative disproportionality analysis against
  olanzapine based on FAERS data." *Psychiatry Res* 2026;364:117270. According to PubMed,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117270) (PMID 42288069).
- BMS press release, "FDA Approves Cobenfy (xanomeline and trospium chloride)… for Schizophrenia
  in Adults," 2024-09. https://news.bms.com/news/details/2024/
- Review of the 2026 ARISE adjunctive-trial result (add-on endpoint miss), 2026.
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12357971/

## 3. A label change built to erase one adverse event — does FAERS show it working?

**Working title:** "The Dose Schedule as a Natural Experiment: ARIA-E Before and After Donanemab's
New Titration"

**Safety question:** ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities, especially ARIA-E edema) is the
central safety concern for the anti-amyloid Alzheimer's antibodies lecanemab (Leqembi) and
donanemab (Kisunla). In 2026 the FDA approved a **modified donanemab titration** specifically to
lower ARIA-E, with the trial (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6) reporting ARIA-E dropping from ~24% to ~14–16%.
Two questions our data can address as a methods lesson: (a) does FAERS ARIA-E reporting for
donanemab shift after the new-titration rollout — i.e., can spontaneous reports even detect a known
dosing intervention? — and (b) how do lecanemab and donanemab compare head-to-head on ARIA
reporting, given a 2026 FAERS study (2,961 lecanemab / 1,542 donanemab reports) found ARIA the
dominant signal for both?

**Why it matters now (dated source):** The FDA's donanemab modified-titration label update landed
in **2026** (NeurologyLive/manufacturer coverage of the TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 6 titration data), and the
anchoring lecanemab-vs-donanemab FAERS/VigiAccess study published **2026-08-18**. The
before/after-a-known-date structure is exactly the "use a label/publication date as a natural
experiment" gotcha the site already used for GLP-1/NAION notoriety timing — here applied to a
*protective* intervention rather than a notoriety spike.

**Which of our data/methods:** FAERS (and VAERS is not relevant; these are drugs). Trajectory plot
of ARIA-E (and "Amyloid related imaging abnormality" PTs) observed counts and EB05 by quarter for
donanemab, annotated with the titration-label date; head-to-head active-comparator ROR/EBGM
lecanemab vs. donanemab for the ARIA cluster. Heavy emphasis on the limitation that spontaneous
reporting cannot measure incidence or confirm a dosing effect — the honest answer may be "FAERS is
too confounded by reporting dynamics to see the titration change," which is itself the lesson.

**Expected data difficulty:** Moderate. Both drugs are recent but heavily reported; ARIA PTs are
specific. The before/after read needs post-2026 quarters in our parquet — confirm coverage before
committing. Watch: APOE ε4 status (the real effect modifier) is essentially absent from FAERS, so
any "risk" framing must be avoided.

**Citations:**
- Xue WY, et al. "Post-marketing safety signals and report-level ARIA-E stratification for
  lecanemab and donanemab: an integrated FAERS and WHO-VigiAccess pharmacovigilance study."
  *Ther Adv Drug Saf* 2026;17. According to PubMed,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/20420986261473013) (PMID 42621237).
- NeurologyLive, "FDA Approves Updated Label for Alzheimer Therapy Donanemab to Lower ARIA-E Risk,"
  2026. https://www.neurologylive.com/view/fda-approves-updated-label-alzheimer-therapy-donanemab-lower-aria-e-risk
- The Medical Letter, "In Brief: Updates for Lecanemab (Leqembi) for Alzheimer's Disease," 2026.
  https://secure.medicalletter.org/TML-article-1741d

## 4. A rare immune-related toxicity that only shows up when you look with the right method

**Working title:** "Aortitis on a Checkpoint Inhibitor: How EBGM Surfaces a 1-in-Thousands
Immune-Related Signal Without Crying Wolf"

**Safety question:** Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) cause a long tail of rare immune-related
adverse events. A newly published FAERS study identified **83 reports** of ICI-associated aortitis
and classical large-vessel vasculitis (giant cell arteritis, Takayasu's), with an overall ROR of
4.45 (95% CI 3.57–5.55), strongest for anti-PD-1 agents (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) and a median
time-to-onset of ~105 days. Can our four-method consensus filter distinguish this genuinely rare
but real irAE from the statistical noise that dominates small-N disproportionality — and what does
the onset-time add that a bare EB05 cannot?

**Why it matters now (dated source):** The anchoring analysis published **2026-08-18** (*Seminars
in Arthritis and Rheumatism*). It's a timely vehicle for one of the site's core methodology points
— that the rare severe endpoint is faint in disproportionality while the pattern (which agents,
what onset window) is what makes it credible — applied to a fresh, specific irAE rather than the
well-worn ICI colitis/pneumonitis.

**Which of our data/methods:** FAERS. EBGM/EB05 + IC across the ICI class (anti-PD-1 vs anti-PD-L1
vs anti-CTLA-4 vs combination) for the "Aortitis," "Giant cell arteritis," "Takayasu's arteritis"
PTs; require the ≥2-of-4-methods rule and show observed counts to make the "rare but survives the
filter" point; layer time-to-onset to reproduce the ~105-day window. Good companion to a caution
that Takayasu's is too sparse to trust while aortitis/GCA hold.

**Expected data difficulty:** Low–moderate. ICIs are among the most-reported drugs in FAERS; the
vasculitis PTs are standard. The counts are small per-agent, so this is explicitly a small-N EB05
piece — pool quarters and be honest about instability.

**Citations:**
- Guo Q, Guo H. "Immune checkpoint inhibitor-associated aortitis and classical large-vessel
  vasculitis: Clinical characterization and pharmacovigilance analysis using FAERS." *Semin
  Arthritis Rheum* 2026;80:153057. According to PubMed,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2026.153057) (PMID 42623759).
- (Background for the "rare irAE" framing) FDA labeling for the PD-1/PD-L1 class carries
  immune-mediated adverse-reaction warnings; large-vessel vasculitis is not individually
  enumerated — supports the "signal precedes the label" angle. Confirm against current labels at
  draft time.

## 5. Same reports, different comparator, different answer — a methods piece on active-comparator disproportionality

**Working title:** "It Depends What You Compare It To: How the Choice of Comparator Decides Which
Vaccine and Drug Signals Survive"

**Safety question:** The most consequential and least-discussed knob in disproportionality is the
*reference group*. A signal computed against "all other reports" (background) can vanish or invert
when computed against an **active comparator** (a similar product), because the background is
contaminated by masking and channeling. Two fresh 2026 papers make this concrete: an
active-comparator FAERS design for COVID-19-vaccine autoimmune events (comparing COVID-vaccine
reports to *traditional-vaccine* reports, with an explicit masking audit and a cluster-collapse
that knocked an acquired-hemophilia ROR from ~13 down to 2.70, CI crossing 1), and a GLP-1
letter arguing that signal thresholds and comparator choice, not the data, drive the headline
disproportionality numbers. This piece would re-derive one of the site's *own* prior VAERS
COVID-vaccine signals under both a background and an active comparator to show the swing.

**Why it matters now (dated source):** The active-comparator COVID-vaccine autoimmune paper
published **2026-08-21** (*Int J Environ Res Public Health*); the GLP-1 signal-threshold/causal-
inference letter published **2026-07-21** (*Clinical Nutrition*). Both land squarely in the site's
"Reading FDA Adverse-Event Data" series and directly extend the existing Cotton/shingles/COVID
VAERS work with a methodological upgrade rather than a repeat analysis.

**Which of our data/methods:** VAERS (for the vaccine worked example) + FAERS (GLP-1 sidebar). Take
one already-published site result (e.g., a COVID-vaccine cardiac or thrombotic PT from the shingles
or Cotton pieces) and recompute EB05/ROR three ways: vs. all-VAERS background, vs. an
active vaccine comparator (e.g., influenza), and after a masking/cluster audit. Show how the
verdict moves. This is a pure-methods article — the deliverable is the *lesson about comparators*,
not a new causal claim about any product.

**Expected data difficulty:** Low. Uses data the site already has and results it already computed;
the work is re-running the same pairs under alternative reference sets. Main effort is the masking
audit and a clean before/after presentation. Lowest data risk of the batch; highest editorial fit
for the methodology lane.

**Citations:**
- Messova AM, et al. "Autoimmune Reporting Signals in FAERS Reports Listing COVID-19 Vaccines as
  Suspect Products: An Active-Comparator Disproportionality Analysis." *Int J Environ Res Public
  Health* 2026;23(8):1088. According to PubMed,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph23081088) (PMID 42652404).
- Li L. "Signal thresholds and causal inference in pharmacovigilance studies of glucagon-like
  Peptide-1 receptor agonists." *Clin Nutr* 2026;64:106731. According to PubMed,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106731) (PMID 42520369).
