# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-07-10

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, and quarterly caterpillar plots.

Voice reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about
what disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting and
confounding caveats. These proposals aim to fit that register.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed —
every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the literature, not to our data.
Where a figure could not be verified against a primary source, that is stated inline.

**No overlap with last week (2026-07-03).** Those five were: orlistat/kidney, compounded
GLP-1 *dosing errors*, teplizumab pediatric baseline, confounding-aware ICI endocrine
methods, and COVID-vaccine myocarditis resolution over eras. The GLP-1 idea below is a
*different endpoint* (alopecia, not medication error); nothing else here reuses those drugs
or framings.

## 1. Fourteen seizure reports and a required warning: does the full FAERS record show a B6-depletion signal for carbidopa/levodopa?

**Working title:** "Depleted, Then Seizing: Testing the New Carbidopa/Levodopa Vitamin-B6
Seizure Warning Against the Full FAERS Record"

**Safety question.** FDA now requires carbidopa/levodopa labeling to warn that the drug can
cause vitamin-B6 (pyridoxine) deficiency and B6-deficiency–associated seizures. The action
rested on 14 FAERS cases. Does an independent four-method disproportionality pass over the
*entire* FAERS database corroborate a seizure excess for carbidopa/levodopa — and, more
sharply, is the signal *mechanistically specific* (co-reported vitamin-B6 deficiency,
peripheral neuropathy, hyperhomocysteinaemia) rather than generic neurological noise?

**Why it matters now.** On **March 20, 2026** FDA announced it is **requiring** a warning
about vitamin-B6 deficiency and associated seizures for all carbidopa/levodopa products,
citing **14 seizure cases** identified in FAERS (and noting likely under-reporting). The
mechanism is concrete and testable: levodopa metabolism consumes B6, and carbidopa binds the
active form of B6, compounding a functional deficiency. A warning built on 14 cases is
exactly the situation where a full-database disproportionality pass is informative — it
either corroborates the regulator's small series or shows the signal is invisible at
population scale. (This is a *different* drug and a *different* mechanism from last week's
orlistat/oxalate idea; the shared shape — small case series → label change → full-DB test —
is deliberate house methodology, not a repeat topic.)

**How the site's data/methods apply.** Carbidopa/levodopa is a drug → **FAERS**. Run
GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for carbidopa/levodopa (and the newer extended-release and
enteral-suspension formulations) against a seizure/neuro-depletion PT cluster: *Seizure*,
*Generalised tonic-clonic seizure*, *Vitamin B6 deficiency*, *Peripheral neuropathy*,
*Polyneuropathy*, *Homocysteine increased*. Report EB05 with ≥2-of-4 agreement, then an
**EBGM caterpillar over FAERS quarters** to show whether the seizure signal predates the
2026 warning (it should, if the biology is real) and whether reporting spikes afterward
(stimulated-reporting check). The mechanistic sidebar — B6 depletion → the co-signals above —
is what turns "seizure disproportionality" into a testable causal-coherence story rather than
a bare number.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Carbidopa/levodopa is a decades-old,
high-volume drug with deep FAERS coverage; the PTs are standard. The nuance is confounding by
indication (Parkinson's disease itself carries seizure and neuropathy risk) — best handled by
noting it explicitly and, optionally, an active-comparator restriction against other
Parkinson's therapies.

- Drugs.com FDA alert (case count, mechanism) — https://www.drugs.com/fda/fda-requiring-warning-vitamin-b6-deficiency-associated-seizures-products-containing-carbidopa-14797.html

## 2. AAV gene therapy and the liver: is Elevidys's fatal-hepatotoxicity signal a product problem or a class problem?

**Working title:** "The Liver Is the Limit: Reading the Elevidys Acute-Liver-Failure Boxed
Warning Against the Whole AAV Gene-Therapy Class in FAERS"

**Safety question.** Elevidys (delandistrogene moxeparvovec) now carries a boxed warning for
acute serious liver injury and acute liver failure after two pediatric deaths. AAV-vector
gene therapies as a class are known to carry hepatotoxicity risk. Does FAERS show hepatic
disproportionality that is *specific* to Elevidys, or is acute liver injury a **class signal**
shared across approved AAV gene therapies — and how do the per-product EB05 values rank?

**Why it matters now.** On **November 14, 2025** FDA approved a **boxed warning** for acute
serious liver injury and acute liver failure on Elevidys and **removed the non-ambulatory
indication**, limiting use to ambulatory DMD patients aged ≥4 — following **two fatal**
acute-liver-failure cases in non-ambulatory boys and at least one additional serious
non-fatal case (mesenteric vein thrombosis, bowel ischaemia, portal hypertension). FDA is
requiring a ~200-patient post-marketing liver-safety study, and the story remained live in
mid-2026 coverage. The boxed warning is ~8 months old (older than the 1–2-month target), so
the *fresh, defensible* angle is not "news of the warning" but the **class question** the
warning raises — which is squarely a disproportionality question and is not answered
anywhere by a simple regulator announcement.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** Gene therapies are biologics → **FAERS** (now
transitioning into AEMS; see idea #5). Run the four-method engine for a hepatic PT cluster —
*Acute hepatic failure*, *Hepatic failure*, *Liver injury*, *Hepatocellular injury*,
*Transaminases increased*, *Hyperbilirubinaemia* — across the set of approved AAV gene
therapies (e.g. delandistrogene moxeparvovec, etranacogene dezaparvovec/Hemgenix,
valoctocogene roxaparvovec/Roctavian, onasemnogene abeparvovec/Zolgensma, fidanacogene
elaparvovec/Beqvez). Present a **per-product EB05 league table** for the hepatic cluster plus
an **EBGM caterpillar** for Elevidys across quarters to show whether the hepatic signal
was visible in FAERS *before* the November 2025 action. The teaching payload: whether AAV
hepatotoxicity reads as product-specific or class-inherent when the same conservative rule is
applied to every product.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Gene therapies are low-volume (narrow indications,
recent launches), so several EB05 estimates will sit near or below threshold — which is itself
an honest finding, and demands explicit small-N caution. Mapping trade names to consistent
FAERS drug strings is the main wrangling task.

- AAP News, "After 2 child deaths, FDA approves updated safety label for Duchenne muscular dystrophy treatment" — https://publications.aap.org/aapnews/news/33714/After-2-child-deaths-FDA-approves-updated-safety

## 3. Which GLP-1s actually flag for hair loss? A four-method reanalysis of the alopecia signal

**Working title:** "Not the Whole Class: Testing Whether the GLP-1 Alopecia Signal Is
Semaglutide- and Tirzepatide-Specific in FAERS"

**Safety question.** Recent disproportionality work reports that, among GLP-1 receptor
agonists, **only semaglutide and tirzepatide** generate statistically significant alopecia
signals — the others (dulaglutide, liraglutide, exenatide, lixisenatide, albiglutide) do not.
Can the site's four-method engine reproduce that *agent-specific* pattern, and is the split a
real pharmacologic difference or an artifact of exposure volume and weight-loss–driven
telogen effluvium?

**Why it matters now.** Two 2026 analyses put this on the map: a *Journal of Cosmetic
Dermatology* FAERS study spanning **2016–2025** reporting semaglutide and tirzepatide as the
only GLP-1 RAs with significant alopecia signals (and the first to publish dechallenge/
rechallenge data), and a March 2026 *Diabetes/Metabolism Research and Reviews* study
confirming agent-specific alopecia reporting with positive/negative-control sensitivity
analyses. An earlier FAERS analysis reported ROR ≈ **2.46** (95% CI 2.14–2.83) for
semaglutide and ≈ **1.73** (1.42–2.09) for tirzepatide. Hair loss is a high-salience,
heavily-searched patient concern for the most-prescribed drugs of the moment — a natural fit
for a careful, caveat-forward reanalysis. (Distinct from last week's GLP-1 *dosing-error*
idea — different endpoint, different question.)

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for all
seven GLP-1 RAs against an alopecia PT cluster — *Alopecia*, *Alopecia areata*, *Madarosis*,
*Hair growth abnormal*, *Hypotrichosis* — and present a **per-agent EB05 grid** to show which
agents clear the ≥2-of-4 threshold and which don't. Add an **EBGM caterpillar** for
semaglutide/tirzepatide across the 2016–2025 quarters to see whether the signal tracks the
weight-loss prescribing boom (an exposure/notoriety-reporting tell). The essential caveat —
which the site already deploys well — is confounding: rapid weight loss itself causes telogen
effluvium, so a within-class disproportionality cannot separate a drug effect from a
weight-loss effect. Say so plainly; that limitation *is* part of the story.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Enormous report volume and standard dermatologic
PTs make signal detection easy. The interesting work is interpretive: exposure-volume
imbalance across agents (semaglutide/tirzepatide dwarf the others) can manufacture apparent
specificity, and that must be addressed rather than glossed.

- Lee et al., "Not All GLP-1 Receptor Agonists Are Alike: Real-World Evidence of Differential Endocrine and Dermatologic Safety," *Diabetes/Metabolism Research and Reviews* (2026) — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dmrr.70163

## 4. First-line and a bigger exposed population: a Trop-2 ADC neutropenia/diarrhea class check as Trodelvy expands

**Working title:** "Two Boxed Warnings, Moving Upstream: A Comparative FAERS Look at Trop-2
ADC Neutropenia and Diarrhea as Trodelvy Goes First-Line"

**Safety question.** Trodelvy (sacituzumab govitecan) carries boxed warnings for **severe
neutropenia** and **severe diarrhea**. Its new first-line metastatic-TNBC approval enlarges
and shifts the exposed population earlier in the disease course. Does FAERS disproportionality
for these two boxed-warning toxicities differ **across the Trop-2 ADC class** (sacituzumab
govitecan vs. datopotamab deruxtecan), and what is the honest pre-expansion baseline against
which a first-line safety signal should later be judged?

**Why it matters now.** On **June 24, 2026** FDA approved sacituzumab govitecan-hziy for
first-line metastatic TNBC — as monotherapy (immunotherapy-ineligible patients) and in
combination with pembrolizumab (PD-L1-positive tumors). Coverage emphasized that the safety
profile stayed consistent with an SN-38–based Trop-2 ADC, including high rates of serious AEs
and the two boxed warnings. A first-line move is precisely when a surveillance baseline is
most useful and least likely to already exist — and a *class* comparison (rather than a
single-drug baseline) is what distinguishes this from a routine label read. (This is a
comparative class analysis, not the pre-exposure single-drug baseline framing used for
teplizumab last week.)

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for
sacituzumab govitecan and datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway) against the boxed-warning PT
cluster — *Neutropenia*, *Febrile neutropenia*, *Neutrophil count decreased*, *Diarrhoea*,
*Colitis*, *Dehydration* — reported as a side-by-side EB05 table with ≥2-of-4 agreement, plus
a **quarterly EBGM caterpillar** for sacituzumab to establish the pre–first-line baseline that
a follow-up piece can revisit once first-line exposure accrues. A four-method-consensus rule
(all methods must agree) is well-precedented for oncology ADC pharmacovigilance and keeps the
class comparison conservative.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Sacituzumab has several years of FAERS reporting, so
its estimates are stable; datopotamab is newer (2025 launch) and lower-volume, so its EB05
values may be less precise — a caveat to state, not hide. Confounding by heavy prior
chemotherapy and by indication (advanced cancer) is real and should be flagged.

**Citations.**
- FDA, "FDA approves sacituzumab govitecan-hziy as monotherapy and in combination with pembrolizumab for first-line treatment of triple-negative breast cancer" (Jun 24, 2026) — https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-sacituzumab-govitecan-hziy-monotherapy-and-combination-pembrolizumab-first-line
- Healio, "Trodelvy earns FDA approval as first-line treatment for triple-negative breast cancer" — https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20260625/trodelvy-earns-fda-approval-as-firstline-treatment-for-triplenegative-breast-cancer

## 5. The ground is moving under our data: what AEMS replacing FAERS/VAERS means for independent signal work

**Working title:** "From FAERS to AEMS: A Reproducibility Check on the Data Foundation This
Site Is Built On"

**Safety question.** FDA has consolidated its adverse-event systems into a single **Adverse
Event Monitoring System (AEMS)**, retiring the separate FAERS and VAERS pipelines this site's
articles are built on. Can a *known, previously published* disproportionality result — e.g.
the Pfizer-BioNTech myocarditis EB05 signal from our own Christine Cotton piece — be
**reproduced** from AEMS-era data, and does the transition change what independent analysts
can and cannot compute?

**Why it matters now.** On **March 11, 2026** FDA launched AEMS, which **replaces FAERS,
VAERS, and the animal AERS**, with MAUDE, HFCS, and CTPAE slated to fold in by end of May 2026;
Commissioner Makary framed the legacy systems as "outdated and fragmented." This is the single
most consequential change to the raw material of every article on this site — the parquet
schemas, quarterly cadence, and field definitions the four-method engine consumes all trace
back to FAERS/VAERS. A "state of our data source" piece is both timely and genuinely in the
site's interest. (Anchored March 11, 2026 with an ongoing mid-2026 transition; slightly older
than the 1–2-month window, but live and directly load-bearing for the site.)

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS/VAERS → AEMS.** The analyzable core is a
**reproducibility harness**: take one or two already-published signals (the Cotton myocarditis
EB05; an established FAERS example) and attempt to recompute EB05/PRR/ROR/IC from AEMS-exposed
data, documenting exactly where field mappings, de-duplication, and quarter boundaries do or
don't line up. Deliverables: a side-by-side "legacy vs AEMS" EB05 comparison for a handful of
canonical pairs, and a plain-language note on any methods that become harder or impossible.
This is a methods-forward companion to the site's analytic articles — the kind of transparent
"here's what our numbers rest on" piece that reinforces its careful-reading reputation.

**Expected data difficulty.** **High / uncertain.** Depends entirely on what AEMS actually
exposes to external analysts (bulk download vs. look-up tool only) at the time of writing. If
AEMS bulk data is not yet analyst-accessible, the honest article is a *scoping* piece: what
changed, what is knowable today, and what an independent reanalyst should watch for. Verify
data access before committing to a computational version.
