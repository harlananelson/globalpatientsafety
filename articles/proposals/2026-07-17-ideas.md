# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-07-17

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months (mid-May to mid-July 2026), matched to what this site already does:
independent disproportionality reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine
(GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC), EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, and
quarterly EBGM caterpillar plots.

Voice reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece and the *Signal & Noise* methods framing —
modest, testable claims; explicit about what disproportionality can and cannot show; careful
with stimulated-reporting, notoriety, masking, and confounding caveats; "the drug is the
example, the method is the point." These proposals aim to fit that register and lean into the
site's methodology-first lane.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed —
every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator, a court/Congressional record, or the
peer-reviewed literature, not to our data.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the 2026-07-03 batch (orlistat kidney, compounded GLP-1
dosing errors, teplizumab pediatric baseline, confounding-aware ICI endocrine, myocarditis
resolution across eras) and the current queue (Zostavax vs Shingrix, GLP-1→NAION survivability,
FAERS→AEMS reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC), and the drafted articles (carbidopa/levodopa B6, GLP-1
alopecia, AAV/Elevidys liver, AEMS data). Idea #5 touches the GLP-1 class but from a genuinely
new angle (the first *oral* agent's route-specific AE fingerprint), not the alopecia/NAION/
compounding stories already in hand.

## 1. A drug being pulled for a faked efficacy trial also has a real liver signal — does the full FAERS record confirm it?

**Working title:** "Two Reasons a Drug Dies: Separating the Avacopan Efficacy Scandal From Its
Drug-Induced-Liver-Injury Signal in FAERS"

**Safety question.** Avacopan (Tavneos) is being withdrawn primarily over *efficacy* — a
retracted pivotal trial — but there is a **separate, genuine hepatotoxicity safety signal**
riding alongside it. FDA's post-market series describes drug-induced liver injury (DILI)
including *vanishing bile duct syndrome*. Does an independent four-method disproportionality
pass across the entire FAERS database corroborate a hepatobiliary excess for avacopan — and
is it specifically the cholestatic / bile-duct-injury pattern the FDA cases suggest, rather
than generic hepatic noise? This is the kind of "warning built on a bounded case series →
test it at population scale" question the site does well (cf. the orlistat and carbidopa/
levodopa pieces).

**Why it matters now.** A fast-moving, multi-track story through Q2 2026:
- **March 31, 2026** — FDA issued a Drug Safety Communication identifying **76 cases of likely
  drug-induced liver injury**, 74 with serious outcomes, **54 hospitalizations and 8 deaths**,
  median onset **46 days**, including cases of **vanishing bile duct syndrome (VBDS)**.
- **~April 27–30, 2026** — FDA's CDER formally *proposed to withdraw* approval of Tavneos
  (Federal Register notice dated April 30, 2026), citing lack of substantial evidence of
  effectiveness and untrue statements in the original application.
- **June 2026** — Amgen/ChemoCentryx requested a formal hearing to contest withdrawal; the
  submission deadline was extended to **July 29, 2026**.
- **June 29, 2026** — *The New England Journal of Medicine* **retracted the pivotal ADVOCATE
  trial**, the same week the EMA moved to revoke the EU marketing authorization.

The efficacy/fraud track and the liver-safety track are being conflated in coverage; a
methods-first piece can cleanly separate them and adjudicate only the part FAERS can speak to
— the DILI signal.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for
avacopan (Tavneos) against a hepatobiliary MedDRA PT cluster — *Drug-induced liver injury*,
*Hepatocellular injury*, *Cholestasis*, *Vanishing bile duct syndrome*, *Bile duct loss*,
*Hepatic failure*, *Transaminases increased* — reporting EB05 with ≥2-of-4 agreement and the
observed count beside every estimate. Then an **EBGM caterpillar over FAERS quarters** to show
whether the hepatic signal predates the March 2026 communication (it should, if the biology is
real) and whether reporting spikes afterward (a textbook **notoriety / stimulated-reporting**
check — the same gotcha the NAION piece teaches). The transferable lesson: a regulatory
withdrawal can bundle an *efficacy* problem and a *safety* problem that have nothing to do with
each other; disproportionality can test the second but says nothing about the first.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Avacopan is a narrow-indication, relatively
low-volume drug, so counts will be modest and some EB05 estimates may sit near threshold —
which is itself an honest finding to report with the trajectory. The hepatobiliary PTs are
standard and well-defined; VBDS is rare enough that it may appear only as raw counts, which is
worth stating plainly rather than forcing an estimate.

## 2. "Masking": the reason a real signal can hide inside a database full of COVID-vaccine reports

**Working title:** "Hidden in Plain Sight: How Report Volume Masks Signals — and What Happens
to Our Own VAERS Numbers When You Correct for It"

**Safety question.** When one product dominates a spontaneous-reporting database, its sheer
report volume inflates the background against which *every other* product is compared —
mechanically **shrinking** the disproportionality of other drug/event pairs. This is
**masking** (a.k.a. competition bias), a well-documented, non-partisan property of PRR/ROR/EBGM
math. It is also now a live news topic. The testable question: for a signal this site can
compute in VAERS, does correcting for masking (recomputing EB05 with the dominant reporter
removed from the denominator) *change the answer* — and by how much?

**Why it matters now.** On **April 29, 2026** the U.S. Senate Permanent Subcommittee on
Investigations held a hearing, "Unmasked: How Biden Health Officials Purposely Turned a Blind
Eye Toward COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Signals," releasing a staff report alleging FDA declined to
adopt a masking-adjusted data-mining method and citing analyses that found "extreme masking"
examples. The political framing is contested and outside this site's lane — but *masking itself
is a real, quantifiable phenomenon*, and the news moment is a natural hook to explain it neutrally
and show it in action. The peer-reviewed literature already demonstrates it empirically: removing
COVID-19-vaccine reports from EudraVigilance *unmasked* several drug/event signals whose lower
confidence bound crossed threshold only after correction (Micallef et al., *Drug Safety* 2023).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** This is a pure methods piece in the *Signal &
Noise* register. Take a well-populated pair the site already works with (e.g., a non-COVID
vaccine → an adverse event, or a specific COVID-vaccine event), compute EB05 the ordinary way,
then recompute it against a **restricted denominator** that removes the dominant COVID-19-vaccine
reports — the direct analogue of the Micallef "omit-and-recompute" design — and show the ROR/EB05
lower bound move. Present it as a worked "before/after" with observed counts, and state the rule:
*a below-threshold EB05 in a database dominated by one product is not evidence of absence; check
whether the background is being inflated by a competitor.* Pairs naturally with the site's existing
insistence that a disproportionality score is a question generator, not an answer.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** The recompute is a straightforward denominator
restriction on the VAERS parquet already on hand; the conceptual care (choosing a defensible
"dominant product" stratum and a fair comparison event) is the real work, which suits a
methods-forward article. No new data acquisition required.

**Citations.**
- U.S. Senate Committee on Homeland Security & Governmental Affairs, PSI hearing "Unmasked: How Biden Health Officials Purposely Turned a Blind Eye Toward COVID-19 Vaccine Safety Signals," Apr 29, 2026 — https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/subcommittees/investigations/hearings/unmasked-how-biden-health-officials-purposely-turned-a-blind-eye-toward-covid-19-vaccine-safety-signals/
- According to PubMed: Micallef B et al., "An Exploratory Study of the Impact of COVID-19 Vaccine Spontaneous Reporting on Masking Signal Detection in EudraVigilance," *Drug Safety* 2023 — the empirical unmasking demonstration ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-023-01346-9))
- According to PubMed: Fusaroli M et al., "Charting and Sidestepping the Pitfalls of Disproportionality Analysis," *Drug Safety* 2025 — masking, notoriety, confounding, and subgrouping trade-offs in one place ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-025-01604-y))
- According to PubMed: Sankar C et al., "Signal Monitoring for Adverse Events Following Immunisation with COVID-19 Vaccines… South African Surveillance System," *Drug Safety* 2025 — three vaccine signals emerged only after augmenting the dataset to reduce masking ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-025-01547-4))

## 3. The signal a disproportionality database structurally cannot see: sevoflurane, ancestry, and a mitochondrial variant

**Working title:** "When the Answer Isn't in the Table: Why FAERS Can't Find the Sevoflurane
Catastrophe — and What That Teaches"

**Safety question.** FDA is investigating catastrophic neurologic outcomes and deaths after
routine sevoflurane anesthesia, concentrated in a genetically and ancestrally defined subgroup.
Disproportionality math works on drug/event *counts* — it has no column for ancestry, and no
column for a mitochondrial genotype. So the honest question is not "does FAERS flag this?" but
"**can** FAERS flag this at all?" This is a rare-but-loud teaching case for the site's recurring
lesson that *a real, even lethal, subgroup signal can be invisible in aggregate disproportionality*
— the flip side of "the rare outcome is invisible; the mechanism is loud."

**Why it matters now.** On **~July 2, 2026** FDA alerted clinicians to published reports of
severe neurologic adverse outcomes and death in **adult and pediatric patients of maternal
Venezuelan ancestry** following routine general anesthesia with **sevoflurane**, associated with
a rare mitochondrial variant (**MT-ND4 m.11232T>C**). This is a fresh (≈2-week-old), unusual
pharmacogenomic safety story, and a rare instance where the interesting analysis is a
*demonstration of a method's boundary* rather than a signal hunt.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Two-part worked example: (1) run the
four-method engine for sevoflurane against a neurologic-catastrophe PT cluster
(*Encephalopathy*, *Coma*, *Brain injury*, *Metabolic encephalopathy*, *Death*, *Lactic
acidosis*) and show what it does and does not surface at the whole-database level; (2) explain,
with the observed counts in hand, *why* a genotype-restricted, ancestry-restricted, and likely
under-reported catastrophe won't produce a clean aggregate EB05 — the events are diluted into a
huge denominator of ordinary anesthesia reports, and the defining covariates aren't in the data.
The transferable rule: **absence of a disproportionality signal is not absence of risk**, and
some of the most important safety questions are precisely the ones spontaneous-reporting
disproportionality is structurally blind to (a point Fusaroli et al. 2025 make about the
power cost of the subgrouping that *would* be needed).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The FAERS run is easy; the article's value is
interpretive, and the risk is over-claiming from sparse counts — which is on-brand to handle
carefully. The MT-ND4 / ancestry detail comes from the FDA notice and the underlying published
reports, not from our data, and must be cited as such.

**Citations.**
- FDA Drug Alerts & Statements, "FDA Alerts Health Care Providers to Cases of Neurologic Complications from General Anesthesia Linked to Genetic Variant in Patients of Maternal Venezuelan Ancestry," ~Jul 2, 2026 — https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-cases-neurologic-complications-general-anesthesia-linked-genetic
- Secondary coverage, "FDA probes anesthesia-linked neurological complications in some patients of Venezuelan ancestry" — https://www.regionalmedianews.com/news/national/health/fda-probes-anesthesia-linked-neurological-complications-in-some-patients-of-venezuelan-ancestry/
- According to PubMed: Fusaroli M et al., "Charting and Sidestepping the Pitfalls of Disproportionality Analysis," *Drug Safety* 2025 — subgrouping mitigates some biases but cuts sample size and power ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-025-01604-y))

## 4. A safety review that starts before any signal: baselining the infant-RSV immunizations in VAERS

**Working title:** "Before the Review: A VAERS Baseline for the Infant RSV Immunizations FDA Is
About to Re-examine"

**Safety question.** FDA has announced a safety review of the two infant RSV immunizations,
scheduled to begin in summer 2026 — explicitly *not* triggered by an identified signal. That is
exactly the moment a transparent, pre-review VAERS baseline is most useful: what does the
existing reporting look like for nirsevimab and clesrovimab across the standard adverse-event
clusters, and how does it compare with the older-adult / maternal RSV vaccines that already
carry a Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) warning? A baseline piece the site can later compare
against once the review reports.

**Why it matters now.** FDA announced (late 2025) a **safety review of two infant RSV
immunizations set to begin summer 2026**, described as proceeding despite no safety concern
having been identified — a live, dated regulatory action landing now. Context: the maternal and
older-adult RSV vaccines **Abrysvo and Arexvy received a GBS warning in prescribing information
on Jan 7, 2025**, so RSV-product safety is an active surveillance area with an established
comparator signal.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for the infant
RSV immunizations (nirsevimab, clesrovimab) against a standard pediatric AE cluster — with an
explicit, careful note that these are **monoclonal antibodies reported to VAERS as
immunizations**, not vaccines, and that infant reporting patterns differ from adult vaccine
reporting. Include a side-by-side with the RSV *vaccines* (Abrysvo, Arexvy) on the known
**GBS / neuro** PT cluster to show the established-signal contrast. The deliverable is a modest,
timestamped **baseline snapshot + EBGM caterpillar**, framed as "here is the reporting picture
the day before FDA's review begins," so a follow-up can measure what (if anything) the review
surfaces. Reinforces the site's prospective-surveillance framing (as in the teplizumab-baseline
idea) rather than only retrospective signal-chasing.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Infant-RSV-immunization reporting is younger and
sparser than adult vaccine reporting, so several EB05 estimates may be below threshold —
honestly reportable for a baseline. Care needed mapping the newer product (clesrovimab) to
VAERS vaccine-type fields, and stating the monoclonal-vs-vaccine caveat clearly.

## 5. The first oral GLP-1 has no needle — so what should (and shouldn't) show up in its FAERS fingerprint?

**Working title:** "No Injection, Same Class: What the Adverse-Event Fingerprint of Orforglipron,
the First Oral GLP-1, Should Look Like in FAERS"

**Safety question.** Orforglipron is the first once-daily **oral** small-molecule GLP-1 receptor
agonist. Because it is swallowed, not injected, its FAERS fingerprint should *lack* the
injection-site and self-injection **dosing-error** signals that dominate the injectable-GLP-1 and
compounded-GLP-1 reporting — while *sharing* the class GI profile (nausea, vomiting,
constipation, diarrhea). Does an early FAERS baseline bear that out, and does the oral route
introduce anything distinctive (e.g., higher discontinuation from GI intolerance, or hepatic PTs
the trials said were *absent*)? A route-specific contrast the site's prior GLP-1 pieces
(alopecia, NAION, compounded dosing errors) have not touched.

**Why it matters now.** FDA approved orforglipron on **April 1, 2026**, the first oral
small-molecule GLP-1 RA for weight management — a genuinely first-in-class launch and a major
2026 approval. A brand-new route-of-administration within a heavily-used class is precisely when
a pre-signal baseline is most informative and least likely to already exist. Trial data give a
concrete, testable prior: **dose-dependent GI adverse events and discontinuations**, and —
notably — **no hepatic safety signal** (aminotransferase elevations no higher than placebo per
LiverTox), so a hepatic PT appearing in FAERS would be a real deviation worth flagging.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for orforglipron
against (a) the class **GI cluster** (*Nausea*, *Vomiting*, *Constipation*, *Diarrhoea*) and
(b) the **injection/dosing-error cluster** the injectables light up (*Injection site reaction*,
*Incorrect dose administered*, *Medication error*) — the prediction being that (a) mirrors the
class and (b) is conspicuously *absent* because there is no injection. A within-class
**active-comparator** contrast against injectable semaglutide/tirzepatide makes the route effect
visible; observed counts beside every estimate; explicit "too early — this is a baseline"
framing. The methods payload: route of administration is a first-order determinant of a drug's
AE fingerprint, and reading a fingerprint means knowing which PTs are mechanism vs. which are
*delivery artifacts*.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium–High.** Orforglipron launched only in April 2026, so
FAERS counts will be thin and several EB05 values likely sub-threshold — the honest state of a
freshly-launched drug, and worth showing as a trajectory to revisit. The interesting comparison
(oral vs injectable within-class) is tractable given the large injectable-GLP-1 volume already
in the parquet.

**Citations.**
- Patient Care Online, "FDA Approves Orforglipron, First Oral GLP-1 Receptor Agonist for Weight Loss…," Apr 2026 — https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/fda-approves-orforglipron-first-oral-glp-1-receptor-agonist-for-weight-loss-with-no-food-or-water-restrictions
- LiverTox (NCBI Bookshelf), "Orforglipron" — hepatic-safety baseline (no clinically apparent liver injury; ALT no higher than placebo) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK623465/
- According to PubMed: Hageen A et al., "The Gastrointestinal Safety of Orforglipron, a GLP-1 Receptor Agonist… A Network Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials," *Endocrinol Diabetes Metab* 2026 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70222))
