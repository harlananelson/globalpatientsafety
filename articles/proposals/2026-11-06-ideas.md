# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-11-06

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–3 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot **plus**
quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
notoriety, and confounding-by-indication caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature (cited inline), not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their*
numbers; the point of each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own
four-method engine. Several citations come from PubMed and carry DOI links as required.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the current queue (Zostavax-vs-Shingrix *zoster-as-outcome*,
GLP-1→NAION survivability, AEMS reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC), the 2026-07-03 batch (orlistat

## 1. The FDA just boxed-warned an iron infusion — the mechanism was screaming in FAERS years ago; the deaths never showed

**Working title:** "A Boxed Warning the Data Saw Coming: Ferric Carboxymaltose, Hypophosphatemia,
and the Fatal Outcome FAERS Can't See"

**Safety question.** On September 1, 2026 the FDA added a **boxed warning** to ferric
carboxymaltose (Injectafer) for symptomatic hypophosphatemia — a metabolic injury that can
progress to osteomalacia and fractures — after two earlier label updates (2020, 2023) failed to
change practice. Two questions sit squarely in our lane: (a) does our four-method engine detect
the hypophosphatemia signal for ferric carboxymaltose, and how far back in the FAERS quarters
does it go relative to the 2020/2023/2026 label actions (a natural-experiment timeline), and (b)
is the signal *specific to this product* within the IV-iron class, or shared with iron sucrose
and ferric derisomaltose? This is the carbidopa/levodopa and AAV-liver template exactly — **the
rare fatal endpoint is invisible, but the mechanism upstream of it is one of the loudest signals
in the database.**

**Why it matters now.** The regulatory action is dated and concrete: the FDA's September 1, 2026
drug-safety communication requires a boxed warning describing the risk of symptomatic
hypophosphatemia, notes that FDA **Sentinel** data showed serum-phosphate testing occurred in
**fewer than 20%** of ferric-carboxymaltose administration episodes despite the 2020 and 2023
label changes, and extends the requirement to generic ferric carboxymaltose. The methodology
anchor is even better: **according to PubMed**, a 2025 FAERS + VigiBase disproportionality study
(Galigutta et al.) already found hypophosphatemia to be an *enormous* ferric-carboxymaltose
signal (FAERS PRR ≈ 520.7, IC ≈ 8.0) **while finding no death signal at all** (PRR ≈ 0.3) — the
cleanest possible illustration of the site's "mechanism loud, fatal outcome invisible" gotcha,
published *before* the boxed warning landed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-025-03849-z)).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) EBGM/PRR/ROR/BCPNN caterpillar for ferric
carboxymaltose × a phosphate-metabolism PT cluster (*Hypophosphataemia*, *Blood phosphorus
decreased*, *Osteomalacia*, *Fatigue*, *Pathological fracture*) across quarters, with observed
counts beside every EB05 and the 2020 / 2023 / 2026 label dates marked on the trajectory —
showing the signal was present and strong long before the boxed warning. (2) The class contrast:
run the same cluster for iron sucrose and ferric derisomaltose to test whether disproportionality
tracks the *specific* product (consistent with ferric carboxymaltose's distinctive FGF23-mediated
mechanism) rather than IV iron in general — the "does it track route/product, not indication"
move from the AAV-liver piece. (3) The teaching payload: show the *death* PT flat/absent next to
the towering hypophosphatemia PRR, and explain why a spontaneous-reporting system under-captures a
slow metabolic death while loudly capturing the lab-value mechanism — and why the FDA reached for
Sentinel (active surveillance) and a testing-rate statistic, not FAERS counts, to justify the
action.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Ferric carboxymaltose is high-volume in FAERS with
standard PTs; the published PRR magnitude guarantees the pair is well-populated. The only real
work is a clean IV-iron comparator set and honest handling of the near-absent death signal.

## 2. Same drug, opposite sex, different warning — and a second database to check yourself against

**Working title:** "Read It By Sex, Then Check It Abroad: Allopurinol, Febuxostat, and Why a
Pooled Signal Hides Half the Story"

**Safety question.** A September 30, 2026 FAERS study of the two workhorse gout drugs reports
that the strongest safety signals are *sex-specific* and point in different directions:
allopurinol flags for haematologic injury (pancytopenia) predominantly in women, while febuxostat
flags for rhabdomyolysis predominantly in men — and the authors then re-check the direction of
each signal in a *second, independent* spontaneous-reporting database (EudraVigilance). Two
transferable methods lessons the site hasn't written yet: (a) a sex-pooled disproportionality
run can average away a signal that is real in one stratum, and (b) the right robustness test for a
spontaneous-reporting signal is not a bigger p-value — it is *directional replication in a
database with different reporters and different biases*.

**Why it matters now.** **According to PubMed**, Chen Y, Lu W, Tan L, et al., "Sex-stratified
disproportionality signals of adverse events with allopurinol and febuxostat: a retrospective
pharmacovigilance analysis of FAERS and EudraVigilance," *Clin Rheumatol*, published online
September 30, 2026, analyzed FAERS 2004Q1–2024Q4 and reported allopurinol → pancytopenia in women
(ROR ≈ 6.87; 271 cases) and febuxostat → rhabdomyolysis in men (ROR ≈ 4.55; 79 cases), with
directionally consistent patterns in EudraVigilance ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-026-08452-w)).
The febuxostat half carries extra regulatory weight: the FDA added a **boxed warning for
cardiovascular death** to febuxostat in **February 2019** after the CARES trial (documented, dated
— current label status should be re-checked before drafting), so febuxostat is already a drug
where a randomized signal and a spontaneous-reporting signal must be read side by side.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Run the four-method engine for allopurinol
and febuxostat against the haematologic cluster (*Pancytopenia*, *Agranulocytosis*,
*Thrombocytopenia*) and the muscle cluster (*Rhabdomyolysis*, *Blood creatine phosphokinase
increased*, *Myopathy*) **pooled**, then **split by sex**, showing observed counts beside every
EB05 and the quarterly caterpillar for each stratum — a live demonstration that the pooled EB05 is
a weighted average that can sit below threshold while a sex stratum sits well above it. (2) The
replication move: our VAERS/FAERS engine is a natural home for an external-consistency check —
state plainly that "survives in a second database" is a *weaker but real* form of evidence than an
RCT, and that non-replication is informative too. (3) Caveats front and center: differential
reporting by sex (who reports, for which drug), confounding by indication (both drugs treat the
same disease, so a within-class read is cleaner than vs-background), and the allopurinol
hypersensitivity/HLA-B*58:01 context for the haematologic signal.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Both drugs are high-volume with deep FAERS history;
PTs are standard. Sex stratification is a simple cut we already support. The only genuinely new
plumbing is a defensible way to show the EudraVigilance *direction* (we needn't recompute EV — we
can cite the paper's EV result and reproduce the FAERS half ourselves, which keeps the article
honest about what we did and did not run).

  "Sex-stratified disproportionality signals of adverse events with allopurinol and febuxostat: a
  retrospective pharmacovigilance analysis of FAERS and EudraVigilance," *Clin Rheumatol*
  2026;45(11):6947–6959 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-026-08452-w)
- Healio Rheumatology, "FDA adds boxed warning to febuxostat for increased mortality risk"
  (2019 CARES-based action — regulatory context) —

## 3. When "the drug stopped working" is the adverse event — the blind spot behind a brand-new boxed warning

**Working title:** "The Signal FAERS Struggles to See: Anti-Drug Antibodies, Loss of Efficacy, and
the Adzynma Boxed Warning"

**Safety question.** On September 23, 2026 the FDA directed Takeda to add a **boxed warning** to
Adzynma (the recombinant ADAMTS13 enzyme-replacement therapy for congenital thrombotic
thrombocytopenic purpura) because **neutralizing anti-drug antibodies** can block the enzyme and
render it ineffective, with fatal outcomes — a signal the agency says was identified through FAERS.
The methodological question is the uncomfortable one: disproportionality is built to flag *events
that happen*, but an immunogenicity failure presents as *the intended effect not happening* — a
relapse, a "drug ineffective," a "therapeutic product effect decreased." Can our four-method engine
surface a loss-of-efficacy signal at all, and what does it take (the right PT cluster:
*Drug ineffective*, *Therapeutic product effect decreased*, *Neutralising antibodies*, plus the
disease-relapse PTs) to make a *negative* — the absence of benefit — visible in a system designed
for positives? This is the "structural blind spot" lesson (cousin to the sevoflurane-subpopulation
piece, but a different blind spot: outcome *type*, not subpopulation).

**Why it matters now.** The FDA action is this-quarter and specific: the agency took action on a
new boxed warning for Adzynma over neutralizing/blocking antibodies that can prevent the drug from
working, with serious outcomes including death (reported in a pediatric case), and identified the
concern via post-marketing surveillance. For a recently launched orphan biologic, this is a clean,
dated regulatory hook attached directly to spontaneous-reporting data.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) The honest negative first: run the engine
for Adzynma against classic AE clusters and show how little is there (tiny-N orphan drug) — then
pivot to the *efficacy-failure* PT cluster and the immunogenicity PTs, showing whether anything
crosses EB05 ≥ 2 with ≥2 methods and, crucially, how wide the credible intervals are on a handful
of reports. (2) The methods payload: explain why loss-of-efficacy and immunogenicity are
systematically under-represented in disproportionality (they are often coded as disease
progression, not as an AE; the "non-case" background for "drug ineffective" is enormous and noisy),
and why a boxed warning here likely rested on case-level review and mechanism, not on a towering
EB05. (3) Explicit scope: a rare-disease biologic is exactly where passive disproportionality is
weakest, and saying so is the article — *the strength of the method is also the shape of its blind
spot.*

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium–High.** Adzynma is a very-low-volume orphan product, so
counts will be small and most estimates unstable — which is the point, but it requires disciplined
restraint (no over-reading a 3-report EB05) and a clear comparison to how the "drug ineffective"
PT behaves across the whole database. The interesting wrangling is building a loss-of-efficacy PT
cluster that isn't swamped by the generic "drug ineffective" background.

**Citations.**

  Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (cTTP)" (September 2026) —
  https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/fda-takes-action-new-boxed-warning-adzynma-enzyme-replacement-therapy-congenital-thrombotic

## 4. The comparator *is* the finding — why one HIV-prevention drug "flags" and another doesn't

**Working title:** "Change the Comparator, Change the Signal: Reading HIV PrEP Safety in FAERS
Without Fooling Yourself"

**Safety question.** An October 10, 2026 FAERS study of the three FDA-approved HIV pre-exposure
prophylaxis (PrEP) agents — oral FTC/TDF, oral FTC/TAF, and long-acting injectable cabotegravir —
shows that *which comparator you choose* reshapes the entire safety picture: FTC/TDF's broad renal
and metabolic signals **attenuate** when the comparator is switched from "all non-PrEP reports" to
"other HIV drugs," while cabotegravir's injection-site and procedural signals are reproducible
whatever the comparator. The transferable lesson the site hasn't written explicitly: in a
spontaneous-reporting system there is no single "the signal" — there is a signal *relative to a
reference set*, and the honest analyst reports how the answer moves as that set changes. This
extends the catalogue's "the raw leaderboard lies" entry into a concrete recipe.

**Why it matters now.** **According to PubMed**, Rozza D, Poluzzi E, Antonazzo IC, et al.,
"Formulation-Specific Safety Issues of HIV Pre-exposure Prophylaxis Drugs: A Disproportionality
Analysis of the FAERS Database," *Clin Drug Investig*, published online October 10, 2026 (FAERS
2012–2024, 3,201 PrEP reports), used two comparator groups (all non-PrEP ICSRs and non-PrEP HIV
drugs) and found that comparator choice "substantially influences the detection and interpretation"
of signals — FTC/TDF's renal/metabolic pattern largely attenuating against HIV-treated comparators,
while cabotegravir showed specific, reproducible injection-site signals
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40261-026-01602-7)). PrEP is a high-interest public-health topic
with an expanding long-acting-injectable segment, so a careful "how to read this without being
misled" piece is both timely and squarely methodological.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Run the four-method engine for each PrEP
agent against a renal/metabolic cluster (*Renal impairment*, *Blood creatinine increased*,
*Fanconi syndrome*, *Weight decreased*) and an injection-site cluster (*Injection site pain*,
*Injection site nodule*, *Injection site reaction*), **twice** — once with an all-reports
background, once restricted to other HIV drugs — and put the two caterpillars side by side so the
reader *watches* FTC/TDF's renal signal shrink and cabotegravir's injection-site signal hold.
(2) The payload: the drug that appears to be the comparator ("other HIV drugs") is doing the heavy
lifting; confounding by indication (PrEP users are healthy; HIV-treatment users are not) is the
reason the background matters so much. (3) Caveats: formulation confounds reporting (injectables
generate injection-site PTs by construction), reporter mix differs by formulation, and no
denominators — all stated plainly.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** FTC/TDF is well-populated; cabotegravir-for-PrEP is
newer and sparser (small-N on the injectable). Building a defensible "other HIV drugs" comparator
set from FAERS drug strings is the real work — and it is itself the article's methods content.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Rozza D, Poluzzi E, Antonazzo IC, Boccia A, Paccini M, Gallucci F,
  Ponticelli D, Geraci G, Agüero F, Mazzaglia G, Mantovani LG, Raschi E, Ferrara P,
  "Formulation-Specific Safety Issues of HIV Pre-exposure Prophylaxis Drugs: A Disproportionality
  Analysis of the FAERS Database," *Clin Drug Investig* 2026;46(11):1327–1339 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40261-026-01602-7)

## 5. A GBS signal that can't name the vaccine — and an engine that can (VAERS)

**Working title:** "The Signal Without a Brand: Reading Vaccine-Associated Guillain-Barré in VAERS
When the Report Won't Say Which Product"

**Safety question.** A 21-year VAERS disproportionality study (published August 2026) finds that
several vaccines meet four-method concordant criteria for Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) — including
a **herpes-zoster signal and a signal among recently introduced vaccines (RSV, zoster)** — but the
zoster signal is reported for *brand-unspecified* zoster vaccine, so it cannot say whether the
risk sits with the recombinant (Shingrix) or the live (Zostavax) product. Our VAERS engine resolves
vaccine products (it already distinguishes Zostavax from Shingrix in the queued zoster piece), so
the methods question is sharp: when a published signal is blurred by missing product fields, can
product-resolved reanalysis sharpen it — and does the GBS signal that drove the **2025 RSV-vaccine
GBS label warning** reproduce in our four-method consensus? This is a *different endpoint* (GBS, not
zoster-as-outcome) and a *different anchor* (the RSV label) from the queued Zostavax→zoster item, so
it is new ground; zoster here is only the product-resolution demonstration.

**Why it matters now.** **According to PubMed**, Su et al., "Temporal and disproportionality
analysis of vaccine-associated Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS study," *Front Neurol*
2026 Aug 4;17:1814946, analyzed 9,755,435 VAERS reports (2004–2024) with 2,216 GBS cases using
ROR/PRR/IC/EBGM and reported a brand-unspecified zoster-vaccine GBS signal (ROR ≈ 3.01, 95% CI
2.31–3.93; IC025 ≈ 1.19; EBGM05 ≈ 2.40 on 55 reports), flagging RSV and zoster as recently
introduced vaccines with concordant signals ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)).
The regulatory anchor is real and documented: in January 2025 the FDA required a GBS warning in the
prescribing information for the RSV vaccines Abrysvo and Arexvy, and 2026–27-season reviews
(CIDRAP, JAMA) report the signal is being monitored with no new safety signals identified — so the
topic is live even though the RSV label action predates the ~1–2-month window (see the freshness
note below). A separate 2026 VAERS/EudraVigilance study comparing zoster products reported a GBS
signal for the recombinant (RZV) but not the live (ZVL) vaccine — a concrete, testable contrast for
the product-resolution demonstration.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** (1) Reproduce the all-vaccine GBS landscape with
the four-method consensus filter (EB05 ≥ 2, ≥2 of 4 methods), observed counts beside every EB05,
and the quarterly caterpillar — a map of which vaccines carry a GBS reporting signal. (2) The
product-resolution centerpiece: split the brand-unspecified zoster signal into Shingrix vs Zostavax
and test the published RZV-vs-ZVL contrast — showing that product-resolved VAERS can answer a
question the pooled paper explicitly could not. (3) Check the RSV (Abrysvo/Arexvy) GBS signal that
drove the 2025 label and mark the label date on the trajectory as a natural experiment for
stimulated reporting. (4) Caveats doubled: GBS has strong background-incidence and seasonal
structure, stimulated reporting after the 2025 RSV warning will inflate later quarters, and VAERS
cannot provide incidence or causation — only relative reporting patterns.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The all-vaccine GBS run is well-populated and
straightforward in our VAERS engine; the zoster product split and the RSV cohort are smaller-N and
need careful product-code and age handling (GBS risk and RSV vaccination both concentrate in older
adults). Mapping VAERS vaccine-type codes to products cleanly is the main wrangling.

**Note on recency.** The RSV GBS label (Jan 2025) is older than the 1–2-month target, and the Su
paper is ~3 months old (Aug 2026); both are included deliberately because this is the only
VAERS-native idea in the batch and the topic is demonstrably ongoing (2026–27-season monitoring,
2026 product-comparison literature). If a fresher ACIP/FDA RSV or zoster action lands before
drafting, lead with that.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Su et al., "Temporal and disproportionality analysis of vaccine-associated
  Guillain-Barré syndrome: a 21-year VAERS study," *Front Neurol* 2026;17:1814946 (PMID 42614293)
  — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1814946)
- Contagion Live, "FDA Requires Guillain-Barré Syndrome Warning on 2 RSV Vaccines" (Jan 2025
  label action) —
  https://www.contagionlive.com/view/fda-requires-guillain-barr-syndrome-warning-on-2-rsv-vaccines

## Also noted (not drafted — logged for the methods series / queue)

Two strong GLP-1 methods papers published this window. **Not** proposed as headliners (the queue is
already GLP-1-heavy and the NAION notoriety-bias piece is queued), but both are peer-reviewed
*worked examples of methods this site already champions* and would strengthen a future "Reading FDA
Adverse-Event Data" methods article:

- **A published validation of the queued NAION notoriety-bias method.** According to PubMed,
  Melamed et al., "Semaglutide and Panic Attacks: Active-Comparator Disproportionality Analysis,
  Temporal Assessment of Notoriety Bias and Evidence Triangulation in Post-Marketing
  Pharmacovigilance," *Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf* 2026 Nov;35(11), shows a crude semaglutide–panic
  signal that **attenuates after active-comparator adjustment, is non-significant before the July
  2023 regulatory communication, and surges afterward** (reports per 10,000 rising 6.7 → 42.0) — a
  textbook notoriety-bias signature and a direct external corroboration of the queued GLP-1→NAION
  survivability argument ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.70517)).
- **Reporter identity changes signal prominence.** According to PubMed, Liu XY, Wang WF, "Real-World
  Adverse Event Reporting Patterns of Tirzepatide Versus Semaglutide: A Stratified Study of General
  and Healthcare Professional Cohorts in FAERS," *Diabetes Obes Metab* 2026;28(12), shows signals
  shifting materially between consumer-reported and HCP-reported subsets (e.g., HCP share 5.0% vs
  23.4%) — a clean anchor for a future "who filed the report?" reporter-bias gotcha
  ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.71373)).
