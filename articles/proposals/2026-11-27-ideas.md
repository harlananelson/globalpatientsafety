## 1. Zolgensma's cancer signal: when the severe outcome is 9 cases in 6,000 and the mechanism is molecular, not statistical

**Working title:** "Nine Tumors, Six Thousand Patients: Why Disproportionality
Can't See Zolgensma's Insertional-Oncogenesis Signal — and What Can"

**Safety question.** The FDA has opened an investigation into malignancies in
children treated with Zolgensma (onasemnogene abeparvovec), the AAV9 gene
therapy for spinal muscular atrophy, after Novartis reported **nine tumor cases
among >6,000 patients treated since 2019**, including **two cases with vector DNA
integrated near the GLI1 oncogene** (confirmed insertional oncogenesis). Does
FAERS show a malignancy signal for Zolgensma — and, more to the point, *can a
disproportionality method detect a true risk this rare at all*?

**Why it matters now (dated source).** FDA safety communication dated **2026-11-06**
(Novartis disclosure 2026-11-05); the concern explicitly extends to Novartis's
intrathecal product **Itvisma**, which uses the same vector/construct. FDA says
benefits still outweigh risks but is reviewing whether the prescribing
information and long-term follow-up studies are adequate.
- BioSpace, "FDA investigates Novartis' Zolgensma following 9 cases of tumors":
  https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/novartis-confirms-9-reported-cases-of-tumors-in-patients-treated-with-zolgensma
- Rare Disease Advisor, "FDA Probes Cancer Risk Linked to SMA Gene Therapy":
  https://www.rarediseaseadvisor.com/news/fda-investigates-tumor-risk-sma-onasemnogene-abeparvovec-gene-therapy-aav-vector/
- The Medicine Maker (Nov 2026), "FDA Investigates Malignancy Risk Linked to Zolgensma Gene Therapy":
  https://themedicinemaker.com/issues/2026/articles/november/fda-investigates-malignancy-risk-linked-to-zolgensma-gene-therapy/

**Site data / methods.** FAERS. Zolgensma is already in the database with a
published disproportionality profile to anchor against (Chen et al., *Front
Pharmacol* 2025, a full Zolgensma FAERS adverse-event analysis — PMC11747325).
Approach: pull Zolgensma reports for malignancy/neoplasm PTs (e.g. the
"Malignant or unspecified tumours" SMQ) across quarters; show the **observed
counts** (likely single digits) beside any EB05/PRR. The teaching point writes
itself and is squarely in the site's catalogue of gotchas:
- **The rare severe outcome is invisible; look upstream / at mechanism.** Nine
  events against a vast background cannot clear EB05 ≥ 2.0 with any stability —
  and that is exactly *why* molecular evidence (GLI1 integration) rather than
  disproportionality is driving regulatory action. A worked example of the limit
  of passive-report signal detection.
- **Class view.** This dovetails with the existing (draft) AAV piece
  (`aav-gene-therapy-liver.qmd`): that one argued the *hepatic* signal tracks the
  IV-AAV delivery route; insertional oncogenesis is a *distinct, vector-integration*
  endpoint. A short "two different class risks, two different mechanisms, both
  under-detectable by raw FAERS leaderboards" contrast is natural — but keep it
  clearly separate from the liver article's thesis.

**Expected data difficulty.** Low-to-moderate. Zolgensma strings and neoplasm
PTs are present in FAERS; the honest finding is likely "signal too small/noisy to
assess," which *is* the article. Confounders to state plainly: SMA natural
history, pediatric population, and stimulated reporting after the Nov-2026 news.

**Citations.**
1. BioSpace, 2026-11 (FDA investigation, 9 cases / >6,000, GLI1): https://www.biospace.com/drug-development/novartis-confirms-9-reported-cases-of-tumors-in-patients-treated-with-zolgensma
2. Rare Disease Advisor, 2026-11: https://www.rarediseaseadvisor.com/news/fda-investigates-tumor-risk-sma-onasemnogene-abeparvovec-gene-therapy-aav-vector/

4. The Medicine Maker, 2026-11: https://themedicinemaker.com/issues/2026/articles/november/fda-investigates-malignancy-risk-linked-to-zolgensma-gene-therapy/

## 2. When the FDA *removes* a warning: the menopausal-HRT de-labeling as a natural experiment FAERS was never built to referee

**Working title:** "The Black Box Comes Off: Can FAERS Say Anything About the
Breast-Cancer, Stroke, and Dementia Risks the FDA Just Un-Warned?"

**Safety question.** Through 2026 the FDA has been **removing the boxed warnings**
(cardiovascular disease, breast cancer, probable dementia) from menopausal
hormone-therapy products, moving the softened language into the body of the
label. If a label warning is a natural experiment, the reverse is too: what does
FAERS disproportionality show for these exact outcomes on estrogen/estrogen-
progestin products before and across the de-labeling — and, more honestly, *is
FAERS even capable of adjudicating chronic, long-latency, high-background
outcomes like breast cancer, stroke, and dementia?*

**Why it matters now (dated source).** The de-labeling is a live, rolling 2026
regulatory action: HHS/FDA announced the removal of the boxed warnings
(2025-11-10 press announcement), approved the **first batch of six HRT products'
label changes on 2026-02-12**, and has continued approving further batches
(additional estradiol transdermal patches reported ~late 2026). This is a rare
case of a warning being *withdrawn*, and it is squarely topical.
- FDA/HHS press announcement, "HHS Advances Women's Health, Removes Misleading FDA Warnings on Hormone Replacement Therapy":
  https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/hhs-advances-womens-health-removes-misleading-fda-warnings-hormone-replacement-therapy
- Urology Times, "FDA approves label changes for first batch of menopausal hormone therapy products" (first six, 2026-02-12):
  https://www.urologytimes.com/view/fda-approves-label-changes-for-first-batch-of-menopausal-hormone-therapy-products
- Harvard Health, "FDA removes menopause hormone therapy black box warnings":
  https://www.health.harvard.edu/womens-health/fda-removes-menopause-hormone-therapy-black-box-warnings

**Site data / methods.** FAERS. This is the strongest *pure methodology* piece in
the batch and extends several entries in the running gotcha catalogue:
- **Passive reports ≠ incidence, and some outcomes are structurally invisible.**
  Breast cancer and dementia are high-background, long-latency, under-reported-
  to-FAERS events; a weak or absent EB05 for "breast cancer" on estrogens is a
  statement about *reporting*, not about risk. The piece can show this directly —
  compute EB05/PRR/ROR/IC for the warned PTs (breast cancer, DVT/PE, ischaemic
  stroke, dementia/cognitive decline) on conjugated estrogens / estradiol /
  estradiol-progestin and let the numbers demonstrate the limit.
- **The warning drives the reporting (stimulated reporting / notoriety).** The
  decades-old boxed warning likely inflated reporting of exactly these PTs;
  trajectory plots across the de-labeling window test whether reported signals
  decay as attention is withdrawn — a clean "signal tracks attention, not
  biology" demonstration.
- **Right tool for the job.** Honest close: these are the outcomes for which
  WHI-style cohort/RCT evidence, not spontaneous reports, is decisive — a useful
  "know when FAERS is the wrong instrument" lesson.

**Expected data difficulty.** Moderate-to-high. Many HRT products and salt/route
variants to normalize into a clean exposure group; long-latency outcomes will be
sparse. Confounding by indication and by age is heavy and must be stated. The
"it's mostly invisible, and here's why" result is the point, not a limitation.

**Citations.**
1. FDA/HHS press announcement (boxed-warning removal): https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/hhs-advances-womens-health-removes-misleading-fda-warnings-hormone-replacement-therapy
2. Urology Times (first-batch label changes, 2026-02-12): https://www.urologytimes.com/view/fda-approves-label-changes-for-first-batch-of-menopausal-hormone-therapy-products
3. Harvard Health (overview, warnings removed): https://www.health.harvard.edu/womens-health/fda-removes-menopause-hormone-therapy-black-box-warnings

## 3. Resmetirom, the first MASH drug: a textbook confounding-by-indication case in the liver, hiding in plain sight

**Working title:** "ALT Up on a Liver Drug Given to People With Liver Disease:
The Resmetirom Hepatic Signal as a Confounding-by-Indication Masterclass"

**Safety question.** A newly published FAERS study of resmetirom (Rezdiffra, the
first FDA-approved MASH/metabolic-dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis drug)
reports prominent **hepatobiliary signals** — ALT/AST increased, hepatic
cirrhosis — alongside the expected GI events. But resmetirom is prescribed *to
people who already have liver disease*. Is the hepatic disproportionality a drug
effect, or is it the indication reporting itself?

**Why it matters now (dated source).** Zhong et al., "Real-world pharmacovigilance
analysis and cross-database consistency assessment of resmetirom-related adverse
events using FAERS and VigiAccess," *Front Pharmacol*, **e-published 2026-10-09**,
analyzing FAERS through 2026Q1: 1,409 FAERS reports, **62 robust 4-method PT
signals**, with hepatobiliary signals (ALT increased, AST increased, hepatic
cirrhosis) flagged as warranting attention; 16 robust PTs not in current
US/EU labeling.
- According to PubMed: Zhong et al., *Front Pharmacol* 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1898840) · PMC13691544.

**Site data / methods.** FAERS. The site's standard filter (EB05 ≥ 2.0, ≥2 of 4
methods) applied to resmetirom reproduces the hepatic signal — then the article
does what the source study flags but a teaching piece can make central:
- **Confounding by indication, the canonical gotcha.** The comparison that
  matters is not resmetirom vs. all-drugs background (where a liver drug trivially
  flags liver events) but an **active comparator** — resmetirom vs. other agents
  used in the same MASH/cirrhotic population, or vs. the PT rate already expected
  in that population. Lay out why the raw signal is near-uninterpretable and what
  comparator would make it mean something.
- **Expectedness vs. novelty.** Separate the "already-on-the-label, expected"
  hepatic PTs from the 16 off-label robust PTs the study highlights; show how
  "robust by 4 methods" and "clinically novel" are different claims.
- **Cross-database concordance ≠ causation.** The source leans on FAERS/VigiAccess
  agreement (Spearman ρ = 0.998); a good note on why concordant *reporting* is
  supportive but non-causal.

**Expected data difficulty.** Moderate. Resmetirom approved 2024, so report
counts are modest and recent — **verify the drug string and hepatobiliary PT
counts exist with enough observed reports in the site's signals parquet before
drafting** (the parquet's latest build and quarter coverage determine whether
there's enough to plot a trajectory). If counts are too thin for a stable EB05,
pivot the piece to "why it's too early, and what the comparator design would be."

**Citations.**
1. According to PubMed: Zhong et al., "Real-world pharmacovigilance analysis… resmetirom… FAERS and VigiAccess," *Front Pharmacol* 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1898840)
2. (Background) resmetirom = first FDA-approved MASH therapy (Rezdiffra, 2024) — for the indication/confounding framing; cite the FDA approval/label at draft time.

## 4. The GLP-1 NAION warning is now official and class-wide — so watch the FAERS reports *spike after* the label, not before

**Working title:** "The Label Finally Caught Up: What Happens to a FAERS Signal
*After* the FDA Warns — GLP-1s and NAION, the Stimulated-Reporting Half"

**Safety question.** On **2026-11-09** the FDA required a **class-wide
Warnings-and-Precautions** update for all GLP-1 receptor agonists over
non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). The queued NAION piece
(`naion-glp1-survivability.md`) dissects the *pre*-label notoriety cascade (every
non-semaglutide GLP-1 ignited only after the July-2024 semaglutide study). This
is the mirror-image, *post*-label question: now that a class-wide warning exists,
does FAERS reporting of NAION on GLP-1s jump again — a second, regulator-induced
stimulated-reporting wave — and how should an analyst discount it?

**Why it matters now (dated source).** FDA Drug Safety Communication requiring
class-wide GLP-1 NAION labeling, **2026-11-09**, citing an analysis of 126 RCTs
and ~15 probable NAION events (estimated incidence 6.4 vs 3.8 per 100,000
patient-years). A fresh FAERS study (Thawkar et al., *Clin Nutr ESPEN*,
e-pub 2026-10-12) independently lists "optic ischaemic neuropathy" among
semaglutide's high-disproportionality signals, confirming the signal is now
visible in spontaneous data.
- FDA DSC: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential
- Pharmacy Times, "FDA Requires Class-Wide GLP-1 RA Labeling Changes for Potential NAION Risk": https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-requires-class-wide-glp-1-ra-labeling-changes-for-potential-naion-risk
- According to PubMed: Thawkar et al., *Clin Nutr ESPEN* 2026 (semaglutide FAERS, optic ischaemic neuropathy signal). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnesp.2026.105169)

**Site data / methods.** FAERS. Methodology through-line: **two stimulated-
reporting waves** on one signal — the 2024 study wave (covered by the queued
piece) and the 2026 label wave (this piece). Plot NAION-on-GLP-1 EB05 trajectory
with the two dated vertical lines; the shape *is* the lesson — a known publication
date and a known label date are two natural experiments on the same pair, and
neither is biology.

**⚠ Duplication note for the editor.** This overlaps the queued
`naion-glp1-survivability.md`. Two clean options: (a) **fold** the 2026 label
event into that piece as its closing "and here comes the second wave" section

piece has already published and the post-label reporting spike is empirically
visible in a new quarter. Do **not** draft a near-duplicate standalone. Flagging
rather than assuming.

**Expected data difficulty.** Low. NAION PTs and GLP-1 strings are well-populated
and already verified for the queued piece; this reuses that harness plus one more
quarter.

**Citations.**
1. FDA Drug Safety Communication, GLP-1 class-wide NAION labeling, 2026-11-09: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-requires-safety-labeling-changes-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonists-glp-1-ras-due-potential

## 5. Does a signal replicate in a second country's database? Macrolide cardiotoxicity beyond QT, tested in FAERS *and* JADER

**Working title:** "If It's Real, It Should Show Up Twice: Reading a Macrolide
Heart-Failure Signal Across Two National Databases"

**Safety question.** Macrolides have a well-established QT/torsade signal. A new
cross-national study reports macrolide cardiotoxicity extends *beyond* QT —
azithromycin uniquely flagging heart-failure PTs, clarithromycin flagging
ischemic heart disease — and, crucially, shows the azithromycin/clarithromycin
QT signals **replicate independently in Japan's JADER**. The methodology question
for the site: what does cross-database replication buy you, and what can it still
not rule out (shared reporting culture, indication, protopathic bias)?

**Why it matters now (dated source).** Zhou et al., "Cardiotoxicity of macrolide
antibiotics: a pharmacovigilance study integrating FAERS and JADER,"
*Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Arch Pharmacol*, **e-pub 2026-09-26** (FAERS + JADER,
2016Q1–2025Q4): erythromycin long-QT ROR 81.8, azithromycin acute RV failure
ROR 52.5, with azithromycin/clarithromycin QT signals confirmed in JADER.
- According to PubMed: Zhou et al., *Naunyn Schmiedebergs Arch Pharmacol* 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-026-05949-8)

**Site data / methods.** FAERS (the site's own data) for the US side; the JADER
side is cited from the paper (JADER isn't in-house). This is a **methods-first**
entry extending the catalogue with a gotcha not yet covered: **replication as
corroboration, and its limits**. Reproduce the azithromycin QT/TdP and
heart-failure signals in-house with the 4-method filter; separate the *expected*
QT signal (on-label, mechanistically known) from the *novel* heart-failure/
ischemia claims; then discuss why two spontaneous databases agreeing raises
confidence but cannot defeat shared confounding (both skew toward sick,
hospitalized, co-medicated patients) — a natural companion to the "cross-database
concordance ≠ causation" point in idea #3.

**Expected data difficulty.** Low. Azithromycin/clarithromycin and cardiac PTs
are among the best-populated in FAERS; trajectories and observed counts are easy.
Lower *novelty/urgency* than #1–#4 — this is a "when we need a quieter week /
methods-series" pick.

**Citations.**
1. According to PubMed: Zhou et al., "Cardiotoxicity of macrolide antibiotics… FAERS and JADER," *Naunyn Schmiedebergs Arch Pharmacol* 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-026-05949-8)
2. (Context) existing macrolide QT/TdP labeling — cite azithromycin label at draft time for the "expected vs novel" split.
