# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-09-18

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot **plus**
quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
protopathic, and confounding-by-indication caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the current queue (Zostavax-vs-Shingrix zoster,
GLP-1→NAION survivability, AEMS reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC) and the prior batches
(2026-07-24: sevoflurane subpopulation, atorvastatin neuro-leaderboard; 2026-07-03: orlistat
kidney, compounded GLP-1 dosing errors, teplizumab pediatric baseline, ICI endocrine
confounding, myocarditis-across-eras). No GLP-1 idea is proposed this week — the queue is
already GLP-1-heavy. Two notes on near-neighbours: idea #3 shares the *live-vs-not* platform
frame with the queued Zostavax/Shingrix piece but is a different mechanism and endpoint
(serious neuro/cardiac events in older travellers, validated by an actual license suspension —
not the target disease as the outcome); idea #4 is a **bispecific antibody** (BCMA×CD3), a
different class and a different lesson from the queued Trop-2 **ADC** piece.

## 1. A boxed warning built on 134 reports — and the harm it names is a lab value, not the injury

**Working title:** "When the Label Warns About the Blood Test, Not the Broken Bone: Reading the
New Injectafer Hypophosphatemia Warning in FAERS"

**Safety question.** On September 1, 2026 the FDA added a **Boxed Warning** to ferric
carboxymaltose (Injectafer) for symptomatic hypophosphatemia. The warned event —
*hypophosphataemia* / *blood phosphorus decreased* — is a **laboratory** preferred term, while
the outcome patients actually feel (osteomalacia, insufficiency fractures, profound fatigue,
sometimes after *repeated* dosing) is downstream and coded elsewhere. So the two-sided question
this site is built to ask: (a) does ferric carboxymaltose show a disproportionality signal for
the *labeled* lab event, and (b) does the same signal appear for the *downstream* skeletal harm
— or is the injury that motivated the warning largely invisible to spontaneous reporting because
it is slow, cumulative, and rarely attributed back to an infusion given months earlier? Plus a
clean **class contrast**: ferric carboxymaltose is the IV-iron formulation mechanistically tied
to FGF23-mediated renal phosphate wasting, whereas ferric derisomaltose and iron sucrose are
not — a head-to-head disproportionality check is a near-textbook worked example.

**Why it matters now.** The FDA Drug Safety Communication (**September 1, 2026**) states the
agency identified **134 reports and published cases** of serious, symptomatic hypophosphatemia
with ferric carboxymaltose between **July 1, 2022 and June 30, 2025**, that the problem persisted
despite labeling changes in 2020 and 2023, and that the Boxed Warning followed a **citizen
petition the FDA granted on August 13, 2026**. This is a live, this-month regulatory action built
on a spontaneous-reporting spine — exactly the material the site examines — and it echoes the
site's own **"a warning built on 14 cases"** carbidopa/levodopa piece: a small, hand-counted case
series driving a top-tier label change is a natural experiment in how (and whether) the same
signal shows up in aggregate disproportionality.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for
ferric carboxymaltose against the labeled event cluster (*Hypophosphataemia*, *Blood phosphorus
decreased*) with observed counts and the quarterly caterpillar — and expect a strong, stable
signal there. (2) The methodological payload: run the *same* engine against the **downstream**
cluster (*Osteomalacia*, *Hypophosphataemic osteomalacia*, *Stress/insufficiency fracture*,
*Bone pain*, *Pseudofracture*) and show whether the injury the warning is really about is loud or
nearly silent — a demonstration that a "labeled" adverse event and the *harm* can be two very
different rows in FAERS. (3) The **class check**: put ferric carboxymaltose beside ferric
derisomaltose, iron sucrose, and ferumoxytol on the hypophosphatemia PTs; the head-to-head RCT
literature predicts ferric carboxymaltose should stand out, so this is a rare case where
disproportionality has a *validated* expected answer to test the method against. Transferable
lesson (adds to the gotcha catalogue): **the labeled event and the harm are not the same PT — and
the slow, cumulative injury is the one FAERS sees worst.**

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Ferric carboxymaltose is high-volume with deep
FAERS coverage; the labeled-event analysis is trivial and the class comparison uses well-known
generic strings. The only wrangling is assembling the downstream skeletal PT cluster and being
honest about attribution lag.

## 2. The same drug, two verdicts: how the comparator you pick decides whether teriparatide "causes" osteosarcoma

**Working title:** "ROR 15 Against All Drugs, Zero Against the Right One: A Teriparatide
Osteosarcoma Signal That Lives or Dies by Its Comparator"

**Safety question.** A just-published FAERS study reports a strong disproportionality signal
linking teriparatide (an osteoporosis anabolic) to **osteosarcoma** when compared against *all
other drugs* — but the **same signal disappears** when the comparator is switched to
romosozumab, another anabolic bone drug used in the same population. Teriparatide famously
carried a **boxed warning** for osteosarcoma (from a rat carcinogenicity finding) that the FDA
**removed in November 2020** after human data showed no excess risk. So this is an almost perfect
teaching case for the single most consequential choice in disproportionality analysis — **the
reference group** — and for channeling/confounding-by-indication: osteoporosis patients are
imaged and worked up for bone pain far more than the average FAERS patient, and the drug's own
notorious history stimulates reporting of the very event it was once warned for.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Balzano N. et al., "Is teriparatide linked to
osteosarcoma? A disproportionality analysis of the FAERS database," *Pharmacological Reports*,
published **September 17, 2026**, found a disproportionate osteosarcoma signal for teriparatide
versus all other drugs (**ROR 15.31, 95% CI 10.53–22.26; IC 3.56**) that persisted in a
post-2006 subset — **but did not reproduce** when the comparator was restricted to romosozumab,
and the authors note prior evidence does not support an increased risk
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43440-026-00900-3)). A fresh paper whose *own result* flips with
the comparator is a gift for the site's methods lane, and it directly extends the existing gotcha
catalogue (**"the raw leaderboard lies"**; notoriety/stimulated-reporting bias).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce both analyses in the four-method
engine: teriparatide vs. all-drugs background, then teriparatide vs. an active-comparator
restriction (romosozumab, and as a second contrast the bisphosphonate/denosumab osteoporosis
pool), showing EB05/PRR/ROR/IC side by side with observed counts. (2) Plot the quarterly
caterpillar and overlay the **November 2020 boxed-warning removal** as a natural-experiment date
— does reporting of osteosarcoma-on-teriparatide rise, fall, or hold across that label change?
(3) The teaching payload: a single drug–event pair can be a screaming signal or a null depending
entirely on what it is compared against, and the "right" comparator is the one that shares the
indication and the workup intensity. Ties naturally to the site's channeling / confounding-by-
indication thread and the survivability-≠-causation lesson from the NAION piece.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low.** Teriparatide has >100k FAERS reports; romosozumab,
denosumab, and bisphosphonate strings are standard; osteosarcoma is a clean PT. The article is
mostly method and framing, not wrangling — a strong methodology-first fit.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Balzano N. et al., "Is teriparatide linked to osteosarcoma? A
  disproportionality analysis of the FAERS database," *Pharmacol Rep* (September 17, 2026) —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43440-026-00900-3)
- Gilsenan A. et al., "Teriparatide and Osteosarcoma Risk: History, Science, Elimination of Boxed
  Warning, and Other Label Updates," *JBMR Plus* 2022 (the 2020 boxed-warning removal and the
  human surveillance behind it; via PubMed) — https://doi.org/10.1002/jbm4.10665 (PMID 36111201)
- Cleveland Clinic Journal of Medicine, "Teriparatide: Label changes and identifying patients for
  long-term use," 2021 (label-change context) — https://www.ccjm.org/content/88/9/489

## 3. A live vaccine, a suspended license, and what VAERS could and couldn't see

**Working title:** "Live vs. Not-Live, Confirmed by a Regulator: The Chikungunya Vaccine
Contrast in VAERS"

**Safety question.** The two US chikungunya vaccines sit on opposite sides of the oldest divide
in vaccine safety. **IXCHIQ** (Valneva) is **live-attenuated**; **VIMKUNYA** (Bavarian Nordic)
is a **non-replicating virus-like particle (VLP)**. After post-marketing use of IXCHIQ turned up
serious neurologic and cardiac events — including a vaccine-strain encephalitis — clustered in
older adults, the FDA **suspended the US license in 2025** and Valneva moved to **withdraw the US
BLA in 2026**. That regulatory action is a rare thing in disproportionality work: an
externally-validated "ground truth" about which product carries the platform-specific risk. The
question: does an independent VAERS disproportionality analysis reproduce the live-vs-VLP
serious-AE contrast the regulators acted on — and, just as important, where does the passive
system's small denominator and older, multimorbid, outbreak-response population make the
comparison *unfair* to read as biology?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Vondeling G.T. et al., "Post-marketing safety
evaluation of the live-attenuated chikungunya vaccine (IXCHIQ)," *Vaccine* (published
**March 20, 2026**), integrated global serious-AE data (VAERS, EudraVigilance, Canada Vigilance,
MHRA) through August 31, 2025: ~55,900 doses, **35 SAEs (6.3 per 10,000 doses)**, with adults
≥65 receiving a third of doses but accounting for **77% of SAEs**, three fatalities in older
multimorbid men, and one probably-related vaccine-strain encephalitis
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2026.128491)). VIMKUNYA, the VLP alternative, was
**FDA-approved February 14, 2025** for ages 12+. A live vaccine paused by regulators, with a
non-live sibling on the market, is squarely in the site's VAERS wheelhouse and its live-vs-
recombinant methodological lane.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** (1) Four-method disproportionality for IXCHIQ
vs. VIMKUNYA against a serious-outcome cluster (*Encephalitis*, *Meningitis aseptic*,
*Encephalopathy*, *Neurological/cardiac SAE PTs*, *Death*), latest-quarter snapshot plus
trajectory, observed counts beside every estimate. (2) The methodological centerpiece is the
**denominator and the population**: VAERS gives numerators, not rates; IXCHIQ's early doses went
to an older, multimorbid outbreak-response cohort, so a raw disproportionality contrast is
confounded by *who got which vaccine* — the article should pair the disproportionality with the
per-dose SAE rate the regulators actually used, and show how the two framings can agree in
direction while differing wildly in what they license you to claim. (3) Transferable lesson:
even when an external regulator has confirmed the signal, VAERS alone can show the *contrast* but
not the *rate* — and platform (live vs. non-replicating) is a legitimate prior, unlike most
raw-leaderboard pairs. Explicitly flag the kinship-but-difference with the queued
Zostavax/Shingrix piece (there the outcome is the target disease; here it is serious
neuro/cardiac events, and the contrast is backed by a license suspension).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Both vaccines are recent, so VAERS counts are modest
and the caterpillar will be short — the piece must lean on the small-N caveats it is teaching.
Dose-denominator figures come from the published surveillance, not our data, and must be cited as
theirs. VIMKUNYA's very low report volume is itself part of the story (and a limitation).

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Vondeling G.T. et al., "Post-marketing safety evaluation of the
  live-attenuated chikungunya vaccine (IXCHIQ)," *Vaccine* (March 20, 2026) —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2026.128491)
- CDC/FDA, "Surveillance for adverse events following use of live attenuated chikungunya vaccine,
  United States, 2024, and the associated public health response" (VAERS SAEs in older adults;
  via PubMed) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12355908/
- Bavarian Nordic / FDA, "Bavarian Nordic Receives U.S. FDA Approval of Chikungunya Vaccine
  (VIMKUNYA) for Persons Aged 12 and Older" (February 14, 2025; the non-live VLP comparator) —
  https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/02/14/3026914/0/en/Bavarian-Nordic-Receives-U-S-FDA-Approval-of-Chikungunya-Vaccine-for-Persons-Aged-12-and-Older.html

## 4. When every signal is one you already expected — reading a bispecific-antibody safety profile without fooling yourself

**Working title:** "Disproportionality Runs Out of Road: What Teclistamab's FAERS Profile Teaches
About Expected-Toxicity Drugs"

**Safety question.** For a drug whose *expected* toxicities are severe and well-labeled — cytokine
release syndrome, ICANS, infections — nearly everything flags on disproportionality, because the
whole reason it's prescribed comes with those risks. So a "70 significant signals" headline is
almost content-free. The honest question for a class like the BCMA×CD3 bispecific antibodies:
once you accept that the big expected toxicities will always disproportion, **what actually adds
information?** The candidate answers the site can demonstrate are (a) **time-to-onset**
structure (which toxicities come early vs. late), (b) **case-level severity/prioritization**
(which signal is not just frequent but *fatal*), and (c) separating the genuinely **novel/unlabeled**
signals from the expected ones — not the raw EB05 ranking.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, He X. et al., "Teclistamab-associated adverse
events: a disproportionality analysis of the FDA adverse event reporting system," *Hematology*,
published **September 15, 2026**, analyzed 2,631 FAERS reports (2022–2025) with four methods
(ROR, PRR, BCPNN, MGPS) plus time-to-onset, Weibull, and clinical prioritization: **85.2%**
serious and **21.7%** fatal outcomes, **70** significant PT signals (52 known, **18 novel**),
with **sepsis the sole high-priority signal (fatal in 58.6%** of sepsis reports) and immune/neuro
events arising earlier than infectious complications
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/16078454.2026.2730041)). Bispecific antibodies are a fast-growing
class, and a paper whose most useful findings come from *time and severity, not the EB05
leaderboard*, is a clean vehicle for a methods lesson the catalogue doesn't yet have.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the four-method engine for
teclistamab and show that the loud signals (CRS, ICANS, infection, pneumonia, neutropenia) are
exactly the labeled ones — disproportionality "confirming" the boxed-warning material teaches
nothing new. (2) The payload: add the **time-to-onset / Weibull** view (immune-and-neuro early,
infection late) and a **fatality-weighted** view that surfaces *sepsis* as the pair that matters
even if it isn't the top EB05 — plus an explicit **labeled-vs-novel** split so the 18 candidate-
novel PTs are triaged honestly as hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. (3) Transferable
lesson (new to the catalogue): **for an expected-toxicity drug, disproportionality mostly re-
states the label; the information lives in onset timing, case-level severity, and the
labeled/unlabeled split.** Anchor the caveats the site always states — small-N novel signals,
indication severity, and no incidence from passive counts.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Teclistamab report volume (~2.6k) is workable but the
novel-signal PTs rest on small counts, so the trajectory/observed-count discipline is essential.
Time-to-onset needs report-level dates that FAERS provides unevenly — the article should show the
missingness, not paper over it.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: He X. et al., "Teclistamab-associated adverse events: a disproportionality
  analysis of the FDA adverse event reporting system," *Hematology* (September 15, 2026) —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/16078454.2026.2730041)
- According to PubMed: Fan L. et al., "Comparative safety profiles of antibody-drug conjugates: a
  real-world analysis of monotherapy and combination regimens in solid tumors," *Drug Deliv*
  (August 5, 2026) — payload/target structure-toxicity precedent for reading oncology-biologic
  FAERS profiles — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10717544.2026.2712694)
- FDA Prescribing Information, Tecvayli (teclistamab-cqyv) — Boxed Warning for CRS and
  neurologic toxicity, to establish which signals are *expected* —
  https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/761291s000lbl.pdf

## 5. A psychiatric event on a psychiatric drug is the hardest signal to trust

**Working title:** "88% Have No Label Warning — Because 88% Are Confounded: Reading a Drug-Induced
Bipolar-Disorder FAERS Screen"

**Safety question.** A new FAERS study mines the database for drugs associated with **bipolar
disorder (BD)** and finds that most of the top-signal drugs carry *no* BD warning in their labels
— which the authors frame as an under-recognized risk. But the site's methods seat should ask the
harder question first: when the **adverse event is a psychiatric diagnosis** and many of the
suspect drugs are **prescribed to psychiatric patients** (antidepressants, antipsychotics,
smoking-cessation and addiction agents), how much of the signal is **confounding by indication**
and **protopathic/reverse causation** — the drug given *because* mood symptoms were already
emerging — rather than the drug *causing* BD? This is a canonical trap, and a concrete, current
paper makes it teachable.

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Du P. et al., "Drug-associated bipolar disorder: A
disproportionality analysis of the FAERS database," *Psychiatry Research*, published
**August 11, 2026**, analyzed ~23.6 million reports (2004–Q3 2025), found **17,620** BD-linked
cases, ranked the strongest signals (top RORs for pizotifen, tafenoquine, flupenthixol,
naltrexone, pemoline; varenicline had the most reports), and emphasized that **44 of the top 50
(88%) drugs lacked any BD mention in labeling**, with 51% of cases arising within 30 days of
exposure ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117375)). The "missing label warning"
framing is exactly the kind of claim the site exists to stress-test, and it extends the catalogue's
**protopathic / reverse-causation** entry with a fresh, high-visibility example.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the four-method screen for a
handful of the paper's headline drugs against the BD PT cluster, with observed counts and the
quarterly caterpillar. (2) The methodological centerpiece: for each drug, ask whether the
indication *is* a mood/psychiatric condition (flupenthixol, naltrexone, varenicline) — in which
case the BD "signal" is confounded by indication and possibly protopathic — versus a drug with
no plausible psychiatric channeling, where an unlabeled BD signal would be more interesting.
Demonstrate the **within-30-days onset** cuts both ways: it can mean a fast drug effect *or*
early symptoms that prompted the prescription (reverse causation). (3) Transferable lesson:
"most of these drugs have no label warning" is not evidence of a missed risk when the event and
the indication are the same clinical territory — the absence of a warning may be regulators
correctly declining to blame the drug for the disease it treats. Reinforces the site's
protopathic-bias and confounding-by-indication threads.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low.** All drug strings and the BD PT cluster are standard and
well-populated; varenicline and the antidepressants have ample reports. The work is analytic
framing (indication mapping, onset interpretation), not data engineering — a strong
methodology-first fit and a psychiatry topic the site hasn't covered.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Du P. et al., "Drug-associated bipolar disorder: A disproportionality
  analysis of the FAERS database," *Psychiatry Res* (August 11, 2026) —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117375)
- According to PubMed: Xu J. et al., "Differential signal strength of male sexual dysfunction
  associated with antidepressants: a disproportionality analysis of FAERS database," *Sex Med*
  (August 28, 2026) — a companion example of a psychiatric-drug/psychiatric-adjacent-event screen
  where confounding-by-indication must be argued explicitly —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/sexmed/qfag082)
- FDA, "Drug-Induced Bipolar Disorder" background via the drug labels themselves (Chantix
  /varenicline neuropsychiatric history) — label as primary source for what *is* and *isn't*
  warned — https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/021928s048lbl.pdf

## Ranking / editor's note

For the methodology-first lane, the strongest two are **#2 (teriparatide — the comparator
decides the verdict)** and **#1 (Injectafer — the labeled event is not the harm)**: both are
tightly current (Sept 2026 regulatory/literature), both teach a single crisp, transferable
lesson, and both have validated "expected answers" to test the method against (romosozumab
comparator; IV-iron RCT class contrast). **#5 (drug-induced BD)** is the cleanest pure-methods
piece and adds a new topic area. **#3 (chikungunya VAERS)** is the strongest VAERS story and
carries an external regulatory ground-truth, but shares a frame with the queued Zostavax piece
and is denominator-limited. **#4 (teclistamab)** introduces a genuinely new catalogue entry
("disproportionality runs out of road for expected-toxicity drugs") but is the least urgent.
All five are **proposals only** — verify every drug string and PT cluster against the FAERS/VAERS
signals parquet, and confirm observed counts, before any drafting.
