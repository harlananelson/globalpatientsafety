# Article Ideas — Week of 2026-07-24

Five data-driven article proposals grounded in pharmacovigilance developments from the
last ~1–2 months, matched to what this site already does: independent disproportionality
reanalysis of FAERS/VAERS using the four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR, BCPNN/IC),
EB05-thresholded signals with ≥2 methods agreeing, latest-quarter snapshot **plus**
quarterly caterpillar trajectory, observed counts shown beside every EB05.

**Editorial lane (per `ARTICLE_QUEUE.md`):** methodology first. Harlan is a statistician,
not an MD, so each piece is framed as a transferable "reading FDA adverse-event data" lesson
anchored to a concrete drug/vaccine example — *not* a clinical causation claim. Voice
reference: the Christine Cotton VAERS piece — modest, testable claims; explicit about what
disproportionality can and cannot show; careful with stimulated-reporting, litigation-cluster,
and confounding caveats.

**These are proposals only.** No analyses have been run and no numbers below are computed
from our data — every quantitative statement is sourced to a regulator or the peer-reviewed
literature, not to our parquet. Numbers from published papers are *their* numbers; the point of
each article is to reproduce (or fail to reproduce) them in our own four-method engine.

**Deduplication check.** These avoid the current queue (Zostavax-vs-Shingrix zoster, GLP-1→NAION
survivability, AEMS reproducibility, Trop-2 ADC) and the 2026-07-03 batch (orlistat kidney,
compounded GLP-1 dosing errors, teplizumab pediatric baseline, ICI endocrine confounding,
myocarditis-across-eras). No GLP-1 idea is proposed this week — the queue is already GLP-1-heavy.

## 1. FDA is investigating sevoflurane — but the population at risk is invisible to FAERS

**Working title:** "When the Signal Hides in a Subpopulation: Why FAERS Can't See the
Sevoflurane–Genetics Story (and What It *Can* See)"

**Safety question.** FDA has opened a safety investigation into sevoflurane and other volatile
anesthetics after catastrophic neurologic outcomes and deaths clustered in patients of maternal
Venezuelan ancestry carrying a rare mitochondrial variant. Spontaneous-reporting databases have
no ancestry or genotype field. So the honest question is two-sided: (a) does aggregate
disproportionality for sevoflurane against severe neurologic/death PTs show *anything* at the
whole-database level, and (b) can it — even in principle — surface a risk concentrated in a
tiny, genetically defined subgroup? This is a near-perfect worked example of a signal that is
real in the world but **structurally dilutable to invisibility** in FAERS.

**Why it matters now.** On **July 2, 2026** FDA issued an alert stating it is investigating the
safety of sevoflurane and other general anesthetics after reports of severe neurologic
complications and death in adult and pediatric patients of maternal Venezuelan ancestry
following routine anesthesia; a rare mitochondrial variant (**MT-ND4 m.11232T>C**) was reported
in a subset of affected patients, and FDA notes the concern extends to all volatile anesthetics.
The anesthesiology community (ASA/SPA joint communications) describes **at least ~36 cases since
2021** across the US, Spain, and Latin America, disproportionately in boys. This is a live,
this-month regulatory action with a spontaneous-reporting spine the site is built to examine.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Run GPS/EBGM + PRR + ROR + BCPNN for
sevoflurane (and, as a class check, isoflurane/desflurane) against a severe-neuro/fatal PT
cluster — *Malignant hyperthermia*, *Encephalopathy*, *Rhabdomyolysis*, *Metabolic
encephalopathy*, *Cerebral oedema*, *Coma*, *Cardiac arrest*, *Death*. Show the latest-quarter
snapshot with observed counts and the quarterly caterpillar. The **methodological centerpiece**
is the negative-space lesson: even if a subgroup relative risk is large, ~36 events diluted
across decades of high-volume anesthesia reporting will not move a whole-database EB05 — so a
*flat* sevoflurane neuro signal is not reassurance, it is a demonstration of FAERS's blind spot
for ancestry/genotype-restricted risk. Pair with the published FAERS CNS-AE disproportionality
work on sevoflurane as method precedent, and state plainly why pharmacogenomic risks need
targeted registries, not spontaneous reporting.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Sevoflurane is high-volume with deep FAERS
coverage and standard PTs, so the aggregate analysis is trivial. The article's value is
conceptual (the invisible-subpopulation argument), not in data wrangling — a strong fit for the
methodology-first lane.

## 2. The biggest EB05 numbers are the least trustworthy — a fresh statin neuro-signal paper as the worked example

**Working title:** "PRR = 833 Means *Look Closer*, Not *Believe Harder*: Reading a Just-Published
Atorvastatin Neuro-Signal Analysis"

**Safety question.** A July 2026 FAERS study reports dozens of neurological signals for
atorvastatin after excluding the drug's own label indications (to strip confounding by
indication) — including plausible clusters (myasthenia gravis, peripheral neuropathy, "brain
fog") *and* astronomically large signals on tiny counts (acute necrotising myelitis PRR ≈ 833,
EBGM ≈ 39) that the authors themselves flag as lacking published case support. Can the site's
four-method engine reproduce the indication-exclusion technique on the world's most-prescribed
drug — and, more importantly, use the contrast between the *plausible-moderate* and the
*implausible-enormous* signals to teach why the top of a disproportionality leaderboard is
where artifacts live, not where truth concentrates?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, He X. et al., "Atorvastatin-associated neurological
adverse events: a disproportionality analysis with adjustment for confounding by indication,"
*Front Med (Lausanne)*, published **July 7, 2026**, applied PRR/BCPNN/MGPS to FAERS
(Q1 2004–Q3 2025), excluded hypercholesterolemia/coronary-artery-disease reports to reduce
confounding by indication, and found 32 of 50 neuro signals persisting after exclusion — while
explicitly noting several extreme signals (acute necrotising myelitis PRR ≈ 832.9,
EBGM ≈ 38.6; vibration syndrome; brachial radiculitis) with **no** detailed case support
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1852942)). Statins are taken by tens of millions, so a
concrete "how to read this correctly" piece has broad reach and directly extends the site's
existing gotcha catalogue (**"the raw leaderboard lies"**; **"small-N EB05 needs the trajectory
before it means anything"**).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the indication-exclusion design:
run the four-method engine for atorvastatin against the neuro PT cluster with and without label
indications removed, and show which signals survive — a live demonstration of a technique the
site already champions. (2) The teaching payload: put the observed counts next to the huge PRR
values, plot the quarterly caterpillar for a moderate signal (e.g., peripheral neuropathy) vs.
an extreme one (e.g., acute necrotising myelitis), and show that the moderate signal is stable
and well-populated while the extreme one rests on a handful of reports and no trajectory. Ties
naturally to the site's "brain fog" familiarity and the statin-nocebo literature (careful,
non-clinical framing).

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Atorvastatin is the highest-volume drug in FAERS;
coverage and PTs are trivial. The only real work is a defensible indication-exclusion filter
(replicating the paper's design) — which is itself the article's methods content.

**Citations.**

  neurological adverse events: a disproportionality analysis with adjustment for confounding by
  indication," *Front Med (Lausanne)* 2026;13:1852942 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1852942)
- According to PubMed: "Assessing the Risk of Drug-Induced Influenza-Like Illness: A
  Disproportionality Analysis of Two Decades of FAERS Data (2004–2025)," *Curr Drug Saf* 2026
  (companion example of lipid-lowering-agent signals surfacing in FAERS) —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2174/0115748863440350260326055214)

## 3. When disproportionality corroborates a real label warning — but the headline number is a small-N mirage

**Working title:** "Tinnitus Is the Signal; 'Permanent Deafness, ROR 10,000' Is the Artifact —
Teprotumumab and the Hearing-Loss Warning"

**Safety question.** Teprotumumab (Tepezza) carries an FDA label warning for hearing impairment,
including permanent hearing loss, and is the subject of active litigation. A May 2026 FAERS study
finds ear/labyrinth disorders dominating its signal profile — tinnitus the single most-reported
PT, but "permanent deafness" carrying a ROR in the *ten-thousands*. This is the mirror image of
idea #2: here disproportionality **correctly** corroborates a mechanistically plausible,
regulator-recognized signal — yet the most eye-catching number (ROR ≈ 10,000) is a small-N,
litigation-amplified artifact. Can the site show *both* halves: the robust, well-populated
tinnitus signal that deserves trust, and the astronomical permanent-deafness ROR that does not?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Du J. et al., "Five year of post-marketing safety of
teprotumumab: A disproportionality analysis of the FDA adverse event reporting system,"
*Eur J Ophthalmol*, published **May 6, 2026** (FAERS Q1 2020–Q1 2025), found ear-and-labyrinth
disorders as the predominant SOC, tinnitus the most frequent PT (n = 146), and extreme
disproportionality for permanent deafness (ROR ≈ 10,021; 95% CI 5,544–18,115)
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/11206721261446011)). The regulatory anchor is concrete and dated:
in **July 2023** FDA added hearing-loss/impairment warnings to the Tepezza label. With 270+
lawsuits filed, teprotumumab is a textbook setting for the site's **notoriety/litigation-cluster**
gotcha layered on top of a genuinely real effect.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** Four-method engine for teprotumumab against an
otologic PT cluster — *Tinnitus*, *Deafness*, *Deafness permanent*, *Hypoacusis*, *Autophony*,
*Eustachian tube patulous*. Show observed counts beside every EB05, then the quarterly
caterpillar: tinnitus should be well-populated and stable (trustworthy), while
"deafness permanent" rests on few reports with a wide CI and a reporting trajectory that likely
tracks the 2023 label and the litigation timeline (a natural-experiment date, as in the
NAION piece). The transferable lesson: *disproportionality can be simultaneously right about the
biology and grossly misleading about the magnitude* — the mechanism (IGF-1R blockade →
cochlear/vestibular injury) is loud, but the headline ROR is a rare-outcome + stimulated-reporting
artifact.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Teprotumumab is moderate-volume (narrow TED indication,
2020 launch), so several PTs will be small-N — which is exactly the point for the
permanent-deafness contrast, but requires care not to over-read. Separating the litigation-era
reporting surge from baseline is the interesting wrangling.

**Citations.**

  teprotumumab: A disproportionality analysis of the FDA adverse event reporting system,"
  *Eur J Ophthalmol* 2026 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/11206721261446011)
- Ophthalmology Times, "FDA adds hearing loss and impairment warnings to TEPEZZA label"
  (July 2023 label change) —
  https://www.ophthalmologytimes.com/view/fda-adds-hearing-loss-and-impairment-warnings-to-tepezza-label

## 4. Head-to-head within a class: a rare-disease steroid where the *comparator*, not the background, is the point

**Working title:** "Vamorolone vs Deflazacort in Duchenne: Why an Active Comparator Beats a
Whole-Database Background for a Rare-Disease Drug"

**Safety question.** A June 2026 FAERS study compares the novel dissociative steroid vamorolone
against the traditional glucocorticoid deflazacort in Duchenne muscular dystrophy, reporting
*lower* reporting-odds for growth retardation, osteoporosis, and femur fracture with vamorolone
but comparable psychiatric signals — plus early-onset (Weibull "early failure") HPA-axis and
neurobehavioral signals. For a rare, uniformly steroid-treated disease, a whole-database
disproportionality background is nearly meaningless (everyone on the drug has DMD). Can the site
demonstrate *why* an active-comparator design (steroid vs steroid, within DMD) is the correct
frame here — and reproduce the direction of the skeletal-vs-psychiatric contrast?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Zeng Y. et al., "Post-marketing safety surveillance
and signal characterization of the novel dissociative steroid Vamorolone in Duchenne muscular
dystrophy: a comparative disproportionality analysis based on FAERS data," *Front Pharmacol*,
published **June 26, 2026** (FAERS 2023Q4–2025Q4), analyzed 1,171 cases (97% confirmed DMD),
applied ROR/PRR/BCPNN/MGPS with Bonferroni correction, and reported crude-OR advantages for
vamorolone over deflazacort on growth retardation (OR 0.15), osteoporosis (OR 0.27), and femur
fracture (OR 0.38), with comparable psychiatric signals ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1838974)).
Vamorolone (Agamree) is a recently launched DMD therapy, so a careful "how to compare two drugs
in the same rare indication" piece is both timely and methodologically distinctive.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Show the failure mode first: vamorolone
against a whole-database background produces uninterpretable signals dominated by the DMD
indication itself (confounding by indication — the site's "raw leaderboard lies" gotcha in its
purest form). (2) Then the fix: restrict to a within-class active comparator (vamorolone vs
deflazacort/prednisone) and recompute reporting-odds for the skeletal cluster (*Growth retardation*,
*Osteoporosis*, *Bone density decreased*, *Femur fracture*) vs the endocrine/psychiatric cluster
(*Adrenal insufficiency*, *Cushingoid*, *Aggression*, *Mood swings*). Include the standard small-N
and channelling caveats (newer drug may be preferentially prescribed to different patients). A
clean, transferable lesson on comparator choice for rare-disease drugs.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium–High.** Both drugs are low-volume (rare disease, recent
vamorolone launch), so counts are modest and channelling bias (which patients get the new steroid)
is a real confound the article must foreground rather than hide. The head-to-head contingency
tables are simple; the honest caveats are the work.

**Citations.**
- According to PubMed: Zeng Y, Wu H, Wang X, Tan X, Wang R, Liu Y, Duan J, "Post-marketing safety
  surveillance and signal characterization of the novel dissociative steroid Vamorolone in
  Duchenne muscular dystrophy: a comparative disproportionality analysis based on FAERS data,"
  *Front Pharmacol* 2026;17:1838974 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1838974)
- FDA, Agamree (vamorolone) approval / labeling background (for indication and launch context) —
  https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=215239

## 5. A live-attenuated vaccine flagged for the disease it prevents — and a real FDA suspension behind it (VAERS)

**Working title:** "The Vaccine That Was Pulled: Reading the IXCHIQ Chikungunya Safety Signal in
VAERS, With Age Stratification Done Honestly"

**Safety question.** The live-attenuated chikungunya vaccine IXCHIQ had its US license
*suspended* in 2025 after serious neurologic/cardiac events clustered in older adults, and
Europe's PRAC added an aseptic-meningitis warning in March 2026. A live-attenuated vaccine
plausibly producing chikungunya-*like* disease (fever, arthralgia, neuro events) is the same
methodological theme as the queued Zostavax→zoster piece — but here it is attached to an actual
regulatory withdrawal. Does VAERS disproportionality corroborate the older-adult skew that drove
the pause, and can the site show why an *age-stratified* reading is essential (the raw signal
mixes a low-risk younger population with the high-risk 65+ group)?

**Why it matters now.** The safety story is concrete and dated: after VAERS reports of serious
neurologic/cardiac events (including cases 3–5 days post-vaccination in men ≥65), CDC/ACIP added
an age ≥65 precaution in 2025, and the **US license was suspended effective August 22, 2025**;
**EMA's PRAC recommended an aseptic-meningitis product-information update in March 2026**; and a
2026 peer-reviewed post-marketing evaluation is now published. This is an unusually clean
regulator-action-plus-data pairing for a VAERS piece.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** Four-method disproportionality for IXCHIQ
against the relevant PT cluster — *Chikungunya*/*Arthralgia*/*Pyrexia* (vaccine-mimics-disease),
*Encephalitis*/*Meningitis aseptic*/*Guillain-Barré syndrome* (neuro), *Atrial fibrillation*/
*Myocarditis* (cardiac) — **stratified by age band**, with the site's caterpillar for whatever
quarters have reports. The methodology payload: (1) live-attenuated vaccines can flag for the
target disease (shared with the Zostavax idea, but here confirmed by a real withdrawal); (2)
age-stratification changes the conclusion — a pooled VAERS signal understates the concentrated
65+ risk; (3) the standard stimulated-reporting caveat is *doubly* important here, since the 2025
pause and media coverage will have inflated later reporting (a natural-experiment date to check
against). Explicitly frames what VAERS can (relative reporting pattern, age skew) and cannot
(incidence, causation) settle.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** IXCHIQ is a very-low-volume, recently launched vaccine,
so counts are small and several EB05 estimates may sit below threshold — which is itself the
honest finding, and the age-stratification is the analytically interesting part. Mapping VAERS
vaccine-type codes and age bands cleanly is the main wrangling.

**Note on recency.** The core regulatory events (US suspension Aug 2025; PRAC update Mar 2026)
are >2 months old, but the situation is ongoing (license status, published 2026 post-marketing
eval) and it is the only VAERS-native idea in this batch — included deliberately for source
diversity. If a fresher FDA/ACIP IXCHIQ action lands before drafting, lead with that.
