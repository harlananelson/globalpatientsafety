## 1. A brand-new boxed warning where the dangerous endpoint is invisible and the mechanism is loud

> **⚠ Duplicate — see Correction above.** Already proposed in PRs #101 (2026-10-09, #1) and #89
> (2026-09-18, #1). Kept here for the record; prefer the existing write-ups.

**Working title:** "The Boxed Warning for a Lab Value: Reading the New Ferric Carboxymaltose
(Injectafer) Hypophosphatemia Signal in FAERS — and Why the Fractures Won't Show Up"

**Safety question.** On **September 1, 2026** the FDA added a **boxed warning** to ferric
carboxymaltose (Injectafer) for **symptomatic hypophosphatemia** — a warning whose serious
downstream endpoints are osteomalacia and fractures, but whose proximate, mechanism-defining
event is a *lab value* (low serum phosphate). Does the site's four-method engine see the
hypophosphatemia/phosphate-related signal for ferric carboxymaltose, and — the methodological
payload — does it see the *downstream* skeletal endpoints (osteomalacia, pseudofracture, bone
pain, stress fracture) at all, or are they diluted to invisibility the way the carbidopa/levodopa
seizure endpoint was while the B6-depletion mechanism blared? This is a textbook case for the
site's **"the rare severe outcome is invisible; the mechanism is loud — look upstream"** gotcha,
on a this-month regulatory anchor.

**Why it matters now.** The regulatory action is concrete and dated. The FDA's September 1, 2026
Drug Safety Communication added a boxed warning describing the risk of **symptomatic
hypophosphatemia**, advising serum-phosphate monitoring for at-risk patients and for anyone
receiving a repeat course within three months; secondary coverage reports the agency acted
because cases continued **despite prior labeling revisions in 2020 and 2023**, that serum
phosphate was checked in **fewer than ~20%** of administration episodes, and that the label now
names hospitalization, **osteomalacia and fractures** as serious outcomes (with hypophosphatemia
occurring even in patients with normal baseline phosphate). The action followed a citizen
petition granted **August 13, 2026**. The known biology is a clean mechanistic spine: ferric
carboxymaltose distinctively drives **FGF23-mediated renal phosphate wasting**, which is *why*
this IV iron does what iron sucrose and ferric derisomaltose largely do not — making a
within-class contrast the natural design.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Four-method engine (GPS/EBGM, PRR, ROR,
BCPNN/IC) for ferric carboxymaltose against a phosphate/skeletal PT cluster — *Hypophosphataemia*,
*Blood phosphorus decreased*, *Osteomalacia*, *Fracture*, *Stress fracture*, *Bone pain*,
*Muscular weakness* — latest-quarter snapshot with observed counts beside every EB05, plus the
quarterly caterpillar (does the signal step up after the 2020 and 2023 label changes — a
reporting/natural-experiment check?). (2) The within-class contrast that makes it a *methods*
piece, not a clinical claim: run the same cluster for **iron sucrose, ferric derisomaltose,
ferumoxytol, iron dextran**. The teaching point mirrors the AAV-liver route-not-product lesson —
if hypophosphatemia tracks ferric carboxymaltose specifically and the comparators stay flat, the
signal is attributable to the molecule's chemistry (FGF23), not to "IV iron" as a class. (3) The
negative-space lesson: if *Osteomalacia*/*Fracture* are near-absent while *Hypophosphataemia* is
loud, that is the expected shape of a slow, mechanistically-downstream harm in spontaneous
reporting — the boxed warning is built on the mechanism, and FAERS can only corroborate the
mechanism, never the fracture incidence.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Ferric carboxymaltose is high-volume with years of
FAERS coverage; *Hypophosphataemia* is a standard PT. The within-class comparators are all
well-populated. The only care needed is the confounding-by-indication note (IV iron patients are
iron-deficient, often with IBD/CKD/menorrhagia comorbidity) and not over-reading the small-N
skeletal endpoints — which is exactly the article's point.

## 2. The signal that evaporates under an active comparator — a just-published methods paper as the worked example

**Working title:** "140,000 Cancer Reports, Almost All of Them Litigation: The Ranitidine Signal
That Disappears the Moment You Pick the Right Comparator"

**Safety question.** A **October 2026 *Drug Safety*** paper shows that ranitidine's huge FAERS
cancer "signal" is a near-perfect artifact: ~141,000 cancer reports concentrated in 2020–21,
mostly consumer-submitted and geographically clustered in litigation regions, producing
high-magnitude disproportionality for GI/GU cancers — that **vanishes** under an active-comparator
(famotidine) analysis and a time-restricted (pre- vs post-recall) split, with the companion JADER
analysis collapsing from PRR 3.54 to 0.92 once all involvement types are counted (protopathic
bias). Can the site reproduce this collapse in its own four-method engine — the single cleanest
demonstration of three of its headline gotchas at once (**raw leaderboard lies**,
**notoriety/litigation cluster**, **the natural-experiment date**)?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Rathod M. et al., "Disproportionality Analysis of
FAERS Data Divergence and a Systematic Review of Comparative Safety Studies: Lessons Learned from
the Example of Ranitidine and Cancer Risk," *Drug Safety*, published **October 10, 2026**, report
140,985 cancer reports in FAERS during 2020–21 (>two-thirds of all cancer reports that period),
predominantly consumer-submitted and clustered in litigation-affected regions; the high-magnitude
unrestricted signals were "markedly attenuated or absent" under active-comparator (famotidine) and
time-restricted analyses, and the JADER signal disappeared entirely under full involvement-type
analysis — the authors conclude the signal reflects the 2019–20 NDMA regulatory alerts and
litigation, not intrinsic carcinogenicity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-026-01730-1)). The
natural-experiment spine is firmly dated: FDA warned of NDMA in ranitidine in **September 2019** and
**requested market withdrawal of all ranitidine on April 1, 2020** — and explicitly reported that
its testing found *no* NDMA in famotidine, the ideal active comparator. This is a top-journal,
this-month paper that is, in effect, a tutorial on the exact techniques the site champions.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Reproduce the failure mode: four-method
engine for ranitidine against a cancer PT cluster on the full database and show the inflated
latest-window EB05/PRR — then the caterpillar, where the 2020–21 explosion and its alignment with
the recall/litigation timeline is visible to the eye (a natural-experiment date, as in the NAION
piece). (2) Reproduce the fix the site already preaches but hasn't demonstrated this cleanly: swap
the whole-database background for an **active comparator (famotidine)**, recompute, and show the
signal converge toward null; add a **pre- vs post-April-2020** time split. (3) The transferable
lesson: a disproportionality signal that is enormous, consumer-driven, and time-locked to a
regulatory/legal event is a *reporting* phenomenon; the active comparator and the calendar are how
you tell stimulated reporting from biology. Pairs naturally as a "companion" to the atorvastatin
huge-PRR piece already queued.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Low–Medium.** Both ranitidine and famotidine are high-volume with
deep FAERS coverage; the cluster is standard. The real work is a defensible active-comparator
contingency build and the pre/post time split — which *is* the article's methods content.

## 3. Who gets the blame? Primary- vs secondary-suspect coding, and how polypharmacy inflates a signal

**Working title:** "Blame the Right Drug: How 'Primary Suspect' vs 'Also Present' Quietly
Triples a FAERS Signal — a Pediatric Worked Example"

**Safety question.** A **October 2026** FAERS study of bone/growth harms in juvenile idiopathic
arthritis shows that the *suspect role* assigned to a drug on a report — primary suspect vs merely
secondary/concomitant — can change a disproportionality signal by a median of ~87%: of 21 drug ×
outcome signals under the usual broad (primary-or-secondary) analysis, only **5 survived** when
restricted to **primary-suspect** reports, and the two highest-*exposure* biologics
(etanercept, adalimumab) showed **no** signal at all once the restriction was applied. This is a
gotcha the site has *named* ("passive reports ≠ incidence") but never isolated: in a polypharmacy
population, co-medication coding manufactures signals for the drug that merely happened to be in
the report. Can the site demonstrate the primary-suspect restriction as a standard hygiene step
and show which signals are robust to it?

**Why it matters now.** According to PubMed, Erdem F, Gürbüz N, "Bone and growth safety profiles of
biologic and conventional DMARDs in juvenile idiopathic arthritis: a 21-year FAERS disproportionality
analysis," *Inflammopharmacology*, published **October 6, 2026** (FAERS 2004–2024; n = 13,459
pediatric JIA), ran parallel broad (primary+secondary suspect) and strict (primary-suspect-only)
disproportionality with multi-criteria thresholds; only methotrexate (bone and growth), infliximab,
tocilizumab, and abatacept (bone) survived the strict restriction, the broad magnitudes attenuated
by a **median 87%**, and the highest-exposure TNF inhibitors (etanercept, adalimumab) lost their
Composite-A signal entirely ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10787-026-02409-w)). The design is
itself a transferable methods lesson — the authors explicitly recommend dual-track (broad + strict)
reporting for pediatric pharmacovigilance.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Pick the paper's cleanest contrast
(methotrexate — survives; etanercept/adalimumab — do not) and reproduce both tracks in the
four-method engine: all-suspect vs primary-suspect-only, observed counts beside every EB05. (2) The
teaching payload: show the *same* drug/outcome pair's EB05 drop when the denominator is restricted
to reports where the drug is the reporter-attributed cause, and explain *why* — in a disease where
every patient is on multiple immunomodulators, a drug can ride along on another drug's report and
inherit its outcome. (3) Fold this into the site's running "Reading FDA Adverse-Event Data" series
as a new, concrete gotcha: **the suspect-role field is a free, powerful bias control most leaderboard
analyses skip.** Non-clinical framing throughout — this is about *how to read the database*, not
about DMARD safety claims.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The suspect-role fields are in the raw FAERS extracts, so
the restriction is mechanically simple, but our app-ready parquet may not retain the primary/secondary
flag — confirm the field is available in the signal-compute inputs before committing (if not, this
piece motivates adding it). Pediatric subsetting and the known small-N caveats are routine.

**Citations.**

  conventional DMARDs in juvenile idiopathic arthritis: a 21-year FAERS disproportionality
  analysis," *Inflammopharmacology* 2026 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10787-026-02409-w)
- (Method precedent for primary-suspect restriction) According to PubMed: Rathod M. et al., *Drug

## 4. Two drugs, same class, different fingerprints — and a labeled genetic risk the database can't stratify

> **⚠ Substantial overlap — see Correction above.** The ARIA-H-vs-ARIA-E worked example was
> already proposed in PRs #101 (2026-10-09, #3) and #89 (2026-09-18, #4). Only the APOE4
> genotype-blind-spot angle below is distinct.

**Working title:** "Lecanemab Bleeds, Donanemab Swells: Two Anti-Amyloid Antibodies, Two ARIA
Fingerprints in FAERS — and the APOE4 Line FAERS Can't Draw"

**Safety question.** Two 2026 FAERS studies of the anti-amyloid Alzheimer's antibodies report a
clean, drug-specific split: **lecanemab** (Leqembi) shows stronger disproportionality for
**ARIA-H** (microhaemorrhage/haemosiderin), while **donanemab** (Kisunla) shows stronger
disproportionality for **ARIA-E** (oedema/effusion) — the central, mechanism-defining class
toxicity, now with fatal cases and a mid-course-MRI label change. Can the site reproduce the
*differential fingerprint* (not just "both flag ARIA," but which subtype dominates for which drug),
and use it to teach two things at once: (a) head-to-head within a new class is where the signal is
informative, and (b) the biggest real-world risk modifier — **APOE4 carriage** — is exactly the
kind of labeled, genotype-restricted risk that spontaneous reporting structurally cannot stratify
(the sevoflurane-subpopulation lesson, here attached to a drug class millions may take)?

**Why it matters now.** The class is under active, dated safety scrutiny. According to PubMed,
Feng X. et al., "Comparison of safety of lecanemab and donanemab: a real-world disproportionality
analysis using the FDA adverse event reporting system," *Front Pharmacol*, published **June 22,
2026** (FAERS through 2025 Q4; 2,602 lecanemab / 1,038 donanemab primary-suspect reports, four
algorithms, FDR-corrected), found nervous-system disorders the only SOC positive across all four
methods for both drugs and a subtype split — **lecanemab stronger for ARIA-H, donanemab stronger
for ARIA-E** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1868789)). According to PubMed, Xue WY. et al.,
"Post-marketing safety signals and report-level ARIA-E stratification for lecanemab and donanemab:
an integrated FAERS and WHO-VigiAccess study," *Ther Adv Drug Saf*, published **August 18, 2026**,
analyzed 2,961 lecanemab / 1,542 donanemab FAERS reports, found serious outcomes clustered in the
early treatment window, and reported a donanemab × body-weight interaction for ARIA-E
([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/20420986261473013)). The regulatory anchor: the FDA's lecanemab
safety labeling added an additional MRI before the third infusion after serious ARIA-E cases
(including fatalities) in FAERS, and APOE4 testing is recommended before starting either drug
because ARIA risk is markedly higher in ε4 carriers (dose-dependent in homozygotes).

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **FAERS.** (1) Four-method engine for lecanemab and
donanemab against an ARIA PT cluster split by subtype — ARIA-H side (*Cerebral microhaemorrhage*,
*Haemosiderin deposition*, *Cerebral haemorrhage*) vs ARIA-E side (*Amyloid related imaging
abnormality-oedema/effusion*, *Vasogenic cerebral oedema*) — observed counts beside every EB05,
and the head-to-head contrast table that is the article's spine (reproduce the H-vs-E split, don't
assert it). (2) Time-to-onset note: both papers find early clustering — a caterpillar/early-failure
observation the site can show. (3) The negative-space lesson, explicitly cross-referenced to the
sevoflurane-subpopulation piece: the dominant modifier of real ARIA risk is **APOE4 genotype**, and
FAERS has no genotype field — so a drug-level EB05 averages over a high-risk ε4 subgroup and a
lower-risk non-carrier group, and cannot be read as the risk any individual faces. Modest,
non-clinical framing: the fingerprint contrast is a reporting pattern, not an incidence or causal
claim.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** Both drugs are recently launched and moderate-volume, so
several subtype PTs will be small-N (handle with the trajectory + observed counts, as standard).
MedDRA ARIA PT coding is relatively new and may be inconsistently applied across quarters — confirm
the ARIA-E/ARIA-H PTs and their synonyms are present in the parquet before drafting.

## 5. Masking, made concrete: the BMJ's "hidden signal" claim, tested in our own engine (VAERS)

**Working title:** "When Comparing a Vaccine to Itself Hides the Signal: Reproducing the 'Masking'
Problem the BMJ Says the FDA Was Warned About"

> **Relationship to prior proposals — flagged openly.** This *elevates* the "masking" idea first
> sketched in `2026-07-17-ideas.md` (#2), which was never drafted. A this-month BMJ investigation
> has made it newsworthy, and the proposed angle here is different: a **worked empirical
> reproduction** of masking in our own four-method engine (does a real signal null out under a
> self-dominated comparator, and return under the right one?), not the conceptual explainer
> originally sketched. Include only if the site wants to engage a high-profile, contested methods
> story; the framing must be scrupulously method-focused and non-partisan.

**Safety question.** "Masking" is a known, uncontroversial property of disproportionality: when one
product dominates a reporting database, comparing a second product against that database as
background can null out a real signal, because the dominant product's events are baked into the
"expected" rate. A **September 2026 BMJ investigation** reports that FDA statisticians warned
internally in 2021 that the routine screen used on COVID-vaccine VAERS reports was vulnerable to
exactly this, and that a more sensitive method surfaced signals the routine one did not. The site's
engine *is* a disproportionality engine — so the honest, in-lane question is: can we **reproduce
masking as a measurable phenomenon** in VAERS, show the conditions under which a known signal (e.g.
mRNA-vaccine myocarditis, already characterized in our Christine Cotton and shingles pieces) is
attenuated by a poorly-chosen comparator, and demonstrate the standard fixes (all-else-as-comparator
vs vaccine-specific comparator; stratification; EBGM shrinkage vs raw PRR)?

**Why it matters now.** The development is dated and concrete: on **September 16, 2026** the BMJ
published an investigation (reporter David Willman, built from FOIA-released emails and Senate
materials) reporting that FDA officials were warned in early 2021 about a "masking" weakness in the
data-mining screen applied to COVID-vaccine reports, that a more sophisticated method existed and
produced signals the routine method missed, and that CDC did not run proportional-reporting-ratio
screening of VAERS until 2022; critics dispute the *framing* (suppression vs a defensible rollout
trade-off) but **both sides agree masking can hide signals** — the mechanism itself is not in
dispute. Because >90% of early VAERS COVID reports involved Pfizer/Moderna, comparing one mRNA
vaccine against a database dominated by the other could produce no alert — a clean, teachable
instance of the masking mechanism. The site's distinctive value here is pedagogical and empirical,
not political: show the mechanism, show the fix, stay out of intent.

**How the site's data/methods apply.** **VAERS.** (1) Take a well-established signal the site has
already handled (mRNA-vaccine myocarditis/pericarditis) and compute its four-method EB05 under
**two comparator constructions**: mRNA vaccine vs *all other vaccines* (standard) and the
pathological construction — mRNA vaccine vs a background *dominated by the other mRNA vaccine* —
to show attenuation. (2) Contrast **raw PRR against the Bayesian shrinkage methods (EBGM/BCPNN)**
the engine already runs, since the BMJ account turns on method choice; demonstrate where the
methods agree and diverge on the same data. (3) Show stratification/comparator choice as the fix,
and state plainly the limits: VAERS gives relative reporting patterns, never incidence or
causation, and this piece is about *detector behaviour*, not about adjudicating the vaccines. Ties
directly to the site's existing COVID-vaccine and shingles VAERS work and to the "Reading FDA
Adverse-Event Data" series as its most important methods entry — the choice of comparator is not a
technicality; it can decide whether a real signal is seen at all.

**Expected data difficulty.** **Medium.** The site already has the VAERS COVID-vaccine data and a
characterized myocarditis signal, so the raw material exists. The work is constructing the
alternative (self-dominated) comparator cleanly and presenting the PRR-vs-EBGM contrast honestly;
the editorial care (non-partisan, mechanism-only framing) is as important as the computation.
